Veikėjai¶

Name

Description

Years

Algirdas

Grand Duke of Lithuania (1345–1377), co-ruler with Kęstutis who expanded the realm deep into Rus‘.

c. 1296–1377

Baltramiejus Rogala (Oleśnicki’s envoy)

The envoy who meets the crown embassy at the Noteć ford in Book IV (Aug 1430); wears Henė’s golden chain.

?–?

Birutė

Record: death year unknown (Wigand: drowned at Brest on Jogaila’s orders, 1382 or later; at Constance 1416 she was said to have died naturally in old age). Saga: mortal life ends 1384 (ch. 27); continues as the Amber Queen.

c. 1330–1384

Danila (Vytautas’s scribe)

Scribe and personal servant to Vytautas in Vilnius and Trakai (Book IV ch. 1, 16, 24).

c. 1385–1435

Danutė

Died 1424.

c. 1358–1424

Dargis (Palanga fisherman)

Rimgailė’s husband; runs to the beach when the keeper collapses by the burning statue and carries him in (Book V ch. 29).

?–?

Edigu

Emir of the Nogai Horde and military leader of the Golden Horde; victor over Vytautas at the Vorskla (1399).

c. 1352–1419

Eglė (vaidilutė → Pelkių Eglė)

Vaidilutė at Šventaragis grove who witnesses the putting out of the sacred flame in 1387; becomes Pelkių Eglė.

c. 1355–1420

Elena (Ona’s maid)

Stays in Vytautas’s cell; executed. The saga’s invention for the name, faith and fate: the Lithuanian Chronicle has Ona persuade a maid to change clothes with Vytautas (Wigand: Ona’s own clothes), but the maid’s name and execution are a 19th-century addition.

c. 1360–1382

Elijas, son of Mošė (Karaim guard captain)

Karaim captain of the Trakai guards; stands at the vault door. His younger son holds the door in 1430.

?–?

Galminas

Died before 1382 in a raid; reanimated by the Root-Mother. ‚died‘ = becomes a grove at Grunwald. The saga’s invention (record:false): shares his name only with the historical Jurgis Galminas, Vytautas’s deputy in the 1409 rising, at Constance 1415, Samogitian elder 1432-34 (VLE; en.wikipedia Samogitian uprisings); the Book III afterword says so.

c. 1340–1410

Gediminas

Grand Duke of Lithuania (1316–1341), founder of the Gediminid dynasty and builder of Vilnius; died in 1341, before his sons swore the November 1345 Silent Pact.

c. 1275–1341

Gedvilė (Rimgailė’s granddaughter)

Eight in 1450, long-legged, scarred knee from climbing on boats; keeps sweeping the stone; mother of the old man (b. c. 1459) of the 1553 epilogue (Book V ch. 29-30).

c. 1442–?

Günther von Hohenstein (invented Order brother)

The saga’s invention. A young brother of the Order (22 in 1353) who shares a name with a real, older Osterode komtur (c. 1310-1380); the third knight at Lake Ublik, translator and meal-bearer at Marienburg 1361, at Kaunas 1362 (Book VI). NOT the 1381 informant: that was Kuno von Liebenstein.

c. 1331–?

Hanulas (Riga merchant)

Riga merchant who led the townspeople in taking Vilnius castle for Jogaila on 12 Jun 1382; Jogaila’s deputy (first elder) in Vilnius 1382-87; died in Kraków c. 1417-18 (en.wikipedia, VLE). Saga: Henė’s first mask (1380-82).

c. 1340–1418

Jadvyga (Jadwiga)

Queen of Poland (1384–1399); married Jogaila in 1386, uniting Poland and Lithuania; venerated as a saint.

1373–1399

Janusz I of Masovia

Duke of Warsaw and Czersk; husband of Danutė Kęstutaitė and loyal brother-in-law to Vytautas.

c. 1346–1429

Jogaila

Grand Duke of Lithuania (1377–1434) and King of Poland (Władysław II Jagiełło from 1386); architect of the Kreva Union.

c. 1362–1434

Jonas (vilkų vyresnysis)

Elder of the Iron Wolves who re-assembles the pack after the 1382 purge at Trakai.

c. 1330–1384

Jonas Goštautas

Voivode of Trakai, then Vilnius voivode and chancellor; conspirator of the 1432 coup; coordinated the election of Casimir (1440). VLE calls his part in the 1440 murder uncertain.

?–1458

Julijona Alšėniškė

Grand Duchess of Lithuania; Vytautas’s second wife (from 1418), daughter of Ivan Olshanski.

c. ?–1448

Kalvis (the Smith)

Leader of the Vilnius packs; guards the 1387 flame; dies at the Vorskla (1399). His ash feeds the flame afterwards.

c. ?–1399

Kaributas

Duke of Novgorod-Seversky, son of Algirdas; brother to Jogaila and Skirgaila.

c. 1355–1404

Karigaila (Jogaila’s brother)

Died when the Crooked Castle fell, 16 Sep 1390; his head was carried on a stake round the Lower Castle. Not Kaributas (Dmitry Korybut).

?–1390

Kirmėlė / Worm (the Old Tracker)

Elder wolf of the Iron Wolves; carried five-year-old Spark from the burning village of Šermukšniai in winter 1395-96 (Book II ch. 14); dies at Pabaiskas in 1435, whispering his carried names to Pilkoji (Book V ch. 13).

c. 1360–1435

Konrad Zöllner von Rotenstein

Grand Master of the Teutonic Order (1382–1390); oversaw the aftermath of Dovydiškės and the Krėva crisis.

c. 1325–1390

Konrad von Jungingen (Grand Master 1393-1407)

Grand Master 30 Nov 1393 - 30 Mar 1407; elder brother of Ulrich (the saga’s Book II epilogue shows the two at the Marienburg map after Salynas).

c. 1355–1407

Krivis of Šventaragis

Chief pagan priest of Lithuania at the sacred grove of Šventaragis; keeper of the eternal flame.

c. 1310–1387

Kuno von Liebenstein

Komtur of Osterode, godfather of Danutė; the Order’s informant who warned Kęstutis of the Dovydiškės treaty in Aug 1381 (Ivinskis 1978, via en.wikipedia; VLE names no informant). Dates unknown.

?–?

Kęstutis

Grand Duke of Lithuania and Duke of Trakai (1345–1382); defender of the pagan west, strangled at Krėva.

c. 1297–1382

Marquard von Salzbach

Komtur of Ragnit; Vytautas’s captive (1384) and adviser (1389), then enemy. Signs Lyck 1390, helps negotiate Salynas 1398, fights at the Vorskla 1399 (Posilge: nine of the Order killed, he is rescued), insults Birutė after Raciąż 1404, beheaded by Vytautas after Grunwald (komtur of Brandenburg). Birth year unknown.

?–1410

Mykolas Žygimantaitis

Žygimantas Kęstutaitis’s son; commander of the Lithuanians at Pabaiskas (1435); pretender against Casimir after 1440; died in Moscow shortly before 10 Feb 1452. Birth year unknown.

c. ?–1452

Ona

Grand Duchess of Lithuania (d. 1418); Vytautas’s first wife and steadfast political partner who orchestrated his escape from Krėva.

c. 1352–1418

Pilkoji (the Grey One, Vilkų Motina)

Last Mother of the wolves; born c. 1365 (38 in 1404, Book III ch. 7; 84 in 1449, Book V ch. 28). Carries Eglė’s four names (Eglė’s own, her father’s, her mother’s, and the pine she sat under as a child: Book V ch. 1); keeps the silver wolf and the pebble from Vytautas’s body until Kūčios 1430; lies down in the oldest bog of the Dykra with the Iron Wolf’s skull (Vėlinės, 2 Nov 1449, Book V ch. 28).

c. 1365–1449

Pušis (old Kęstutis wolf)

Grey old wolf who served Kęstutis; at Kirmėlė’s exposure in the Trakai vault he says ‚we leave him to the bog, alive‘ (Book II ch. 11); stands in the Kūčios line of 1405 (Book III ch. 9).

?–?

Rimgailė (Palanga fisherwoman)

About sixty in 1450; receives the silver wolf from the Flame-Keeper (Book V ch. 29-30). Not Kęstutis’s daughter Rimgailė. Husband Dargis; granddaughter Gedvilė.

c. 1390–?

Samuelis (Elijas’s elder son, Karaim)

Leads the family’s prayers; Zacharijas’s older, wiser brother (Book V ch. 4).

?–?

Sigismund of Luxembourg

King of Hungary, Croatia, Germany and Bohemia, Holy Roman Emperor; allied with Vytautas and sender of the crown.

1368–1437

Skirgaila

Duke of Trakai and Kyiv, regent of Lithuania; Jogaila’s fierce right hand and governor in Vilnius.

c. 1354–1397

Sofija Vytautaitė

Grand Princess of Moscow; only child of Vytautas and Ona, regent of Moscow for Vasily II.

c. 1371–1453

Spyglys (young ash-bearer)

Wolf; tends the Trakai vault flame after Kalvis; stays behind when the lantern leaves in 1410.

?–?

Tautvilas Kęstutaitis (Kondrat)

Kęstutis’s son; baptised Kondrat 21 Oct 1383; fought with the Order and was killed besieging the Crooked Castle, „believed by a crossbow bolt“ (Wigand). Birth year unknown.

?–1390

Tokhtamysh

Khan of the Golden Horde; allied with Vytautas after losing his throne to Timur.

c. 1342–1406

Uliana of Tver (Julijona Tverietė), Algirdas’s second wife

Mother of Jogaila, Skirgaila, Kaributas, Švitrigaila and others; Orthodox, a dynast who steered the 1377 succession; died 17 Mar 1391 (en.wikipedia, Uliana of Tver). Not Julijona Alšėniškė (Vytautas’s wife).

c. 1325–1391

Ulrich von Jungingen

Ulrich von Jungingen, Grand Master of the Teutonic Order (1407–1410); killed at the Battle of Grunwald.

1360–1410

Uosis / Ash (elder wolf)

White-muzzled elder wolf of the Iron Wolves; accompanies Pilkoji in 1430; dies in the winter of 1448-49 (Book V ch. 28).

c. 1375–1449

Vasily I of Moscow

Grand Prince of Moscow (1389–1425); son of Dmitry Donskoy and husband of Vytautas’s daughter Sophia.

1371–1425

Vytautas

Grand Duke of Lithuania (1392–1430); protagonist of the saga, victor of Grunwald, known as Vytautas the Great.

c. 1350–1430

Vytautas’s sons Jurgis and Ivanas (hostages; contested)

Only a late tradition (Simon Grunau, 16th c.): „Iwan and Georg“ poisoned by a knight to avenge the betrayal. No reliable source knows the incident; Sofija is the only child in the record. The saga adopts the tradition (Book II ch. 6-10).

c. 1375–1392

Winrich von Kniprode

Grand Master of the Teutonic Order (6 January 1352–24 June 1382); the older 1351 dating is superseded here by the specialist biography.

c. 1310–1382

Zacharijas (Elijas’s younger son, Karaim)

Holds the Trakai vault door in 1430 and tends the old brass lantern after the flame goes out (Book V ch. 4); wife Sara.

?–?

Zbigniew Oleśnicki

Born 5 Dec 1389; royal secretary to Jogaila from about age 20 (c. 1409-10); fought at Grunwald (15 Jul 1410); bishop of Kraków from 9 Jul 1423 (so no bishop’s ring in 1411); cardinal created 18 Dec 1439 by Eugene IV (the antipope Felix V also named him, 20 Jan 1440); died 1 Apr 1455 at Sandomierz (en.wikipedia).

1389–1455

Švitrigaila

Grand Duke of Lithuania (1430–1432), youngest son of Algirdas; rival to Žygimantas in the brutal civil war.

c. 1370–1452

Žiežirba (the Flame-Keeper, Book V POV)

Working skin-name Žiežirba. Born c. Oct 1390-Mar 1391: about 5 in winter 1395-96 (Book II ch. 14), 8-9 in 1399, 14 and nearly 15 at Kūčios 1405 (Book III ch. 9), 48 in 1438. Tends the vault flame as a boy from 1399 (Kalvis’s ash, Book II ch. 29) and is sworn into the wolves at Kūčios 1405: the one wolf named below the usual age of 16. Arrives at Palanga on 12 Dec 1449 (the longest night, by the drifted calendar) and is named that night; dies in early March 1450; cremated on the beach (Book V ch. 29).

c. 1390–1450

Žygimantas Kęstutaitis

Grand Duke of Lithuania (1432–1440), brother to Vytautas; survivor of Order captivity, assassinated at Trakai.

c. 1365–1440
