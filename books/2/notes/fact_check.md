# Book II fact-check (Auksinis Šešėlis, 1387-1399)

Pass date: 2026-10-12. Rule: D3, history is the spine; invention fills gaps, or picks a side only where historians genuinely disagree, and the afterword says so. Format per item: claim -> record (source) -> action. Chapter numbers are Book II. Binding decisions applied: Jogaila b. c. 1362; Žygimantas an Order hostage 1390-98.

Sources most used: en.wikipedia Treaty_of_Lyck, Lithuanian_Civil_War_(1389-1392), Siege_of_Vilnius_(1390), Ostrów_Agreement, Treaty_of_Salynas, Skirgaila, Anna,_Grand_Duchess_of_Lithuania, Tautvilas_Kęstutaitis, Marquard_von_Salzbach, Battle_of_the_Vorskla_River, Andrei_of_Polotsk, Šapoka/Ivinskis-based text on prodeoetpatria.lt (Vytautas Didysis); VLE (vle.lt) Skirgaila, Tautvila 1, Žygimantas Kęstutaitis, Vytautas Didysis; lt.wikipedia Vytautas Didysis and Vorsklos mūšis; Marcin Grala, "Kilka uwag na temat politycznych okoliczności małżeństwa Wasyla Moskiewskiego i Zofii Witoldówny" (Zapiski Historyczne 85/1, citing Posilge and the Trinity Chronicle); J. Šiaučiūnaitė-Verbickienė, "Vytauto Didžiojo 1388 m. privilegija Brastos žydams" (Lietuvos istorijos metraštis 2021/2); m-zharkikh.name essays on 1397-99; karaim.eu.

## Known issues from the brief

1. **The hostage sons poisoned (1392)** -> Only a late tradition (Simon Grunau, 16th c.): en.wikipedia Ostrów_Agreement (citing Jonynas 1984) says Grunau spread a "false rumor" that two young sons, "Iwan and Georg", were poisoned by a knight "to revenge their father for the betrayal", and that no reliable source knows of the incident or of any sons. Šapoka-type account (prodeoetpatria.lt, Vytautas Didysis): "some sources" put the poisoning after the 1384 or the 1392 defection; Vytautas's own 1384 letter names only his sister and brother as hostages, he never accused the Order of killing sons in his later letters, and the 1390-92 hostage list does contain a "Jurgis and Jonas", but they were kinsmen (Belz princes in that text; Jonas Alšėniškis and Jurgis Narimantaitis in VLE Jurgenburg), and they came back alive. lt.wikipedia and en.wikipedia Anna: Sofija is the only child. **So the names Jurgis and Ivanas are not the author's invention: they are the tradition's** (the previous notes and afterword claiming "my names" were wrong). **Timing:** the tradition makes the poisoning the Order's *revenge for the betrayal*, i.e. after the break; the book already does that inside ch 9 (the Order hears of the burned forts, the cook vanishes, the boys sicken at Balga) and before Ostrów (4 Aug), so the order of events holds; the real break is "July" (en.wikipedia) / summer 1392, the exact day is unknown (see item 9 below), so the saga's Rasos night is invention. -> **Action:** kept as a contested tradition the saga adopts. Ch 6: the boys are **not on the Order's written hostage list** (clerk: "the household's escort, nobody writes those down"), which also encodes the documentary silence. Ch 9: Ona's speech and Dietrich keep the revenge motive; no document is cited. Ch 10 keeps Henrik's "kai kurie sako, kad ne". Afterword "Vytauto sūnūs" rewritten: says it rests on tradition, lists the silence of Vytautas's letters, the Jonas/Jurgis kinsmen, that the names come from the tradition, and that the saga chose it. Notes part1 corrected.
2. **Ch 6, the Lyck hostage list (19 Jan 1390)** -> Treaty signed at Lyck (Ełk) on 19 Jan 1390 by Marquard von Salzbach, komtur Arnold von Bürglen and Thomas son of Survila (en.wikipedia Treaty_of_Lyck). Hostages demanded: brother Sigismund, wife Anna, daughter Sophia, sister Rymgajla, brother-in-law Ivan Olshanski and other nobles (same article; en.wikipedia Tautvilas is also named there, but Tautvilas fought **on the Order's side** and died at Vilnius in Sept 1390, so that one is dubious and was not used). **Sofija was a hostage**; the Moscow embassy came to Prussia at the end of summer 1390 and she left from Marienburg via Danzig by ship and Pskov (Grala, citing Posilge pp. 167-168; Ivan Olshansky escorted her, en.wikipedia Ivan_Olshansky). VLE says the agreement was renewed "Gardine"; en/pl say Lyck. -> **Corrected in ch 6:** list now Ona, Sofija, Žygimantas, Rymgailė, Jonas Alšėniškis, other nobles; "Maršalas" -> "komtūras"/"įgaliotiniai" (marshal not attested); Danutė and the Masovian guard removed; Sofija is simply among the hostages and the Order lets mother and daughter spend the day together before her autumn departure. **Corrected in ch 8:** route is Marienburg - Danzig - ship - Pskov - Moscow (not Brest - Smolensk), the letter arrives at Pskov via a Danzig merchant and comes from Ona, not Danutė.
3. **Vorskla 1399, the Order's knights** -> Not rumour. Marquard von Salzbach, komtur of Ragnit, took part with a force (en.wikipedia Marquard_von_Salzbach: "brought 1,600 cavalry", figure unconfirmed elsewhere); Posilge lists nine Order members killed and names Marquard among the rescued (Zharkikh, m-zharkikh.name .../BattleVorskla1399/Survivors.html); a Marienburg treasurer's-book entry of 2 Nov 1399 records the return of the sum given for the unit (V. Orlyk, numismatic article, dspace.kntu.kr.ua). -> **Corrected:** ch 22 (the Order's men are seen under Marquard, "pusantro tūkstančio" only as an unverified boast), ch 26 (Order knights fight at the east wagons; Posilge counts nine dead, Marquard saved), afterword Vorskla paragraph and "Kas iš tikrųjų įvyko" sentence, notes part3, outline ch 22.
4. **Ch 6, Marquard's insult to Birutė** -> en.wikipedia Marquard: after the Peace of Raciąż (1404) he "insulted Vytautas by attacking his murdered mother, Birutė"; at Grunwald (Marquard komtur of Brandenburg) Vytautas beheaded him when he "refused to apologize", from Długosz's Banderia Prutenorum (Ekdahl 1992, pp. 58-59; Urban 2003). In 1390 he was Vytautas's *former captive and old friend* (captured July 1384, adviser 1389). -> **Corrected in ch 6:** Birutė line deleted; Marquard, fluent in Lithuanian, offers a velvet threat to the hostages ("grąžinu skolą ... tavo žmona, vaikai, brolis valgys prie mano stalo"), Vytautas's "Aš tai prisiminsiu" kept; afterword paragraph "Vieną sąmoningą nukrypimą" replaced; **the insult now belongs to Book III ch 6** (see books/3/notes/fact_check.md).
5. **Skirgaila** -> Appointed governor 13 Mar 1386 (Frost 2015, p. 77, via en.wikipedia Skirgaila); VLE gives 1387-92 as vietininkas; Duke of Trakai 1382-92; Polotsk taken Mar/Apr 1387 (Andrius captured, son Simeon killed); Kyiv from 1395; died Kyiv 11 Jan 1397 (VLE header 1397 01 11; en.wikipedia lead 11 Jan, infobox 8 Jan), "circumstances not entirely clear"; VLE: fell ill after a hunt, "some sources" poison. -> **Corrected:** ch 1 "trečius metus" in Trakai -> "penktus" (1382-87); ch 16 "Metraštininkai paminės vienuolį" removed (no chronicle names a monk); afterword Skirgaila paragraph says cause unrecorded and Pachomijus is mine. Confirmed: ch 1 date 1387, Andrius's capture (en.wikipedia Andrei_of_Polotsk: held in Poland seven years, freed 1394 at Vytautas's request: ch 1, ch 22 agree; "Chęciny" removed as unattested), ch 16 date 11 Jan 1397.
6. **Tautvilas Kęstutaitis** -> Son of Kęstutis and Birutė, younger brother of Vytautas (VLE Tautvila 1), baptised Kondrat 21 Oct 1383, fought with the Order at Vilnius and was killed besieging the Crooked Castle; "believed killed by a crossbow bolt" (Marburgietis/Wigand, en.wikipedia Siege_of_Vilnius_(1390)); VLE gives a nonsensical "9-10 June" (the siege was 11 Sep-7 Oct). Karigaila (Jogaila's brother) died in the fall of the Crooked Castle on 16 Sep; his head was carried on a stake round the Lower Castle (same article). -> **Ch 7 corrected:** the axe duel replaced: Tautvilas is hit by a crossbow bolt at the gate (shooter unknown), Karigaila dies when the arch falls, his head carried on a pole afterwards; Sofija's letter in ch 8 changed to match. Parentage confirmed.
7. **Salynas (1398)** -> Signed 12 Oct 1398 on an islet of the Nemunas "probably between Kulautuva and the mouth of the Nevėžis" by Vytautas and Konrad von Jungingen; Samogitia up to the Nevėžis, part of Sudovia, Order's help against the Tatars promised, Sigismund and other hostages freed; Marquard helped negotiate; a "king" acclamation known only from Posilge and doubted by Polish historians (en.wikipedia Treaty_of_Salynas; VLE Žygimantas, Vytautas). -> **Confirmed** ch 19 (island in the Nemunas, date, Jungingen, Žygimantas's release, the doubtful shout presented as ambiguous). The 120/440/350 lists remain invention (afterword says so).
8. **Ch 9 -> 10: boys die before Ostrów** -> see item 1; consistent with the tradition (Order's revenge after the break) and with Jogaila hearing of "fever" in mid-July (invention, flagged by "kai kurie sako, kad ne").
9. **Afterword spot checks** -> Brest privilege: **1 Jul 1388** (Beršadskis via Šiaučiūnaitė-Verbickienė, who also notes a possible Trakai grant of 24 Jun 1388); Grodno 1389, the Grodno text just says Brest's privilege applies (the 18 Jun 1389 date appears in secondary Jewish-history texts only). **The "double compensation like a nobleman" clause is absent from the earliest copies and was added in the 16th century** (same article). -> **Corrected:** afterword (1 Jul 1388 / 1389), ch 4 (dateline "liepos pradžios karštis"; the double-fine sentence replaced by "tokią pat žaizdinę, kokia priklauso už krikščionį"; "lotyniškai ir rusėniškai" order). Ostrów: 4 Aug 1392, location unknown, Ostrów Lubelski most likely (Polekhov 2019) or Ostrovo near Lida -> afterword now says the book picks the Lida variant. Ulrich von Jungingen's election 26 Jun 1407 is Book III. Vilnius-Radom is Book III.

## Systematic pass

- **1389 plot (ch 5)** -> en.wikipedia Civil_War_(1389-1392): Vytautas plotted to seize Vilnius castle with armed men in supply wagons "sent for his sister's wedding"; a spy exposed it; conspirators executed; Tautvilas and Ivan Olshansky lost Navahrudak/Halshany; lt.wikipedia: Kaributas, who stayed in Vilnius, forced the conspirators to surrender; no source found for a wedding of a noble's daughter (Narbutt-type embellishment possible). -> Kept as the tradition; bride Daumantė and father Rimgaudas are invention (afterword now says so). Also Vytautas's wife Anna was in Grodno in 1389 (en.wikipedia Anna): ch 4-5 agree.
- **Lyck/Königsberg oath (ch 6)** -> en.wikipedia: Lyck confirms the 1384 Königsberg treaty (Samogitia to the Nevėžis, vassalage). Ch 6 agrees. Danilevičius/Koneczny doubt the treaty's originality (en.wikipedia Treaty_of_Lyck, Fandom copy); no action.
- **Sofija ⚭ Vasily I, 9 Jan 1391** -> Trinity Chronicle "on Sunday, memory of the martyr Polyeuctus", 9 Jan 1391 (Grala; en.wikipedia Sophia_of_Lithuania cites Fennell 2014 p. 158). Born 1371: 17 in 1388 (ch 4), 19 in Jan 1391 (ch 8): **confirmed**. Vasily I b. 30 Dec 1371 -> 19; four years in the Horde (1382-86); Metropolitan Cyprian officiating: **confirmed**. (A "21 January" in en.wikipedia Civil_War article is not supported.)
- **Siege of Vilnius (ch 7)** -> 11 Sep - 7 Oct 1390 (not "mid-September end"); the Crooked Castle fell on 16 Sep; the Lower and Upper Castles were not taken; ~1,000 townsfolk killed and 2,000 taken; the Crooked Castle never rebuilt; Henry (Bolingbroke) of Derby present (retinue "300 followers", Posilge via ldkistorija.lt; "70-80 household knights" elsewhere); Zöllner d. 20 Aug 1390 at Christburg (en.wikipedia Konrad_Zöllner; the civil-war article instead places his death at the Jurbarkas siege: sources disagree). -> **Corrected ch 7:** dateline "rugsėjis - spalio pradžia"; the town (not the Lower Castle) burned; "prieš savaitę" -> "prieš kelias dienas"; Zöllner "keturias savaites" earlier; withdrawal "spalio pradžioje", "dar tris savaites"; news "iš Prūsijos". Bolingbroke's age 23 (b. Apr 1367) and the Appellants of Nov 1387 ("prieš trejus metus"): confirmed.
- **Jogaila b. c. 1362; Žygimantas 1390-98** -> ch 6 (Žygimantas 25, b. c. 1365), ch 19 (about 33, "aštuonerius metus"): confirmed. VLE says 1389-98, which is outside the binding decision; not used.
- **Ritterswerder / the break (ch 9)** -> Built by the Order in autumn 1391 on a Nemunas island near Lampėdžiai (en.wikipedia Ritterswerder); en.wikipedia civil-war article: summer 1392, Vytautas invited the Knights to festivities there, **most prominent guests were taken prisoner**, then Ritterswerder, Metenburg and Neugarten near Hrodna were destroyed; en.wikipedia Ostrów: July 1392. No source gives a day. -> **Corrected ch 9:** the important guests are seized, the rest of the garrison put out alive. The Rasos / St John's night is **invention** (flagged here only). Henry of Masovia's secret mission (spring 1392, Ritterswerder talks): confirmed. Vytautas 42 in 1392: confirmed.
- **Ona's exit from Prussia (ch 9)** -> en.wikipedia Ostrów_Agreement (Urban 2006, p. 204): Anna "freed from the Knights so she could travel to Lithuania" to spread pro-Lithuanian rumours; the Order also let Vytautas move relatives to Jurgenburg; Žygimantas stayed (prodeoetpatria.lt text). Ona confirmed Ostrów in two letters (to Jogaila and Jadwiga; Encyclopedia Lituanica via en.wikipedia Anna). -> **Corrected:** laundress escape removed, Ona rides in from Lithuania after the Order released her in May; the boys and Žygimantas are the pledge. Ch 10 untouched.
- **Ostrów (ch 10)** -> 4 Aug 1392; Vytautas viceroy for life, Trakai restored to him, Skirgaila gets compensation (en.wikipedia; VLE). **Confirmed.** Ch 10 dateline "4-5 rugpjūčio" is saga (treaty dated 4 Aug).
- **Švitrigaila 1393-94 (ch 12)** -> b. before 1370 / c. 1373 (en.wikipedia disagrees internally); 23 in 1393 is acceptable. en.wikipedia: after Uliana of Tver's death Jogaila named Fedor Vesna regent of Vitebsk; Švitrigaila rebelled; Vytautas and Skirgaila took Drutsk, Orsha, Vitebsk; Švitrigaila sent to Kraków but "not held in a prison". **Confirmed in outline; no change** (Skirgaila's part omitted, acceptable). Kaributas and Fyodor Koriatovich (Podolia, expelled 1393) not checked on a web source: left as in the outline.
- **Vladimir Olgerdovich and Kyiv; Smolensk (ch 13)** -> Kyiv to Skirgaila 1395 (VLE); Smolensk annexed 1395 (VLE) or 1394 (lt.wikipedia Vytautas Didysis), again 1404; the feigned Ryazan campaign is the tradition. **Confirmed**, with the discrepancy noted. The Counter's numbers: "Vytautas 35" = 1395 + 35 = 1430; "pusantrų" for Skirgaila fits (spring 1395 - Jan 1397).
- **Tokhtamysh (ch 15)** -> defeated by Timur at the Terek and Sarai sacked 1395; he reached Vytautas c. 1396-97; terms: help for more Rus' lands (lt.wikipedia Vytautas Didysis). Saga's 10,000 horse and six bombards invention. Ch 15 "dvidešimt valdymo metų" -> "beveik dvidešimt". Jalal ad-Din's presence as a teenager plausible.
- **1397-98 campaigns and the Black Sea (ch 17)** -> Zharkikh: sources on 1397-98 are "sparse"; the 1397 campaign went to the Dnieper (Tokhtamysh restored in the west of the Horde, short-lived); in **March 1398** the campaign produced the fortress of St John on the Dnieper; the march to the Don and Volga is a late 15th-16th c. "fabulous" account. lt.wikipedia Vytautas Didysis only says the Duchy reached the Black Sea by the end of his reign. -> **No change to the scene** (a gap, adjusted by the afterword): the ride into the sea and the shore are tradition/invention; the chapter's "no fort here" holds (the real fortress is on the Dnieper, 1398). Afterword now says so. Outline wording fixed.
- **Karaims (ch 17-18)** -> Tradition: 383 families (some say 483, 330 settled at Trakai), 1397-98, disputed (karaim.eu; researchgate "Karaite settlement in medieval Lithuania"; jewishgen Lita). -> Ch 18 now says Elijas's thirty families are one band of "per tris šimtus"; afterword updated.
- **Papal crusade bull (ch 20)** -> Boniface IX blessed the crusade in 1399 (en.wikipedia Vorskla: May 1399; exact bull date not found); Vytautas's army left Vilnius on 18 May 1399 (lt.wikipedia/geni). Ch 20 places the news after Epiphany: **invention within the year; left**, noted in the outline.
- **Jadwiga and Elżbieta Bonifacja (ch 21)** -> daughter b. Sun 22 Jun 1399, d. Sun 13 Jul; Jadwiga d. Thu 17 Jul (weekday arithmetic checks); the baby was buried in her arms; the jewels bequeathed to refound Kraków University: **confirmed**. Jadwiga's warning against the steppe war has no source found: saga (added to the afterword's list of inventions).
- **Vorskla (ch 22-26)** -> 12 Aug 1399 (en.wikipedia body also says 5 Aug); Wagenburg and cannon; Temür Qutlugh feigned agreement and asked three days, Edigu answered that since he was older Vytautas owed submission and tribute; feigned retreat; Edigu's reserves from behind; Tokhtamysh fled; about 20 of about 50 princes killed (en.wikipedia; VLE Vytautas says 12, lt.wikipedia Vorsklos mūšis "over 20"); Andrius of Polotsk, Dmitry of Bryansk, Spytko II of Melsztyn killed. -> **Corrected ch 24:** "dieną/rytoj" -> "tris dienas". "About 20" is hedged in ch 26 ("niekada nesutars"). The "father / tamga on coins / hand over Tokhtamysh" exchange is the Rus'/Polish tradition as echoed in en.wikipedia Edigu (the river meeting); kept. "Bogurodzica" sung at Vorskla is an anachronistic touch (documented at Grunwald 1410); left, flagged.
- **Kyiv ransom and the raid (ch 28)** -> Kyiv paid, Tatars raided to Lutsk (en.wikipedia Vorskla); the sum (Ukrainian sources: 3,000 roubles) not confirmed by the English article; the chapter keeps the sum vague. Byzantine year 6907/6908 (1 Sep 1399 starts 6908): checked.
- **Epilogue (ch 30)** -> Konrad von Jungingen GM 1393-1407; Ulrich b. c. 1360; Kniprode won at Rudau 1370: confirmed. Order's 'five castles' plan is saga (Salynas: two new castles, Friedeburg at the Dubysa).

Names and temperaments of the boys (beyond the tradition's names); Ona's May release details and Rasos night; Brother Dietrich, Pinčiukas, Vaitiekus, Daumantė, Rimgaudas, Butvilas, Elijas, Gedgaudas, Kantaudas, Pamfilas; Skirgaila's Polotsk-night courier; Šmuelis; the 120/440/350 lists; Edigu's tactics scene; Spytko's last words; the Kyiv monk's chronicle line.
