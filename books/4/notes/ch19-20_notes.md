# Book IV, ch. 19–20 notes (Zigmanto Pasiūlymas; Lenkų Baimė)

## Ch 19 (Vytautas POV, Lutsk, Jan 1429)
- Offer made on a sleigh ride over the frozen Styr; present: Sigismund, Vytautas, a Hungarian driver (no Latin or Lithuanian), Henė on the rear board. Danila stayed in the castle. Sigismund speaks Latin into Vytautas's right ear.
- Terms: Sigismund (as King of the Romans, "in the Empire's name") crowns Vytautas; crown of Kremnica gold; **coronation in Vilnius on Nativity of the Virgin (8 Sept) 1430**; not in 1429 (goldsmiths slow, Poles slow). "No conditions." Sigismund's stated motives: 3,000 horsemen against the Turks without Kraków's leave, the Order stops treating Lithuania as Poland's appendage, and (admitted) Poland splits. Vytautas says **"Taip"** on the sleigh.
- Henė's yellow-brass Hungarian bell (left on Vytautas's knees in ch 17) hangs at Vytautas's belt; it chirps once at "no conditions". Pebble is warm, no warning ("it only warns when he lies to himself").
- Jogaila (his rooms, frost on everything) agrees at once after Vytautas says the line quoted in ch 24: "Aš nenoriu mirti didžiuoju kunigaikščiu, kai visą gyvenimą nešiojau karalystę." Jogaila: "Taip." Also: "Ji visada buvo daiktas, kurį kas nors paveldi." He says the Polish council must consent ("a formality") and does not believe it himself. Krėva is only alluded to, not spoken plainly (ch 26 does that).
- Three days later in the great hall Jogaila, through Olesnickis's whispered relay, withdraws into "the council must consent; the king does not object". Olesnickis to Vytautas privately: prays for his long life because he fears what follows him, and more if he is king. Vytautas likes him.
- Julijona's count is back by the third week. The Counter (floor voice) is audible to Vytautas more clearly than other voices.
- Night: Vytautas hears, in his right ear, spindle and measuring cord at the window: Dalia's sentence from `legends_and_songs.md` §10(b) ("a crown will travel toward him all his life"); the second half lost to the deaf ear, only "niekada" caught. He says "Tegu keliauja." (Source note: the original sentence is Dalia's, not Laima's; the outline said Laima.)

## Ch 20 (Stanislovas Rogala POV, Kraków -> Buda, Mar 1429 – Jul 1430)
- **Oleśnicki's envoy = Stanislovas Rogala**, 29, notary's son from Wiślica, tall, black-haired, Kraków chancery badge (three swords on red wax). Same man as "Stanislovas" at the ford in ch 23 and the dark-haired envoy with the sealed letter in ch 24.
- Oleśnicki's "Polish fear" speech: does not hate Vytautas, fears two crowns, the Order, the Hussites, the Turks; "I need him not to have time." Coin in the corner of his study is hot when he is most certain (ch 14 motif). He wrote to Rome, to the Mazovian dukes, to the Sandomierz lords.
- Rogala is sent to Buda: official errand = king's thank-you letter to Sigismund; unofficial = learn where the crown is made, who carries it, when, by what road. Bribed informant: royal chancery clerk **Lászlo** (round, apologetic).
- **Chain**: Henė gives it to Rogala in a Buda corridor, April 1429, as a "gift, not a bribe", no price ("count it yourself; I only write it down"; "it hasn't finished, hasn't learned to breathe out"). Riga mark: two crossed keys under a cross. Warm like a body. Oleśnicki's letter: superstition is an error, not a sin; wear it, the jester is the king's ear.
- Curse: every night Rogala hears only an **inhale** through clenched teeth, never the exhale. It lengthens over the 15 months (one Ave -> a full Miserere). When Rogala learns the route (early July 1430) it goes **silent**; silence is worse.
- Intel (early July 1430): Lászlo says the crown leaves Buda after St James's day (late July / early August) with 40 horsemen under Hans Ebersdorf (keys on his neck) and goes to the **border river ford**; Henė at Sigismund's table says, as a joke, the crown will cross by the merchants' ford and "if the Poles are clever they will wait on the bank". Rogala sends a cipher letter and rides for Kraków (8 days).
- Kraków, dawn: Oleśnicki orders castellan **Jakub of Gorlice** to hold the ford with ~200 men (white eagles), "kill no one, only stop". Gives Rogala a heavy purse for the border men (the purse he carries in ch 23). Puts the chain to his own ear: hears nothing. Tells Rogala to wear it until September, then give it to the cathedral and have a Mass said "for the one who wore it". Last line: Rogala wonders if the bishop knows.

## End state for ch 21 onward
- Rogala is in Kraków -> riding south to the ford, wearing the chain, silent. Chain's fate after ch 23 is open (graph: Oleśnicki holds 1429–30; bishop says "give to the cathedral in September").
- Henė is in Buda in July 1430, then rides with the crown (ch 21).
- Ch 21 inconsistency avoided: Ch 21 has Henė juggling "two stones" and jester "pusantrų metų" at Sigismund's court (Dec 1428–Aug 1430 ok).

## Verify
- Whether Jakub of Gorlice ("Jakubas iš Gorlicų", introduced in ch 23) historically fits as a border castellan: left as in ch 23.
- Chain bearer in the graph is Oleśnicki himself 1429–30; here held by his envoy, as the outline says.
- The "hear the exhale" payoff is left for ch 29/Book V (nothing in ch 20 commits to it).
- St James's day = 25 Jul (used as the crown's departure marker); ch 21 has the crown at the Hungarian/Polish frontier on 15 Aug.
- Mazovian/Sandomierz letters and "letter to Rome" are Saga detail; no real dates asserted.
