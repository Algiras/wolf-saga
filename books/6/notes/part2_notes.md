# Book VI, Part II (ch. 11–20): continuity notes

Facts established that other parts must respect. Short and factual.

## Dates and places used
- **Ch. 10 → 11 (summer 1349):** the abduction follows the night of ch. 10 on the next morning (ebb tide). Wedding at Senieji Trakai 11 days later. Conception that summer, so **Vytautas is born late March 1350** (last hoarfrost, Senieji Trakai, in the bathhouse). Algirdas married Uliana in **January 1350** (she arrived by sleigh from Tver); he visits Senieji Trakai on the ninth day after the birth.
- **Ch. 13:** 1351 summer; Louis of Hungary is 25, Casimir III is 41 and speaks Lithuanian (learned from Aldona, never told anyone). The camp's place is **not named** (verify). The oath wording: "Kai aš ir visa mano tauta stovėsime Budos šventovėje, karaliaus akivaizdoje, aš priimsiu krikštą." (a condition, never met). Kęstutis returns home six weeks later in early autumn.
- **Ch. 16:** capture in **August 1361** at a ford of the Šešupė (the Order's bait column, 600 horse in the woods). Ten days' march to Marienburg.
- **Ch. 18:** escape on the evening of **1 Nov 1361**, the vigil for the dead (the brothers are at the Office of the Dead, the guard changes). Kęstutis was held about 2.5 months, Aug–Nov.
- **Ch. 19:** the siege lasted about three weeks; the mine under the **east tower** is fired on **Easter morning 1362** (the Order's host sings *Christ ist erstanden*). The Neris and Nemunas are in spate and Kęstutis cannot cross; he reaches the far bank when Kaunas is already burning ("three weeks and a day").
- **Ch. 20:** autumn 1362, the Syniukha river ("Mėlynieji Vandenys"), Podolia. The three beys are **unnamed**; the date is the outline's "autumn (verify)".

## Characters and ages (as written)
- **Vaidotas** b. c.1331 (18 in 1349, 31 at Kaunas); **Butautas** b. c.1335 (14 in 1349). Both are sons of Kęstutis's first wife, dead since c.1343, **unnamed**; she died bearing a third child, who died too. Matches ch. 4 (boys 14 and 10 in 1345) and ch. 21 (Butautas about 30 in 1365). **Conflict with ch. 10**, which gives them as 12 and 8 in 1349 (should be 18 and 14); flagged for the Part I owner.
- **The old priestess** (Birutė's grandmother, the Queen's last walking vessel) is unnamed; **she dies on the Palanga shore in autumn 1349**, a few weeks after the wedding. After ch. 11 the Queen leaves her and waits for Birutė (no vessel until 1382). She also shared a split amber bead with Birutė (two halves, both with Birutė after the old woman's death). It is not the pebble.
- **Birutė** b. c.1334 (15 in 1349, 26 in 1361). Danutė (about 3 in 1361) is the only other child named in my chapters.
- **Jonas**: before 1361 he is a cub of Šešiapirštis's brotherhood called **Žvirblis (Sparrow)**, 19 in 1361 (b. c.1342, not c.1335 as in his persona file), taken at the Šešupė ford, put to the Marienburg kitchens. An arbalest bolt in the thigh left him with a **limp** (the "kreivakojis" of Book I ch. 25). He stabs the sergeant (Samogitians call him "Jonas", the Order "Johann") at the last gate, takes the name, and returns to the Dykra and Šešiapirštis (Wolf-Father, by 1361). **Kęstutis never learns who he was**; Jonas tells no one until ch. 25. He is not one of the hidden nine in 1361 (Kaplys, named in Part I ch. 5, is). He becomes Kęstutis's armour-bearer after 1362 (Part III ch. 24 and 30 agree).
- **Alfas**: a Samogitian bondsman of the Order, mid-aged, carries water to the tower. He stays at the well and is **hanged on the wall over the kitchen gate**; Jonas watches from the reeds on the third morning.
- **Günther von Hohenstein** (spelled "fon Hohensteinas"): aged 22 in 1353, about 30 in 1361; knows Lithuanian from a Curonian nurse of the Ragnit march; asks to serve at Ragnit in 1358; present at the 1361 ford (offers Kęstutis surrender, Kęstutis refuses) and at Kaunas 1362 (pulls Vaidotas out; Vaidotas yields). He carries the prisoner's meals at Marienburg. His debt to Kęstutis (the escape, honesty) feeds the 1381 warning.
- **Kniprodė** is written "Winrichas fon Kniprodė" (matches Part III ch. 22/24), declined as a feminine-type name in -ė. He keeps a chess board with the Šešupė position and decides to take Kęstutis alive. Brother **Johannes**, his chaplain, wants to confiscate the amber; Kniprodė refuses (amber is the Order's coin).
- Casimir III saw through the ox-oath and let Kęstutis slip away at the bridge ("for Aldona").

## Objects
- **Silver wolf:** sewn by Kęstutis into the lining of his coat before every Prussian raid (Šešiapirštis's advice: "Germans take what shines"); at Marienburg he moves it into the lining of Alfas's smock for the escape (Book I ch. 8).
- **Amber bead (Birutė's):** a bead charged by Birutė (Wind, "Vėjas tau neš") the dawn he left, her price a dizzy morning. It lets her hear him on the road; it **goes cold** when the Marienburg bell rings (consecrated stone) and stays cold. The Order lets him keep it. He is shown holding it, cold, during the escape. Not the pebble.
- **Kęstutis's mark** (ch. 13): a pale fern-shaped line from palm to wrist of the right hand, ozone and burnt-flint smell. He cannot give a promise he will not keep: his throat shuts and his teeth bite his tongue (tested in ch. 13, ch. 16, ch. 17). He says "bandysiu" (I will try) instead of "pažadu". Origin of the family words *Duotas žodis nerūdija* is Birutė's line in ch. 13. At Marienburg he refuses parole and baptism for this reason.
- **Perkūnėlis sword** under the grip still bright in 1361 (warm when blood is near).

## Magic ledger (Part II)
- Ch. 11: the old woman's far-hearing ("Vėjas man neš"), cost vertigo/nausea; the Queen leaves her.
- Ch. 12: the Laimės are visitors, not workings. Prophecies as outlined (a crown travels toward him; three baptisms, none by a water he believes in; outlives every heir). Birutė says "Dar ne"; Laima: "Už 'dar' mokama." The secret name whispered at birth is **not given**.
- Ch. 13: Perkūnas's claim (justice and the kept word). Ch. 16–17: consecrated stone damps Kalba. Ch. 18: no magic.
- Ch. 20: the Steppe-Hunger shows as dust devils, horse-hair on the hand and in the well, and a voice in Algirdas's own voice: *Toliau* ("Farther"). He does not refuse (silence read as consent). He calls the south **"pietūs"** and tells his brother only "dulkių verpetus" (matches Part III ch. 22 and 25).
- No pebble, no stones, no mask. A **black dog** with no breath-cloud appears at Algirdas's stable in 1368 (ch. 15), Jogaila's first sight of the Shadow-Seller's small shape. **The Šventaragis flame does not lean toward Jogaila** at his first visit (it leaned toward the infant Vytautas in ch. 12); the Krivis: "Ugnis nieko neprašo. Ji tik atsimena."

## Deviations from the outline
1. Ch. 11 POV and dates follow the outline, but the timeline is moved to **summer 1349** so that ch. 10's night leads straight into it and Vytautas is born in 1350; Kęstutis agrees to the "taking" because the old woman argues the Palanga flame (Algirdas's clause 1) needs a husband for its last keeper. He believes the violence is real; Birutė confesses on the wedding night. The tradition's "uncomfortable, not romantic" is kept.
2. Ch. 17: Kniprodė offers freedom, his lands and a seat at the Table of Honour for baptism; Kęstutis cannot even say "I accept" (the mark). Kniprodė also offers a lighter cell for parole; Kęstutis answers "Nes pabėgsiu".
3. Ch. 19: the Kaunas siege includes a counter-mine scene and a Lithuanian-speaking conscripted miner (from Skalva) warning Vaidotas; **Vaidotas yields** and is taken alive, **fate left open** (he "disappears from the record"). No claim that it was an Amber Debt payment (Part III ch. 25 says the debt was silent after Strėva). Kniprodė is seen only as a white-mantled man on a grey palfrey whom the Order's men dispute is "the Master or only his banner" (verify, as in the outline).
4. Ch. 20 names no beys; Kyiv goes to **Vladimiras, Maria's son** (Orthodox elder line), not to Uliana's sons.
5. Jogaila is written as born spring c.1362 (post-1990 consensus, Tęgowski; historical-fact pass 2026-10-12; the older c.1352 is a minority view), so six in 1368. Ch. 15's dateline is 1350–1368; Skirgaila (b. c.1354) is his elder brother. Algirdas names him Jogaila at the bedside; Uliana baptised him **Jakov** in her own chapel.

## For other parts
- Part III ch. 21 (Butautas, 1365): ch. 12 seeds his hostility to Birutė (at 14: "Pavogta") and Vaidotas handing her the household keys ("Namo raktai turi turėti šeimininkę", echoed in ch. 19).
- Part III's "Kaunas... Atjojai, kai jau degė... velykinio ryto" is honoured. Book I ch. 8 is the only place Kęstutis speaks of Alfas (to Vytautas, 1382).
- Hohenstein's later warning (1381) can cite: "A man who told me he would run, and ran."
- Šešiapirštis's skull-stone scene (ch. 18) shows the Wolf-Father as plain, dry, six-fingered, about 36.

## Canon questions
- Ch. 10's ages for Vaidotas and Butautas (12 and 8) contradict ch. 4 and the Part III chapters (14/10 in 1345; about 30 in 1365).
- Is the "unnamed first wife" ever to be named? Left unnamed; Kęstutis says the dead's names are not spoken before Vėlinės.
- Jonas's persona gives b. c.1335 and "armour-bearer thirty years"; ch. 18 and Book I ch. 25 make him a young wolf in 1361 who joins the duke's service after 1362.
- "Baptised three times" (Laima) is left open: 1383 Wigand, 1386 Aleksandras, and a third (Q4: the Orthodox baptism c.1384, verify).
