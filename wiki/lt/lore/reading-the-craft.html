Magijos ženklų skaitymas¶

Lore of the Craft: a spoiler-aware guide for readers

„If the charm cost you nothing, ask who paid. Someone always did.“

[The saga’s invention] A village proverb of the Dykra margin, after the Unbinding.

The one rule¶

Every working shows its bill. The saga treats magic as a trade, not a superpower: rare, costly, visible on the page, and never the solution to the political problem. When a character calls on an element, look for the bill within a few paragraphs. It is a lost voice, a blind hour, a missing face, a cold hand, a tooth. If you cannot find it, read again, because the author has put it somewhere (Magic rules, §11).

How to spot the rules in a scene¶

Ask these seven questions of any magical moment:

  1. Who casts, and by which path? Born, noticed, or bound.

  2. Which mode? Kalba (asked), a claim (a Force marks you) or a bargain (a deal to the letter).

  3. Is it the zenith or off it? Off zenith, the bill is doubled.

  4. What is the bill, and where is it shown?

  5. Does it obey the Law of Truth? No illusions. Fog is real fog.

  6. Does it leave residue? The land remembers every working.

  7. Does the political problem still need mortals? It always does.

By book (spoilers ahead)¶

Each heading below spoils only its own book and the ones before it. Skip to the book you have finished.

Book I: Vilko Tremtis (spoilers for Book I)¶

Watch Birutė’s bills: sight in ch. 3, voice in ch. 11 and 14, everything that remains in ch. 27. The pebble is the book’s barometer of truth, and ch. 19’s dead amber is the key to the false baptism. Vytautas’s first Five-Voice (ch. 15) costs him warmth and part of his hearing, and the stranger in the bog is „not amber’s, but roots‘“.

Book II: Auksinis Šešėlis (spoilers for Books I-II)¶

The surety is gone, so every working’s price falls on Vytautas alone. Fire at the island vault (ch. 2, 11) and the Five-Voice at the Vorskla ford (ch. 27) show the full bill. The Lagoon witch in ch. 9 explains the Water rule and the forbidden transfer that the book never uses.

Book III: Žaliasis Miškas (spoilers for Books I-III)¶

The oath of ch. 17 is a thunder oath: blood on the perkūnėlis, an oak split by lightning, and a žynys who pays in teeth. Notice the rust on Kęstutis’s sword stop spreading.

Book IV: Nematoma Karūna (spoilers for Books I-IV)¶

The temptation at Lutsk (ch. 22) is a bargain refused: the crown of leaves, the amber’s second scar. In ch. 3 a hearth of blue lights draws his pouch ash down to four pinches, then three. His death (ch. 29) puts out the flame and cracks the pebble.

Book V: Vilko Palikimas (spoilers for Books I-V)¶

The narrator of the oath chapter is amber itself, and the last žyniai are counted aloud. Look for what the unburned dead want and for the Flame-Keeper’s refusal of Žygimantas: a flame lit for one man speaks only with his voice.

Book VI: Šventasis Miškas, the prequel (spoilers for all)¶

Read it last or first; it carries the oath itself (ch. 4), the first Water working at the ice-break (ch. 10), the thunder oath’s cost on Kęstutis’s tongue (ch. 13) and Birutė’s bead (ch. 16).
