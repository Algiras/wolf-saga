Teutonic Order

Type faction
Note State of the Teutonic Knights; military-monastic crusader order in Prussia.

Overview

[Historical record] A crusading order that settled in Prussia in 1226-30 and ruled it from Marienburg. In this saga’s years it is the Grand Duchy’s chief enemy and its main source of hostages and treaties. Grand Masters: Winrich von Kniprode (6 Jan 1352–24 Jun 1382), Konrad Zöllner von Rotenstein (elected 5 Oct 1382, d. 20 Aug 1390), Konrad von Wallenrode (1391-93), Konrad von Jungingen (1393-1407), Ulrich von Jungingen (1407, killed at Grunwald 15 Jul 1410), Heinrich von Plauen (1410-13), Michael Küchmeister (1414-22), Paul von Rusdorf (1422-41). Key dates: Dovydiškės 31 May 1380; Dubysa 31 Oct 1382; Königsberg 30 Jan 1384; Salynas 12 Oct 1398; First Peace of Thorn 1 Feb 1411; Melno 27 Sep 1422; Christmemel 19 Jun 1431; Brest 31 Dec 1435; the Prussian Confederation, 1440.

Patron Force

[The saga’s invention] Geležinis Šventasis, fed by the Reise and forced baptism. Signs: furnace heat in winter, crosses that ring like anvils. It screams at Grunwald (Book III ch. 20).

Internal tensions

The Livonian branch has its own Landmeister and feuds. The Prussian towns drift to the Confederation (1440). Plauen is deposed after Marienburg. Feuds: Vytautas, whom it baptised and armed in 1383 and who burned Marienwerder and Georgenburg in July 1384 (see the Order’s sword), and who defected again in 1392, the year his hostage sons died. Poland and Lithuania after Grunwald.

Notable members

Kniprode, Zöllner, Liebenstein, Marquard von Salzbach, the invented brother Hohenstein, Ulrich von Jungingen.
