# Book III fact-check (Žaliasis Miškas, 1399-1411)

Pass date: 2026-10-12. Rule: D3, history is the spine; invention fills gaps, or picks a side only where historians genuinely disagree, and the afterword says so. Format per item: claim -> record (source) -> action. Chapter numbers are Book III. Binding decisions applied: Jogaila b. c. 1362 (42 at Raciąż 1404, "apie penkiasdešimt" in 1411: consistent; ch 25 "dvylika metų jaunesnis už Vytautą"); Žygimantas an Order hostage 1390-98 (Book II).

Sources most used: en.wikipedia Samogitian_uprisings, Treaty/Peace_of_Thorn_(1411), Battle_of_Grunwald, Union_of_Vilnius_and_Radom, Czerwińsk_Floating_Bridge, Marquard_von_Salzbach, Ulrich_von_Jungingen, Heinrich_von_Plauen, Švitrigaila, Medininkai_Castle, Trakai_Island_Castle; VLE (vle.lt) Raciążo sutartis, Švitrigaila; lt/pl search results for the Brest council ("plan brzeski", Bieżuńskie Zeszyty Historyczne 2010), the Zapiski Historyczne 2009 study on the 1409 declaration of war; Catholic-Hierarchy for the Archbishop of Gniezno; the VU article on the name "Žalgiris" (lituanistika/zurnalai.vu.lt "Žalgiris' Battle: What Answers Do We Have to Unanswered Questions?").

## Known issues from the brief

4 (Book II item). **Marquard's insult to Birutė** -> en.wikipedia Marquard_von_Salzbach: after the Peace of Raciąż (1404) "he further insulted Vytautas by attacking his murdered mother, Birutė"; at Grunwald (komtur of Brandenburg, Ekdahl 1992 pp. 58-59; Urban 2003) Vytautas had him beheaded when he "refused to apologize"; Marquard had also nearly derailed the Raciąż negotiations by accusing Vytautas of treachery. Beheading after Grunwald with Heinrich Schaumburg confirmed (en.wikipedia Battle_of_Grunwald). -> **Moved here: ch 6**: after the signing, at the Order's evening feast, Marquard toasts "the witch" Birutė and the sea that "could not swallow her"; Vytautas answers "Aš tai prisiminsiu", for the second time he says it to Marquard (the Lyck echo from Book II ch 6). **Ch 14** (the Amber Queen names Marquard): "Marienburge ... kai tavo sūnūs dar gyveno" -> "Raciążeke, ... kai tu ką tik buvai parašęs savo vardą po taika". **Ch 21** (the execution): "Marienburge, kai mano sūnūs dar gyveno" -> "Raciążeke, kai buvo pasirašyta taika". Afterword paragraph updated.
8. **Ch 25, Thorn, 1 Feb 1411** -> The peace was signed 1 Feb 1411 at Thorn; Heinrich von Plauen signed for the Order (en.wikipedia Heinrich_von_Plauen); en.wikipedia Peace_of_Thorn does **not** say that Jogaila or Vytautas were there ("Who was present in person: the article does not say"); negotiations ran Oct 1410 (Koronowo), a truce 10 Dec 1410 - 11 Jan 1411, a three-day Jogaila-Plauen meeting at Raciąż that failed, then the treaty; a Polish abstract mentions a ratification on 10 May 1411. No source found that puts either ruler at Thorn that day. -> **Left as invention, now stated openly:** afterword now says "nežinau, ar pats Vytautas ir Jogaila buvo Torunėje". Terms: borders back to pre-1409 except Samogitia; Samogitia to Jogaila and Vytautas for life only, then back to the Order; Dobrzyń to Poland; ~14,000 captives released for 100,000 kopas of Prague groschen in four yearly instalments; **a clause that Jogaila and Vytautas will convert the remaining pagans** (en.wikipedia). **Corrected ch 25:** the clerk now reads the conversion clause (outline's "verify Thorn's clause on conversion" resolved: it exists). 100,000 kopas / four years in ch 25: confirmed.
8b. **Order's declaration of war, Aug 1409** -> en.wikipedia Battle_of_Grunwald: 6 Aug 1409; a Zapiski historyczne (2009) study: Ulrich ordered the declaration letter on 6 Aug, it reached Jagiełło on 14 Aug, hostilities from 16 Aug (New World Encyclopedia gives 14 Aug, evidently the delivery). -> Ch 12 Rūdis says "Rugpjūčio šeštąją": **confirmed** (the day of the letter); afterword now says "datuotas 6 d., įteiktas 14 d.". **Truce:** signed **8 October 1409**, expiring 24 Jun 1410 (en.wikipedia Battle_of_Grunwald and a Lithuanian source); en.wikipedia Samogitian_uprisings says 8 September (the two pages disagree). -> Ch 12 (October scene, "paliaubos bus pasirašytos šį mėnesį") **is right**; the afterword's "rugsėjo 8 d." was wrong and is **corrected to spalio 8 d.**
9. **Afterword spot checks** -> Union of Vilnius and Radom: Vilnius act 18 Jan 1401, Radom 11 Mar 1401 (en.wikipedia Union_of_Vilnius_and_Radom; negotiations from Dec 1400 in Grodno): **confirmed** (the "for life / reversion to Jogaila and the Crown" wording comes from VLE and Lithuanian sources; en.wikipedia only says after Vytautas's death Lithuania was to pass to Jogaila or his legal heir). Ulrich von Jungingen elected Grand Master **26 Jun 1407** (en.wikipedia; Julian-calendar weekday computed: Sunday, so ch 11 "išrinktas sekmadienį" is **confirmed**); b. 1360, aged ~50 at Grunwald (ch 20 "apie penkiasdešimt": confirmed), komtur of Balga 1396-1404, Marshal 1404 (so his presence at Raciąż in ch 6 is plausible). Konrad von Jungingen d. 30 Mar 1407 (not on en.wikipedia's Ulrich page; widely given): ch 11 "mirė pavasarį" ok. Brest/Grodno privileges (Book II): done there.

## Systematic pass

- **First Samogitian uprising (ch 1, 4)** -> en.wikipedia Samogitian_uprisings: began **13 March 1401**, after the Radom ratification; Samogitians captured and burned the two newly built castles (Friedeburg near the Dubysa and one near the Nevėžis); the Order had taken ~500 Samogitian hostages into Prussia; Vytautas did **not** openly back the rebels at first, entering the war in 1402 with the attack on Gotteswerder; Švitrigaila with the Order Jan 1402; Friedeburg's vogt in the sources is Heinrich von Schwelborn. -> **Corrected ch 4:** dateline "kovas" (was April), "three castles (Dubysa, Venta, Nemunas)" -> "two new castles (Dubysa, Nevėžis)"; Vytautas keeps his distance (the chapter had Kirmėlė's arms smuggled "kunigaikštis nieko nedavė": consistent). The **burning of the vogt on his horse** is **not attested for 1401** (the pagan custom of burning captured knights is attested for 13th-14th c. sources; the 1407 Samogitian complaint to the Order describes the Order burning Samogitians); left as saga, now flagged in the outline and the afterword (new paragraph "Pirmasis sukilimas"). Ch 1 hostages: "keturiasdešimt vienas vaikas" from one village is invention within the real ~500 hostages.
- **Švitrigaila 1402 (ch 5)** -> en.wikipedia Švitrigaila: January 1402 travelled disguised as a merchant to Marienburg; **2 March 1402** treaty with the Order; **July 1402** invaded with the Knights toward Vilnius, Vytautas executed six Vilnius burghers, the Knights withdrew (the last Teutonic raids into Lithuania proper); en.wikipedia Medininkai_Castle: 1402, besieged and burned by the Order with Švitrigaila (no month). The Order's treaty and raid are therefore months apart. -> **Corrected ch 5:** dateline "sausis - liepa"; the nine-day march is now a **summer** Reise ("birželio gale, kai pelkės išdžiūvo", "vasaros Reise ... per išdžiūvusias pelkes", the snow on the wall becomes "rąstai ... verkė derva"); Henė's report of Jogaila's wedding "prieš dvi dienas" -> "Žiemą" (29 Jan 1402 Anna of Celje, b. c. 1380/81: "Dvidešimt vienų" confirmed). Švitrigaila's flight: "iš Vitebsko" -> "iš savo apanažo, apsimetęs pirkliu" (he went "disguised as a merchant" per en.wikipedia; where he sat is unclear: Raciąż text says Podolia, VLE gives Seversk lands); "turėjo tik Vitebską" softened. Daugirdas, Žvirblis, Brother Dietrich remain invention.
- **Raciąż (ch 6)** -> en.wikipedia: 22 May 1404 (temporary truce Dec 1403); **VLE: 23 May 1404**, at an island in the Vistula near Raciążek castle, at the confluence of the Drwęca and Vistula, talks from Sep 1403 through Vytautas's plenipotentiaries; terms: the 1398 Salynas borders kept, the Order regains Samogitia and part of Sudovia, ends support for Švitrigaila, Poland may buy back Dobrzyń, Vytautas may move 250 Samogitian noble families into Lithuania; Smolensk retaken in **July 1404** (VLE; it had gone to Moscow/Yuri in 1401). -> **Corrected:** dateline "gegužės 22-23 d., Raciążekas prie Vyslos"; "parapinė bažnyčia" -> "pilies koplyčia"; "Smolenskas grįžo ... prieš žiemą" -> "tą pačią vasarą". Afterword "22-23 d." with the note that sources differ.
- **Ugra and the Muscovite war (ch 10)** -> War 1406-1408 and the Ugra standoff (en.wikipedia's Muscovite-Lithuanian page only describes a 1402 standoff and a Pskov dispute; a Ukrainian source puts the 1408 standoff in autumn); Švitrigaila's move to Moscow: archival work dates his departure to **June 1408**, a May 1408 charter shows him still at Halych (en.wikipedia Švitrigaila says May 1409); Edigu's raid on Moscow Dec 1408 (Vasily at Kostroma): consistent; Sofija's children (Ivan b. 1396/97, Anna b. 1393: ages 12 and 15 in Dec 1408): fine. -> **Corrected ch 10:** Ugra "rugpjūtį" -> "rudenį, rugsėjį" (both occurrences); Švitrigaila "atvyko rudenį" -> "dar tų pačių metų birželį, prieš Ugros stovėjimą"; the Pskov/Novgorod sentence softened (the record of 1406 motives is thin). The "poisoned water" episode is saga.
- **Ulrich's rise (ch 11)** -> see item 9 above. "Šimtą septyniasdešimt metų" in Prussia (1230-1407): ok.
- **Second Samogitian uprising (ch 12)** -> began **26 May 1409** (famine of 1408); Christmemel, Friedeburgh, Dobesinburg taken and burned, only Memel held; Vytautas sent deputies (**Rumbaudas Valimantaitis, Galminas, Getautas, Klausigaila, Vasibutas**); he first backed it secretly, then openly in summer 1409 after the Knights seized 20 grain ships; agreement at Navahrudak in late 1408 (en.wikipedia Samogitian_uprisings). -> **Corrected ch 12:** "gegužės dvidešimtos" -> "dvidešimt šeštos"; the rider says Vytautas "slėpsis tik iki vasaros" (was "nesislėps"). Galminas and Rumbaudas are historical names; their saga roles (root-host; elder who strikes first) are invention, which the afterword already states for Galminas and Rumbaudas as commander.
- **Brest council (ch 13)** -> early Dec 1409 (30 Nov - 7 Dec per Bieżuńskie Zeszyty Historyczne 2010, "plan brzeski"; Jogaila, Vytautas, Mikołaj Trąba); en.wikipedia Czerwińsk_Floating_Bridge places it at **Brześć Kujawski**, a Lithuanian source and dzieje.pl at **Brest-Litovsk**. -> **Left as in the book (Brest on the Bug)**, flagged as disputed in a new afterword paragraph. Sigismund b. 1368, King of Hungary from 1387: "dvidešimt dvejus metus" in Dec 1409 confirmed; his deal with the Order: no treaty date checked. Oleśnicki b. 1389, 20 in Dec 1409, royal secretary: confirmed. Jadwiga b. 8 Apr 1408 ("pusantrų metų" in Dec 1409, "trimetė" in summer 1411): ok. ch 13 "nuo to rugpjūčio, kai ten [Krėvoje] buvo pasirašyta unija" -> the Krėva union is Aug 1385, the death of Kęstutis Aug 1382; **corrected** to "nuo to 1382-ųjų rugpjūčio".
- **The Vistula bridge (ch 15)** -> parts prepared in winter/spring 1410 upstream near **Kozienice** (Radom Forest timber, master Jarosław of Kozienice), floated down ~29 Jun, assembled in about half a day on **30 Jun** above the Czerwińsk monastery (exact spot uncertain); crossing from 30 Jun to 2 Jul (en.wikipedia; a Polish source: 30 Jun - 3 Jul); the king and most Lesser Poland troops crossed on **30 Jun**; the Lithuanians arrived at Czerwińsk the same day (en.wikipedia Battle_of_Grunwald). -> **Corrected ch 15:** the king crosses "tą pačią dieną, jau popiet" (was "Antrą dieną"). The silent upstream building is kept as the book's "žmonės sakė" hearsay (confirmed by the record).
- **Grunwald (ch 16-20)** -> Order's camp at Kauernik by the Drwęca; the allies turned east on 11 Jul and burned Gilgenburg; the night of 14-15 Jul storm (Długosz); two heralds with two swords before noon (en.wikipedia: bearers not named; Długosz: Jogaila received them), Jogaila delayed by Mass (Długosz: **two** Masses); Lithuanians and part of the Polish banners attack the Order's left, the Lithuanian light cavalry retreats (feigned or real: Długosz vs Łastowski/Ekdahl's letter vs Turnbull), three Smolensk banners hold; Jungingen leads 16 banners; Oleśnicki saves Jogaila with a broken spear (Długosz); Jungingen killed (Cronica conflictus: Dobiesław of Oleśnica; Długosz: Mszczuj of Skrzynno); 203-211 of ~270 brothers killed; Jungingen's body at Marienburg 19 Jul; Jogaila wept over it and had it covered in purple (Długosz). -> **Confirmed**; **corrected ch 18:** "Jau trečia mišia ... pradės ketvirtą" -> "antra ... trečią"; Zawisza's carrying of a sword to Vytautas is saga (afterword now says so). Zawisza Czarny b. c. 1379 (31): ok. The book keeps the retreat disputed (ch 19-20 explicitly).
- **Beheadings (ch 21)** -> Vytautas orders Marquard (komtur of Brandenburg) and Heinrich Schaumburg (vogt of Sambia) executed (en.wikipedia Battle_of_Grunwald; Banderia Prutenorum); the Order meanwhile beheaded Nicholas von Renys for treason: not used. Confirmed.
- **Marienburg (ch 22)** -> Plauen (komtur of Schwetz) back at Marienburg about 17 Jul, the allied army at Marienburg 26 Jul, siege lifted 19 Sep (about two months); only 1,427 men returned for pay. -> **Corrected ch 22:** Jungingen's body "kitą dieną" -> "po dviejų dienų" (19 Jul). Vytautas leaving early (his camp sickness is a saga/hearsay reason) hedged in the chapter.
- **The Lithuanian name "Žalgiris" (ch 24 + others)** -> A VU article ("Žalgiris' Battle: What Answers Do We Have to Unanswered Questions?"): the Lithuanian form appeared only at the end of the 19th century ("Žaliagiris" attributed to Petras Vileišis 1885, "Žalgiris" to Maironis 1891); 15th-century sources have Grunwald/Griunvaldas/Tannenberg. The Latin/Ruthenian name was Grünfeld/Griunfeldas. -> **Corrected:** ch 24 (the scene in which Vilnius nobles coin "Žalgiris" now has them say "Griunvaldas" in Ruthenian, with an old noble noting that in Lithuanian it would be "žalia giria"); in-character uses of "Žalgirio lauke" changed to "Griunvaldo" (ch 25 three times, ch 26) or "Tannenbergo" (ch 22) or "Grunvaldo" (ch 30). Chapter datelines "Žalgirio laukas" (headings, narrator) kept. Afterword says so.
- **Archbishop of Gniezno (ch 24 "arkivyskupas Mikalojus")** -> Mikołaj Kurowski, Archbishop 1402-7 Sep 1411 (Catholic-Hierarchy): **confirmed**; Mikołaj Trąba (Halych) in Jogaila's party: not used.
- **Island castle (ch 28)** -> Trakai Island Castle: begun in the second half of the 14th c. (Kęstutis), main works under Vytautas "around 1409" (en.wikipedia) / first half of the 15th c. (Trakai museum); an Order architect supervised (Lithuanian article). -> **Softened ch 28:** "Ji buvo baigta" -> "Pagrindiniai darbai buvo baigti".
- **Epilogue (ch 30)** -> Oleśnicki 22 in 1411 (b. 1389), a royal secretary (the afterword says so); Długosz: Jogaila's tears and the purple shroud: **confirmed** (ch 30).
- **Ages and dates not otherwise changed** -> Vytautas 59-60 (b. c. 1350): ch 13, 25; Ona b. c. 1350, d. 1418; Anna of Celje 30 in 1411; Žiežirba b. c. 1391 (14 at Kūčios 1405, "apie dvidešimt" in 1410-11); Galminas/Ona/Eglė/Pilkoji saga.

Pilkoji's village and Ona's gift of reading; the 41 children of Dirvėnai (vs ~500 hostages overall); Rumbaudas's family; the Order's relief column at the Dubysa bridge (73 dead); Rūdis; Mingaila; the Kražiai grove council; Jogaila's confession attempts; Henė's 1405 and 1409 scenes; the ransom dispute in ch 24 (half and half); Zawisza's errand; the Medvėgalis žynys; the grove of Galminas (the record has only the field and the regrowth is saga).
