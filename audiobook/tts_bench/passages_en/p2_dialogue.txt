— Vytautas.

He flinched but did not turn around. He knew this step — heavy, but sure. His father stood in the doorway, his shadow stretching across the whole armoury, covering half the armour. Kęstutis was still wearing the same clothes in which he had received the envoys — a heavy woollen cloak that now smelled not only of smoke and mead but of something alien, sweet. His father seemed not so much tired as lost in a new time.

— Father, — Vytautas laid the sword on the table, turning the rust stain upward. — Did you see them? Did you truly see them, and not their silks?

— I saw, son. And I accepted their gifts, as our customs require. Jogaila cannot accuse us of lacking hospitality.

— Gifts! — Vytautas felt his hands involuntarily clench into fists until the nails dug into his palms. — Sable furs that rot with a single rain, and Venetian wax that smells like a woman's chamber. These are not gifts, father. They are soothing medicine. They came so that we would shut our eyes to what is real. And that chain? Did you see what mark it bears?

— I saw.

— Ryga's. The Order's city.

— They smell of peace, Vytautas, — he said, but there was no certainty in his voice. This is how a man speaks who is first trying to convince himself.

— No, father. They smell of betrayal. Sweet betrayal always hides the smell of death.
