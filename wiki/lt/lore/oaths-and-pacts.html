Priesaikos ir sutartys¶

Lore of the Craft: Priesaika, the Silent Pact and the Pack’s Word

„They swore with iron on a stone and blood on a bead, and no one asked the land whether it agreed. That is the whole of our history, if you strip off the dates.“

[The saga’s invention] A Samogitian hedge-woman, quoted in a Franciscan’s letter, c. 1415.

Priesaika: the oath that binds¶

[Historical record] Pagan Lithuanians swore by Perkūnas or the earth, and treaties were sworn with pagan rites. In 1351 Kęstutis swore to Louis of Hungary with an ox sacrificed to Perkūnas. In the saga, an oath sworn in the right form is literally binding (Priesaika; Power systems, §2.6):

An oath sworn on a claimed person’s name binds the Force too. At Grunwald’s eve in Book III ch. 17 Vytautas, still bearing Perkūnas’s mark from Krėva, swears on Kęstutis’s rust-eaten sword with his own blood, and the old žynys who conducts the rite pays with his teeth. „No one has ever sworn like this without paying.“

The Silent Pact (Tylioji Sutartis)¶

[The saga’s invention] Sworn on Vėlinės night, 1345, in the Šventaragis valley, when Algirdas and Kęstutis divided the realm after deposing Jaunutis (Book VI ch. 4; Book V ch. 5; the Pact). The Krivis is a witness, not an envoy.

A fourth party was never asked: the people. „Niekas nėra klausiamas.“ The Pact is quiet because no one asked.

Three clauses¶

  1. The flames. The sacred flames (Šventaragis, Palanga, the Samogitian groves) are never extinguished. Algirdas speaks it: „While one of my blood walks this land, no hand will put them out“ (Book VI ch. 4).

  2. No Gediminid blood. Kęstutis speaks it: no man of Gediminas’s blood will shed it. If one does, „let the gods go free, and let the land fall to whoever comes“.

  3. The Amber Debt. For every land lost to plough and cross, royal blood is owed.

The Last Flame promise¶

Vytautas’s half of the Sutartis of Book I ch. 3 is Kol aš gyvas, paskutinė ugnis neužges. After 1387 he keeps one flame lit in secret (the promise). In Book II ch. 2 he comes to understand it: „an oath is not words but a labour that repeats every night“. The promise is tested at Lutsk in Book IV ch. 22, where Henė offers the crown for the flame. Vytautas answers by speaking the vow again, and his amber blazes.

The Pack’s Word¶

The Iron Wolves swear nine rules on the skull of the Iron Wolf (The Iron Wolves, §6). A lie spoken over it is heard by every wolf in the Dykra.

Rules 2 and 9 together make the brotherhood’s tragedy: in 1382 the Šventaragis flame stands in Jogaila’s Vilnius, so the brotherhood owes Jogaila, and no wolf may lift a hand against Skirgaila whatever he does.

How an oath is broken, and what follows¶

Breaking a Kalba Sutartis brings loss of gift, curse of the elements, memory loss, madness and, in the extreme, death. Breaking the Pact itself unwinds the world in stages:

Left open: the Pact’s rite has no fixed liturgy beyond the pieces named here (iron on stone, secret names to the fire, blood on an amber bead), and it is not said what, if anything, a living person can do to renew it. Book V’s ending leaves that open.
