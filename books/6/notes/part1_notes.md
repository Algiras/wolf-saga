# Book VI, Part I (ch. 1–10): continuity notes

Facts established in prose that other parts must respect. Part II's ch. 11–12 were read and the ages, the scar and the March 1350 setting were aligned to them.

## Dateline ladder

- Ch. 1 winter 1341/42 (pyre). Ch. 2 1342–44; the bog rescue is autumn 1344; Algirdas's rider ("Palauk rudens") comes before winter 1344. Ch. 3 the night before Vėlinės 1345 (taking of Vilnius, then Algirdas at dawn). Ch. 4 Vėlinių night 1345 (the Pact). Ch. 5 December 1345 (the wolves' *krikštas*, silver wolf). Ch. 6 spring 1347. Ch. 7 2 Feb 1348 (Candlemas). Ch. 8 February 1348 (Narimantas's pyre, days later). Ch. 9 August 1348 (after Žolinė). Ch. 10 **March 1350**, the spring ice-break (outline said c. 1349–50; moved to match Part II ch. 11).

## Ages and family

- Kęstutis b. c.1297: 44 in ch. 1, 48 in 1345. Algirdas c.1296. Jaunutis c.1300 (41 in 1342). Andrius Algirdaitis 20 in 1345, 22 in 1347 (Maria's eldest). Maria of Vitebsk d. **1346**, buried in the earth in the Rusų gatvė churchyard (Algirdas allowed it).
- Kęstutis's **first wife**: alive in 1344–45, d. **c.1347** (ch. 12: "three years" before 1350). Never named; her name is no longer said at home. She gave him the Candlemas candle stub he carries (ch. 7). Sons **Vaidotas b. 1331 and Butautas b. 1335** (13 and 9 in ch. 2; 14 and 10 in ch. 4; 19 and 15 in ch. 10). Butautas's ch. 2 scene: loses a wooden-sword fight and weeps with rage because the older brother was gentle with him (seed for 1365).
- Kęstutis's mother d. when he was 23 (ch. 4); she had whispered his true name at birth.
- Birutė is 15 in March 1350, sworn at 13 (Rasos) to the shore-fire: "Aš neatiduosiu savęs jokiam vyrui". Her grandmother, *senoji*, is the Amber Queen's current vessel. The Queen speaks through her ("Tu paskutinė"). Palanga's fire sits between three stones in the lee of the hill.

## The Silent Pact (ch. 4), exact form

- Sworn on Vėlinių night 1345 in Šventaragis, by Kęstutis, Algirdas and the **Krivis** alone. The fire stands taller than men; the spirits of place gather (felt, not seen; Kęstutis has no gift). Swords are laid on a stone; the *perkūnėlis* warms.
- The Krivis says he is a witness, not the gods' envoy: the Pact is with **the land and all that lives on it and under it**. Gods give: the heartland will not be conquered; the Order may burn, raid and build on the banks but "the land will not admit it". Condition: "while you keep your word".
- Rite: each brother whispers his **true name** into the fire (Krivis does not listen); the Krivis cuts his own palm and drops blood on a **Palanga amber bead** (sent a week earlier by the old priestess: "Jūra liudys") and casts it into the flame; it hangs blue in the fire's heart and never falls.
- Algirdas: "Aš duodu ugnį. Vilniaus. Palangos. Žemaičių giraičių. Kol vienas mano kraujo vaikščios šia žeme, nė viena ranka jų neužgesins." Kęstutis: "Aš duodu kraują. Joks Gedimino sūnus nepralies Gedimino sūnaus kraujo. Jei kuris pralies, tegul dievai eina laisvi, ir tegul žemė atitenka tam, kas ateis."
- The **third clause is voiced by the fire alone** (not in words; a pressure): *"Už kiekvieną vagą, kurią išars kryžius, karaliaus sūnaus kraujas. Kas sumokės?"* Both brothers look away. Kęstutis says it aloud (reason: an unspoken debt is collected in the wrong coin; a spoken one can at least be counted). **Algirdas's raised hand hangs in the air for one or two heartbeats**: he was about to say it himself and Kęstutis took that choice from him. "Tu pasakei." / "Kada?" Algirdas never forgives it (ch. 8: "negaliu to išsiaiškinti").
- **Algirdas demands silence** ("Skolą, kuri žinoma, pradeda skaičiuoti visi"). The Krivis: "Tai ir bus tylioji sutartis." "Niekas nėra klausiamas. Vaikai gimsta į skolas. Tai ir yra šeima."
- **Bill:** the Krivis's fire-work costs him soul-chill: he feels nothing for about three days ("Dažniausiai"). Kęstutis stands surety for nothing; the cost is moral (his two sons).
- Algirdas had been approached by the Krivis a year before and kept it from Kęstutis ("Palauk rudens"). Whether Mantigirdas opened the gate on his own or was asked, Kęstutis never learns.
- Wolves at the Vilnius hall doors in ch. 3 let Kęstutis pass: "Ugnis nepasakė" (the flame is the Krivis's; rule 9).

## Krivis

- Unnamed in dialogue; his private name **Sirvydas** appears once (ch. 8) as something no one says; he took the staff c.1322 after the old Krivis, who told the boy Kęstutis the dream (ch. 1). He keeps a **knotted cord** under his shirt (one knot after Strėva) and two amber beads: the Pact bead (in the fire's heart) and a wet one that appeared on the stone after Narimantas's pyre, smelling of the sea: "Taip jūra rašo. Todėl tai ir vadinasi gintaro skola." The old vaidilutė **Ginta** heard the extra words at the pyre and asked nothing.
- The funeral formula: "Skola atiduota. Kelias atviras." (eldest son lights the first log, paying for the spleens). At Narimantas's pyre the Krivis adds, aloud, **"Pirmoji skola sumokėta."** Most of the crowd does not notice.

## Wolves

- Wolf-Father **Kuolas**: broad, grey at one temple, speaks little; was once drowned in the same bog. Šešiapirštis is his intended successor ("Šitas liks").
- **Silver wolf** given in December 1345 on the bog-island, wrapped in linen: thumb-sized, snout raised, old pattern marks on the flank, and under the belly the brotherhood's sign (**a wolf's head with no collar**); the silver is from spurs and buckles of three knights the wolves drowned the winter before. "Kas mato šitą, mato mus. Parodyk jį pelkėje. Ir duok duonos." Kęstutis hangs it on a leather thong at his chest from then on.
- **Hidden nine** chosen by bread (each wolf decides whether to take it): **Kaplys** (grey Samogitian; he carries the sewn-up moss, packs Kęstutis's cheek), **Briedys, Šepetys, Kirvis, Nevėžis**, four unnamed. The roster is not fixed over the years (Jonas joins later). Kuolas: "Jūs einate ne pas jį. Jūs einate pas ugnį, kurią jis laiko."
- **Šešiapirštis**: c.20 in 1345; left hand with an extra finger beside the little one; named so by Kęstutis in the 1344 bog ("Kunigaikščio žodis sunkus. Jį sunku atšaukti"). In the *krikštas* he is pressed under the mud; **a part of him stays below and breathes slightly later than he does** (he hears it for life). His mother ran his wake (three bowls, bread turned upside down: "Šiuose namuose tavo vardo nebėra"); he cannot weep. The Iron Wolf's skull, "grey as iron", lies on a flat stone on the island (the outline's "Lizdeika's skull" corrected to canon).
- Ch. 9: the first Reise column drowned (Aug 1348); **geležinė valanda** costs him the memory of the way to his birth village ("Dabar tau visur namai"). One Prussian guide is spared to carry the howls. Next morning the Root-Mother has taken the land (alder grove over a burnt hillfort); **the way back took two days instead of one**. Only then do people call it **Dykra** (lower-case *dykra* before ch. 9).
- Wolves take nothing not given and repay at once: twelve Order horseshoes tied with a cord for a loaf (ch. 2); the old woman's crust (ch. 9).

## Objects

- Kęstutis's sword: forged autumn 1341 by the Trakai smith, a *perkūnėlis* under the grip fed once with his blood; no nick in 1341. In ch. 1 he lays his old (1323) sword at his father's feet.
- Kęstutis's scar: left cheek, jaw to temple, from a sergeant's long-handled axe at Strėva; pulls the eye-corner down; hearing worse on that side; edges greenish from Kaplys's bog-moss (aligned with Part II ch. 11).
- Candle stub from his wife (never lit). Grabnyčios candle: Lithuanian women light it against lightning, the Order for the Virgin (ch. 7).
- Rusų gatvė judgment oak, the 1347 place (Part III ch. 23 reuses it).

## Strėva (ch. 7)

- The Order column returns up the frozen river with captives; kneels at Candlemas prayers; **furnace heat** melts the ice round them; crosses on shields ring like anvils. Kęstutis never learns whether Dusemer was under the Master's banner. Algirdas's horn sounded "when it should" (Kęstutis: too late); neither brother ever compares versions. The sergeants kill the tied captives rather than lose them. **Narimantas (Gleb), Pinsk** falls to a white knight's horse and sword; Kęstutis kills the knight. More than a thousand Lithuanians dead. Pinsk's Orthodox priest prays "ir už tave".
- Manvydas (eldest) lights the first log at both pyres (ch. 1, 8); his own death in 1348 is not mentioned.

## Ledger / small shapes (Shadow-Seller, no mask)

- Ch. 6: a clerk on an overturned barrel with a wax tablet of tally strokes, clean fingers, "Jūsų vardas jau įrašytas, kunigaikšti. Kaip ir visų, kurie praeina"; a warm-coin heat on Kęstutis's neck. Ch. 8: a black dog at the edge of the firelight; its shadow falls **toward** the fire; the Krivis recognises a velniukas and does not drive it off ("sąžininga sutartis turi turėti liudytoją"). Both left no tracks or only a damp ring.

## Other minor names

- **Mantigirdas** (night-watch captain, Samogitian, whom Gediminas once rewarded). **Gaudėnas** (the vaitas who hanged the 1347 martyrs; decent, afraid). **Daugailė** (refugee woman, ch. 2). Franciscans (two) present at Gediminas's pyre and kneeling at the oak. The 1347 martyrs are called Antonijus, Ivanas, Eustatijus (Eustatijus once held Kęstutis's horse).
- Kęstutis checks the three flames (Vilnius, Palanga, one Samogitian grove) every spring (ch. 10).

## Deviations from the outline

- Ch. 1: the outline says the Krivis told Kęstutis the dream "as a boy". Canon puts the dream in 1323, when Kęstutis was 26; the text keeps it by saying everyone who first carries a sword into the valley is called "boy".
- Ch. 5: the skull is the Iron Wolf's (canon), not Lizdeika's.
- Ch. 10: dated March 1350, not 1349.
- Ch. 3: Jaunutis leaves east by the Smolensk road, unharmed; Kęstutis promises him no Gediminid will shed his blood. Ch. 3 plants the wooden Crooked Castle on Bald Hill (taken without a blow; Kęstutis leaves thirty of his men there and forgets it).
- Ch. 4: the song's "the brothers looked away" and Book V's "Kęstutis said it aloud, Algirdas hesitated" are both on the page.

## Canon questions

- Krivis's name (Sirvydas) used only once, privately.
- The Palanga flame is clause 1's second flame; the old priestess sends the Pact's amber (outline did not say so).
- Whether the clause-2 curse should reach Butautas (a Gediminid's son, not a "Gedimino sūnus") is left to Part III (see its note).
