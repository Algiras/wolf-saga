Canon decisions

Source: world_rules/canon_decisions.md

The single place where contradictions get resolved. If any other doc, outline or chapter disagrees with this file, this file wins until it is amended. Each entry records the decision, the date, why, and what must change.

Precedence for everything else: canon_decisions.md > cosmology.md / magic_rules.md / master_chronology.md > other world_rules/ docs > persona/ > outlines > chapter text (the text gets fixed to match canon, not the other way round).

D1. Birutė is a vessel who becomes the Amber Queen

Decided 2026-10-10 by the author.

  • Birutė is born mortal, the last of the Palanga amber-priestess line (vaidilutės of the shore flame). The Amber Queen (Gintaro Karalienė) is not a person but an office of grief and memory first held by the sea goddess Jūratė (see cosmology.md §3.4). It needs a living vessel to act in the Middle World.
  • Book I traces her becoming: she hears the Queen (ch. 3), is changed by widowhood (ch. 11, the eyes go sea-dark), and is fully transformed at Palanga (ch. 27, the amber kneeling woman).
  • 00_pradzia.qmd (“I am the one they call the Amber Queen, though my true name was Birutė”) is her post-transformation voice. It is canon as a frame narration.
  • Must change: hidden_immortals.md §1 (no longer “never mortal”; no “drowning in Book II”); persona/Birute/divine_origin.md.

D2. Henė (the Shadow-Seller) is a shard of Velnias

Decided 2026-10-10 by the author.

  • Šešėlių Pardavėjas is a splinter of Velnias, lord of the dead, of wealth and of bargains. He wears mortal masks. His best known is Henė the jester, and his first mask in the saga is Hanulas, the Riga-born Vilnius merchant who historically opened Vilnius to Jogaila in 1382 (Saga invention: the names echo).
  • He changes masters: Jogaila’s court (1382), Skirgaila (1387–97), Vytautas’s court (from 1392), Sigismund of Luxembourg (1429–30). He always serves the deal, never the man.
  • The void beneath his mask (ch. 30) is the realm of the dead seen from inside, all of the past at once. Fix the ch. 30 line “neither pagan god nor Christian devil, the essence of time and fate” to “not a man, but the hollow where the dead keep the past”.
  • His props are three blackened stones (the three cousins). The docs’ “three black balls” are corrected to stones.
  • Must change: ch. 30 wording; hidden_immortals.md §2 (Lutsk is Book IV, not V).

D3. History is the record plus mythic gaps

Decided 2026-10-10 by the author.

  • Fixed: real dates, real names of office-holders, real outcomes of battles, treaties, deaths and baptisms (see master_chronology.md).
  • Free: private conversations, motives, unrecorded minor characters, how something happened when the chronicles are silent or contradictory, and anything supernatural.
  • Magic may explain a gap (how Kęstutis died silently, how Vytautas passed the guards). It may not contradict a fact (Kniprode alive in late 1382, Jogaila baptised in Vilnius).
  • When chronicles disagree (e.g. Birutė drowned at Brest in 1382 per the Order chronicler Wigand of Marburg (whether Długosz repeats it: verify) vs. lived to about 1416 per Lithuanian tradition), the saga may pick either and say why in-world. Our pick: both are true. Jogaila’s men tried to drown her in 1382, and the sea refused her (the ring-thrice motif). She “died” a second time by choice in 1384 (ch. 27).
  • Must change: see books/1/story_review.md §3 (H1–H13).

D4. Real names in prose and docs

Decided 2026-10-10 by the author.

  • Prose and docs use Vytautas, Jogaila, Kęstutis, Ona, Skirgaila, Birutė, Švitrigaila, Jadvyga (Jadwiga).
  • “Vyti” survives only as the intimate vocative used by Birutė and Kęstutis. In narration, “Vytautas” always. “Vytis” in narration means the coat of arms (the mounted knight), never the man.
  • The jester is spelled Henė in Lithuanian text and Henne in English docs.
  • Must change: persona folders renamed (Vytis → Vytautas, Jagelis → Jogaila, Kestus → Kestutis, Anna → Ona); books/1/outline.md name table; stray “Kestutis”/“Kęsto” in ch. 1 and 10.

D5. The two Eglės are one woman

Recommended by the reviewer 2026-10-10; the author may override.

  • Eglė is the last vaidilutė of the eternal flame in the Šventaragis valley (Vilnius), not Romuva (Romuva lay in Order-held Prussia by 1382). The prologue’s prophecy night is her last night as a mortal priestess.
  • After the captain’s insult she leaves the city on foot and, days later, walks into the Dykra and is taken by the Root-Mother, becoming Pelkių Eglė, the swamp’s voice. Her žaltys is the folk-tale inheritance of Eglė žalčių karalienė (see legends_and_songs.md).
  • She is not an amber priestess. Ch. 15’s “Tu esi Gintaro Šventikė” becomes Vytautas’s mistake, and Eglė’s silence corrects him: “Ne gintaro. Šaknų.”
  • Must change: prologue location; ch. 15 line; senoves_kalba_magic_system.md lineage examples (fine as is: Forest Whisperers).

D6. One cosmology, three layers

Reviewer decision 2026-10-10, required to make D1 and D2 coherent.

The five-element Senovės Kalba and the Meddling Forces are one system seen from two sides. Gods are the source, Forces are gods hungry and meddling in history, and Kalba is the mortal craft of asking. Full rules are in cosmology.md and magic_rules.md.

D7. The Silent Pact is the series engine

Reviewer decision 2026-10-10, unifies Books I–V.

  • In 1345, when Algirdas and Kęstutis took power, they swore the Tylioji Sutartis with the old gods. The gods would keep Lithuania unconquered while a Gediminid kept the sacred flames, and they would take royal blood for land lost (the “Amber Debt”).
  • Kin-slaying breaks the Pact’s protection (Kęstutis, 1382). Jogaila’s baptism (1386–87) ends it formally. Birutė’s sacrifice (1384, ch. 27) buys a remnant: the Hidden Groves, the gods’ last foothold (Samogitia, until 1413–17).
  • Books II–V are the unwinding of the remnant. Book V’s flashbacks show the oath itself.

D9. Kinship terms (applies to all prose)

  • Kęstutis → Jogaila, Skirgaila, Švitrigaila: dėdė (uncle), and they are his sūnėnai.
  • Vytautas ↔︎ Jogaila, Skirgaila: pusbroliai (cousins).
  • Ona → Kęstutis: uošvis; Ona → Birutė: anyta.
  • Algirdas and Kęstutis: broliai.

D10. The Iron Wolves serve two masters

Proposed 2026-10-10 in iron_wolves.md; the author must confirm.

  • The vilkatai are the Root-Mother’s servants (cosmology.md §3.2) and are sworn to whoever keeps a Gediminid sacred flame. They may never shed Gediminid blood.
  • In 1382 this splits the brotherhood: the Vilnius packs owe Jogaila and stand aside during the ch. 7 massacre at Jogaila’s camp near Trakai (seven of the hidden nine die there; Jonas’s men stayed in Kęstutis’s own camp). After 1387, Vytautas’s secret flame is the only one left, so every surviving wolf answers to him. New wolves can be made only at a living flame, so the brotherhood dies when the flame goes out in 1430.
  • Only wolves sworn twenty years or more can take wolf shape. The folk belief that all vilkatai shape-shift is half wrong.

D11. Choices made while realigning the outlines and revising Book I (2026-10-10; the author may override)

  • Where the Last Flame is: lit in 1387 in Kęstutis’s old hunting lodge beyond the Neris (Book I ch. 29); moved by Vytautas to the half-flooded foundations of the Trakai island castle while Skirgaila holds Trakai (Book II); in the finished island-castle vault after Ostrów (1392 →).
  • Who carries the 1387 ember: Vytautas himself (Book I ch. 29). The Book II outline’s mention of Kalvis (iron_wolves.md) is to be read as Kalvis guarding the lodge flame.
  • The flame goes out at the moment of Vytautas’s death (IV ch. 29); Book V ch. 1 replays the same night from the vault.
  • Galminas becomes a grove at Grunwald (1410); the “last žynys” of Book V is his pupil.
  • The silver wolf passes Kęstutis → Vytautas (through the Krėva stones, Book I ch. 8) → Pilkoji → the Flame-Keeper (Kūčios 1430–31).
  • The tier-4 count from 1387 to 1410 is one: “the Medvėgalis žynys”, who dies at Grunwald.
  • Eglė’s price is “never sell the forest”, which falls due at Salynas (1398).
  • Vaidotas and Butautas are sons of an earlier, unnamed wife of Kęstutis (they are too old for a c. 1350 marriage to Birutė).
  • The Krėva silence in 1382 is Henė’s Tuštumos Kvėpavimas (Breath of the Void); the White Silence first touches Krėva as frost in Nov 1382 (ch. 14) and settles over it at the 1385 union (ch. 28b).
  • Krėva escape date: the eve of Vėlinės (late Oct/early Nov 1382): Wind’s zenith halves the fog’s cost.
  • Ona was released by Skirgaila (to track Vytautas), fled via Brest to Masovia, and became the Order’s surety at Königsberg (1383–84); released by exchange in 1384; the sons stay at Marienburg.
  • The Flame-Keeper’s working skin-name is Žiežirba.
  • Ages of the two fosterlings (settled by the books): Pilkoji is born c. 1365 (38 in 1404, 84 in 1449; this answers Q13). Žiežirba is born c. Oct 1390 – Mar 1391 (5 in winter 1395–96, 14 and nearly 15 at Kūčios 1405). He is sworn at that Kūčios, below the usual age of 16 (iron_wolves.md §4): the single exception to the rule, because he already lives at the flame. He tends the vault flame as a boy from 1399.

Revised manuscript continuity — 13 October 2026

For future story edits, use the shared constraints and intentional exceptions in the bilingual alignment record (docs/LITHUANIAN_ALIGNMENT.md), together with the final case-closure report (docs/FINAL_CASE_CLOSURE.md).
