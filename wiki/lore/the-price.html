The Price

Lore of the Craft: costs and the Law of Balance

lore

“The sea never asks what you wanted. It asks only what you took, and it keeps the tally in your own hands.”

[Saga] A Palanga fisherman’s saying, set down by the amber-keepers.

The Law of Balance

Kiek paimi, tiek atiduok: as much as you take, give back. Magic in the saga is a trade, not a superpower. It is rare, it costs something the reader can see on the page, and it never solves the political problem; it only buys time to face it (Magic rules, §4–5.1).

Three rules govern every bill:

  1. The caster pays. For tau (another) and man (oneself) alike, though man is lighter.
  2. Time and place scale the bill. At the zenith the cost is halved; off it, doubled (elements and zeniths). Consecrated stone doubles it again.
  3. A cost cannot be refused, only deferred, and deferred costs compound: memory first, then health, then years of life, then madness, then death.

The Water-healing rule

Vandens taisyklė paprasta: whoever heals takes the wound into herself. In Book II ch. 9 the old Lagoon witch could draw the poison from the hostage sons “through the hair and the blood, across the whole distance”, but her body is too old to carry two children’s poison. She names the forbidden transfer: a small, unlearned body to take it in her place, the child of the poisoner. “Debt poured into another body that did not choose it finds its owner, sooner or later.” The rule is stated there, never exercised, and Vytautas’s refusal is itself a price paid in sons.

Surety: who pays when someone is bound

In the Sutartis of Book I ch. 3 Birutė speaks Jo skola — mano skola. From 1382 to 1384 she pays part of every bill Vytautas incurs: she hears a fog that costs her voice (Book I ch. 14), and he feels the burning amber at his chest “as if the pain were not his”. At Palanga in Book I ch. 27 she pays the whole remainder and the surety ends. In Book II ch. 27 he thinks of the earlier price in the bog, when “his mother paid part”, and knows that this time no one will pay for him. See Oaths and pacts.

Unpaid debts

A deferred cost does not vanish. Three examples:

  • Žemyna’s price for the burned village (Book I ch. 20) arrives as a red blister under his pouch, “the first part of the debt”, and the amber dies again once she has taken what was hers.
  • Eglė’s price, never to give the living forest to the axe, is held as a pledge, not paid. Vytautas breaks it again in Book II ch. 19 by making peace on the Order’s terms, and the Root-Mother’s reckoning follows at Salynas (1398).
  • The Flame promise. If the Last Flame dies while Vytautas lives, the Pact’s penalties fall on him: madness, then death.

Who always pays

Every working in the notes shows a bill, even when the page does not show the payer. The one exception is the Shadow-Seller, whose bargains are bound only by their letter (What magic cannot do).

Canon gap, flagged: the notes name the Water transfer as “forbidden and costly” but never fix what the cost is, beyond “the debt finds its owner”. This page does not specify it.
