Mokyklos ir magijos praktikai¶

Lore of the Craft: six schools, six tiers, and the last žyniai

„In my grandmother’s day there were forty hands in Lithuania that could speak to the earth. In my mother’s, perhaps twenty. I have counted six, and I am one of them.“

[The saga’s invention] An old beekeeper to a boy with a lantern, paraphrased from the oral record of the last žyniai.

The six schools of asked power¶

Asked power has six schools. Granted power (claims, the Wolf-Change, the Iron Saint’s gift) and bought power (bargains) stand outside them (Power systems, §1–3).

[Historical record] Four of the six are real folk practice taken literally: charms, signs, herbs and oaths. Amber is attested as a medium and a grave good. The five-element Kalba is a saga system, built on real god-names of uneven reliability.

The last žyniai¶

Kantaudas in Book II ch. 19 is „one of seventeen or eighteen remaining hands that can still speak with the earth“, and when he stands petrified Gedgaudas knows „now there will be fewer“. Galminas becomes a grove at Grunwald (1410). The last žynys of Book V is his pupil, the beekeeper (d. 1448), who tells the Flame-Keeper in Book V ch. 27: „There were eight when you came. Now with me there are six. After me five will remain. None will look for another.“ Once he has given his words away, he says, „a žynys dies with his words; a singer can die without them.“ See also Birutė, the Krivis, Eglė and Galminas.
