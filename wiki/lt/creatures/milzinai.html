Milžinai (giants)
Sleepers in the land · Baltic folk belief, saga-true

Origin [Historical record] giants who threw stones, built hill-forts and dwindled (Neringa made the Curonian Spit) · [The saga's invention] asleep in the land

Tier Elder things · beneath Žemyna

Danger None while asleep

Habitat The hills themselves: their bones are the hills; barrows and hill-forts

Wants Nothing. They take no part in events

Takes Nothing, unless you dig into their barrows or move their stones: the earth moves back

Signs Boulders with hand-prints (milžinų akmenys); hill-forts nobody remembers building; tremors under barrows

Banes None needed, and none would work

Traits

Half-wrong truth. The folk say the giants dwindled into ordinary people. They did not: they sleep, and it is men who dwindled, small enough to bargain.

The measure of loss. They are what Lithuania's people were in the age of the first oaths.

Actions

Turning over (after 1387). Still asleep, but landslips on hill-forts and stones that move between one dawn and the next.

Costs

Digging into a barrow. The earth moves back.

Folk source [Historical record]¶

Lithuanian legends credit giants with shaping the land: throwing great stones, building hill-forts, heaping hills. The giantess Neringa is said to have made the Curonian Spit. Many tales say the giants dwindled over generations into ordinary people.

In the saga (where the folk are half-wrong)¶

The giants are not gone; they are asleep in the land, and their bones are the hills. They are what the people were in the age of the first oaths, before men became small enough to bargain. They take no part in events, and that is the point: they measure how much has already been lost. They stand under Žemyna.

Signs & habitat¶

Boulders with hand-prints (milžinų akmenys); hill-forts nobody remembers building; earth-tremors under barrows.

Before / after the Unbinding¶

Before: sleeping. After: still sleeping but turning over: landslips on hill-forts, stones that move between one dawn and the next.

Banes, wards, bargains, and their cost¶

None are needed and none would work. Do not dig into barrows or move giants‘ stones; the earth moves back.

Danger level¶

None (asleep).

Story hooks¶

Book V epilogue: the grandfather’s story of the Iron Wolf told on a giant’s stone, as the old world dwindles into folklore just as the giants did.
