Sandėris (bargain)

Type school
Family bought
Note Sandėris: transactional supernatural contract sealed with an explicit price.

What it is

Sandėris: bought power. A deal worded precisely, with the Shadow-Seller (a shard of Velnias) or a lesser spirit, binding the parties only by the letter, never by the Three Laws.

Who can use it (path & tier)

Anyone desperate (tier 0). Canon bargainers: Jogaila (1380: the “easy way” of the trap), Skirgaila (Krėva), and later Vytautas (Book II’s Vorskla vision; Book IV’s crown). Either party may initiate; the human is rarely informed.

How it works (medium, words, timing)

Velnias never cheats; he drafts. The deal is free at first; the “thread” comes later, a small favour then a larger one. Signs of a bargain in progress: sounds die (the Breath of the Void), warm coins, a figure at the edge of the firelight, the three black stones.

Costs

Paid later, with interest. The price is “something worth more than you thought”. For Jogaila: his line inherits the crown and loses the land. For Skirgaila: to be used and discarded. Deferred costs compound.

Limits & counters

Beatable only by cleverness, never strength or faith: the folk crop-division tale (ATU 1030) is the model, with the peasant planting turnips and rye. A Praurimė seer can see through the Shadow-Seller’s illusions, at the cost of blindness. The Shadow-Seller alone can lie; mortals cannot.

Folk basis [Record]

[Record]. There is no recorded “bargain” system. The folk base is tales of the devil’s contract, the clever peasant who cheats Velnias (ATU 1030) and the aitvaras-keeper whose house burns if the spirit is not fed; the treasure that is Velnias’s. The saga makes the folk figure literal.

Notable practitioners

Jogaila, Skirgaila (graph tier 0); Vytautas’s Vorskla vision (1399); Žygimantas (Book V ch. 19). Henė admires the 1411 Toruń clause “for the lifetimes of king and prince” as a sentence the Devil himself could not draft more cleanly (Book III ch. 25). Book I ch. 19 is titled “Velnio Sandėris”; Book IV ch. 22 “Paskutinis Sandėris” (Lutsk).
