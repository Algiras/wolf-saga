# Birutė (The Priestess-Queen)

**True Name**: Birutė
**Title**: Duchess of Trakai, wife of Kęstutis; *vaidilutė* of the Palanga shore-flame; from 1384, the **Amber Queen** (*Gintaro Karalienė*)
**Age**: About 50 in 1382 (born c. 1330s; married c. 1350 at about fifteen). Mortal life ends by choice at Palanga in 1384
**Archetype**: The Forbidden Love / The Vessel
**Pronunciation**: bi-RU-tė · /bʲɪˈrʊtʲeː/ · English sound-alike "bih-ROO-tay"
**Power**: **Tier 4** (Gintaro šventikė: all five elements, Senovės Sutartis, anchored amber), born to the gift down the Palanga maternal line. Becoming a **tier 5 vessel** of the Amber Queen through Book I: hears the Queen (ch. 3), is changed by widowhood (ch. 11, 1382), and is fully transformed at Palanga (ch. 27, 1384). Afterwards she acts only through amber she touched (D1; `world_rules/power_systems.md` §4).
**Note on names**: calls her son "Vyti".

## Appearance
*   Hauntingly beautiful in youth. Long, white-blonde hair.
*   In maturity, she possesses a "terrible grace."
*   After 1382 her eyes go sea-dark; her voice fails after the Krėva fog and her sight dims as she pays her son's debts.

## Personality
*   **Spiritual**: Truly believes in her vows to the gods, and keeps their letter even when she bends their spirit.
*   **Unendingly Loyal**: She let herself be "stolen" by Kęstutis and loved him all his life, never once lying to him.
*   **Mystical**: The sea speaks to her through amber.

## Motivation
*   **Protection of the Sacred**: Guarding the spiritual heart of Lithuania from the Cross.
*   **The Mother**: Ensuring Vytautas inherits his father's strength but not his father's fate. She stands surety for him: "Jo skola — mano skola."

## Record and canon
*   **[Record]** Tradition makes her a Palanga *vaidilutė* married to Kęstutis c. 1350. One chronicle tradition says Jogaila's men drowned her in 1382; Lithuanian tradition says she returned to Palanga and lived to about 1416.
*   **[Canon, D3]** Both are true: the drowning was attempted in 1382 and the sea refused her (the ring thrice). She "died" a second time, by choice, in 1384.
