# Book III, Part 3 (ch. 21–30): notes for other parts

## Dates and places used
- **Ch. 21** (night 15–16 Jul 1410): Vytautas's camp at the edge of the field; the burning Order camp; Polish *Bogurodzica* in the distance. Beheaded in the dark: **Markvardas fon Zalcbachas** (Brandenburg komtur) and **Heinrichas Šaumburgas** (Sembos vaitas); the reason for the second is deliberately never recorded.
- **Ch. 22** (26 Jul – 19 Sep 1410, Marienburg): Plauen rides in on 17 Jul with what he could raise at Schwetz; Ulrich's body arrives the next day and is laid in St Anne's chapel; the royal army arrives 26 Jul; Plauen burns the suburbs; the siege is lifted on 19 Sep. Vytautas's departure in early Sept is shown only by Plauen's three rumours (sickness, Samogitia, "something else": he stood alone before the church's Madonna mosaic).
- **Ch. 23** (autumn 1410, the field): twelve days' walk from the Dykra; the grove lies between the field and Lubienio lake, over the trench graves; the alder bog of ch. 19 beside it holds the Order knights the roots took.
- **Ch. 24** (Kraków 11 Nov 1410 = St Martin, the prisoners' report day; Vilnius, early winter): see below.
- **Ch. 25** (Thorn, 1 Feb 1411): Dobrzyń to Poland; prisoners freed for **100,000 kopas of Prague groschen in four years** (sum stated once; verify); Samogitia "for the lifetimes of King Vladislovas and Grand Duke Vytautas, and no longer". No conversion clause is stated: the young Order envoy only says "kai Dievas pašauks jus abu, Žemaitija grįš po kryžiumi".
- **Ch. 26** (a week after St George's day, 1411; the Kražiai grove; Mingaila the eldest of 23 elders).
- **Ch. 27** (Wawel, early June 1411).
- **Ch. 28** (May 1411, Trakai island castle; the vault on the east side by the landing).
- **Ch. 29** (Rasos night, 23–24 Jun 1411, the Dykra).
- **Ch. 30** (summer 1411, Kraków chancery).

## Facts established (please respect)
**Vytautas.** After the lightning column of ch. 20 the **left ear hears nothing at all** (before: deaf in practice); the right ear works and rings. He now sits so speakers are on his right (Jogaila notices at Vilnius; Ona has been reading his lips since the Vorskla). The sword's **thin bright line** (plienas švietė sidabru) from the guard to the point stays: the rust has not spread since the oath of ch. 17. Pebble **warm** and *remembers for him*: since Grunwald the warmth brings the dead's faces (the boys, in ch. 21 and 25; "Akmuo atsimena už jį. Ji nepasakė, kad atmintis boli."). He has **no joy** at Grunwald; he is relieved that the Medvėgalis žynys, the only witness, is dead, and pays for the feeling by noticing it. He had Marquard beheaded *because* of his toast at Marienburg (ch. 14: "jūra negalėjo manęs nuryti"); Marquard answered "Kad gaila" and did not beg. Pebble neither warms nor cools at the killing ("akmuo neatsiliepia į teisingumą, jis atsiliepia į tiesą"). Wears plain black armour, silver wolf, grey ash again in the lines of his right hand by Feb 1411 (he washed it, it returned).
**The Order's sword** (one of the two sent by Ulrich, ch. 18) stands where Vytautas planted it, in the camp; no one touches it overnight (ch. 21 ends on its shadow). Not followed further: treat it as taken to the king's treasury afterwards (the record's "two swords" as Polish relics).
**The Medvėgalis žynys:** died on his cart at the bog (ch. 20); the grove-fire in his pot went out with him (ch. 21 shows the cold pot with a pinch of grey ash, which Kirmėlė carried); his clenched fist held his **teeth** (the price of the oath), which a Samogitian put back in his mouth before the pyre; no one else knows what he saw. Tier 4 = 0.
**Wolves at Grunwald:** 23 went in (ch. 19); **seven died in the bog** (ch. 20); **Rūdis's** body (in the Order cloak, killed by an unknowing Samogitian youth with an axe) was brought out by **Pilkoji** and carried with them to the bog as the eighth; eight howls at night, only Rūdis's name known to Vytautas. Pušis, Kirmėlė, Pilkoji, Žiežirba all alive.
**Ona.** At Trakai in May 1411: a dry cough ("tai tik metai"; clean handkerchief), ink to the first knuckle, writes to Moscow, Mazovia and the Samogitian elders throughout the campaign. Her letter to the field (ch. 21, read aloud by **Žiežirba**, who learned to read from her in secret): "Belaisvis, kurio kaina žinoma, vertas daugiau nei galva"; "Grįžk, jei gali. Jei negali, žinok, kad aš žinau." Vytautas realises "she knew everything" (the flame; Book IV ch. 9 can pay this off). She tells him on the landing "Aš nieko neklausiau... tai nebuvo aklumas."
**Jogaila.** Weeps over Ulrich's body next day (Długosz; seen by Oleśnicki; ch. 30) and has it wrapped in purple and sent to Marienburg. The **fifty-one Order banners** hang in Wawel cathedral; the White Silence's frost creeps from Jadvyga's tomb up the wall and stiffens them (ch. 24). Krėva: at Vilnius (early winter 1410–11), standing on the plot where the Šventaragis flame burned (nothing was built there), he begins "Krėvoje... prieš tai, kai Skirgaila nuėjo žemyn. Jis atėjo pas mane ir paklausė." and **sound dies**; Vytautas lays a hand on his shoulder (the first touch since Krėva; it is warm and the snow melts round it) and says "Aš nieko negirdėjau... Tegul taip lieka. Kol kas." The sentence was never completed aloud. In the dream of ch. 27 he realises the question *"Ar jis turi gyventi, Vladislovai?"* had Skirgaila's mouth but **Henė's voice** (aksomas, a distant broken cup); he had told himself for 29 years it was his brother. Confession to the Wawel Franciscan fails the same way (sound dies; the priest absolves him "nuo to, ko nebuvo girdėjęs"). Queen **Anna** (of Celje; called Anna in Lithuanian to avoid Ona) and the three-year-old daughter **Jadvygėlė** (b. April 1408): the child's voice is the only one that reaches him whole through the Silence; she gives him a **grey river pebble** ("kad atšiltų") and sound returns for the morning. Henė, from Vytautas's court, appears in his dream turning **Jogaila's stone** (one of two); he foretells wives (Anna goes soon, another, "trečioji pagimdys") and two sons "kai jūs jau nebesitikėsite".
**Henė.** Two stones throughout (ch. 25: "Du gyvenimai. Du akmenys. Sąžiningas pasiūlymas"; he admires the treaty's clean wording: "pats Velnias nebūtų sugalvojęs švaresnio sakinio"). In ch. 22 he appears to Plauen as a grey Treasury clerk (book, ink, shadow falls sideways) and advises waiting ("Jis išeis"); "šį vakarą aš pasakiau jam tą patį... jam vilkėjau gražiau". In ch. 28 he sits on the wall by the carved **Vytis** over the Trakai inner gate (new, carved winter 1410–11) and says he came to see if the fire came back. He does not appear in ch. 21, 23, 24, 26, 29.
**Plauen (ch. 22).** Counts, fears honestly ("Bijoti leidžiama. Tik tegu bijodami ima dirbti"); gives the heralds an invented round number and props white cloaks on stakes and on the sick; the Order's cellars, which were furnace-warm for a century, are **cold** after the Iron Saint's break; the old kamerarijus **Vilhelmas** (60 years a brother) weeps and pities for the first time; a drop of green sap fell from the Samogitian oak beam in the Grand Master's hall onto Ulrich's empty chair on 17 Jul; Ulrich's grave in St Anne's chapel is cold. Plauen's prayer: "Duok man pakankamai baimės, kad skaičiuočiau teisingai." Not yet Grand Master in ch. 22 (elected 9 Nov).
**Eglė (ch. 23, 29).** The grove over the graves is the Root-Mother's last act: the dead under it neither hunger nor rise ("Kaip motina laiko vaiką, kuris per ilgai buvo vienas"). **Galminas is the central oak** (spiral bark like his tattoos, a hand-branch with fingers), conscious, "Aš nebemirštu"; he knows the Samogitian land will not stay "visam laikui". A young Order brother (about 28, unnamed) came to pray over the unmarked graves; his axe would not cut, "kažkas mane laiko už rankos"; Eglė lets him pray; Galminas: "Palik jį." Eglė's cost: she tore her foot loose from the grove and left a hair-fine piece of herself; she can no longer feel her **left heel**; her žaltys's cheeks turned nearly white. She forgets names (a pine in the Šventaragis valley, the smell of her room there, her mother) and names a **lakštutė** (a lark) only because the grove gave her the word. On Rasos she is translucent; **Pilkoji** keeps her lost names (she "moka nešti vardus"), though she never knew Eglė's mother. She still wears Vytautas's buckle from Book I ch. 16 under the žaltys.
**The Root-Mother's thanks (ch. 29).** "Aš pavalgiau": the harvest of Grunwald (Germans, Poles, Lithuanians alike); thanks to Vytautas for bringing the wolves who carried her children to her bogs; "Tu pardavei mane. Du kartus. Ir tu atėjai. Abu tiesa." She has nothing left: **kirviai ateis su kunigais** ("ne šiemet; gal ne už trejų metų"), she will sleep, not die. She asks him not to ask more of Eglė. At midnight a single dry lightning from the west over Samogitia ("Perkūnas pasakė savo žodį ir nutilo").
**Samogitians (ch. 26).** Mingaila (eldest of 23 elders; son lost at Grunwald; the wolves brought him the ashes in a wax-stopped pot, which he keeps in his loft and feeds with a slice of bread every Thursday). His eight-year-old grandson is unnamed; the priest who came at Kūčios told him that "kaukų nėra". The Order's vaitas left at St George's with his wagons; elders count: 23. Vytautas tells them straight: "Ne visam laikui... Aš tik sakau, kiek turiu"; baptism will come after him ("Kunigai ateis... Aš galiu tik pasirinkti, kurie. Ir kaip. Ir kada"). The elders will light **Rasos fires openly for the last time** (Vytautas will be in the Dykra). Mingaila's order: "Mokykite vaikus vardų... Virtuvėse. Prie ugnies." The oaks are cold under his hand; they hear axes being sharpened in Vilnius.
**Trakai (ch. 28).** The island castle is finished (red brick, tiled roofs, the Duke's tower). **The lantern returns** after eleven months; Elijas ("tuščios durys... tokios sunkios"); the flame sits down on the old black ring like a bird on a nest; Žiežirba (about 20; throat-mark; sleeps on the stair) feeds it Kalvis's ash in pinches; Vytautas repeats "Kol aš gyvas, paskutinė ugnis neužges"; Žiežirba asks "O po to?" and Vytautas answers "Po to būsi tu." **Žiežirba's true name is still forgotten** (ch. 15, 9). The flame left the vault once; the public moment was ch. 17 only.
**Oleśnicki (ch. 25, 30; ch. 13, 18, 20).** Named **Zbignevas iš Olesnicos** (formal: Zbignevas Olesnickis); 22 in 1411; large nose, ink-black fingers, wax tablet and stylus (no pencil). He copies the Thorn text, murmurs *kol gyvi* at the Samogitia line, and in 1411 lays Thorn beside the Radom act and sees that both give **a lifetime, not land** ("Todėl ir reikia rašyti"). The **warm gold coin** came from the German knight's slashed purse at Grunwald; he keeps it on a wooden tablet, never carries or spends it; it is always warm. He boils the chancery ink himself. The old clerk **Stanislovas** is invented. Jogaila hears Oleśnicki **perfectly** through the Silence (a hint, not an explanation). Last line: *"Kalavijas savo darbą atliko. Dabar rašalas turi atlikti mūsų."*

## Objects and magic ledger (Part 3)
- Pebble: **warm** from ch. 17; ch. 21/25 show the cost (it returns the dead's faces). Henė's stones: **two**. Golden chain: on Henė (not shown). Silver wolf: on Vytautas (Jogaila at last holds his gaze on it at Grunwald and in Vilnius). Kęstutis's sword: bright line; carried at Grunwald and to Trakai.
- Unbinding 3. Tiers: 4 → **0** (žynys dead). The Hidden Groves: Galminas's grove (Prussia), the Kražiai grove (cold), the Dykra (Pilkoji, Pušis).
- White Silence: banners frozen in Wawel; Wawel's silence gives way near Vytautas (the Šventaragis plot at Vilnius), returns at the Bug bridge.
- Costs on the page: Vytautas (dead ear, memory flooding); the žynys (teeth, life); Eglė (heel, names, lakštutė); the Queen's statue (ch. 14, not repeated); Žiežirba (ugly slip of his name, ch. 15).

## Deviations from the outline (and why)
- **Ch. 21:** Ona's letter is read aloud by Žiežirba. The beheaded are named with offices (komtur of Brandenburg, vogt of Sambia: Wikipedia/Długosz tradition; "verify the occasion" respected: no occasion is stated beyond Vytautas's choice). The wolves "carry their dead to the bog" (seven + Rūdis) per ch. 19–20.
- **Ch. 22:** nothing is said about the Livonian relief or Hungarian threat; Vytautas's reasons for leaving are only rumours.
- **Ch. 23:** the grove is placed beside Lubienio lake and its alder bog (ch. 19), not at the field's centre; the dead are the field's trench graves. Eglė does not know Thorn's terms yet and Galminas says "ne visam laikui" without foreknowledge.
- **Ch. 24:** the Krėva talk is placed at Vilnius in early winter 1410–11 (an **invented private meeting**: the record gives no Vilnius visit; verify); the prisoner-day argument (Poles say the Lithuanians "pasitraukė") keeps the retreat disputed.
- **Ch. 25:** Vytautas is shown at Thorn (verify his presence); the sum "100,000 kopas" appears once and is flagged by his inability to imagine it.
- **Ch. 26:** the Thorn conversion clause is not asserted (outline: verify); the elders infer baptism from the Christian Grand Duke.
- **Ch. 27:** the queen is named **Anna**, never "Ona"; Jadwiga's daughter is a second Jadvyga ("Jadvygėlė").
- **Ch. 29:** Eglė's loss of memory (names) continues Book III ch. 16's loss of the birds.
- **Ch. 30:** Oleśnicki's "royal secretary" office is used loosely (verify); his saving of Jogaila follows ch. 20.
- Word counts are at the lower end of the brief (about 1,500–2,100).

## Canon questions
- Whether Vytautas truly attended the Thorn signing; whether Jogaila visited Vilnius in winter 1410–11 (the saga invents it).
- The **Krėva secret** is now defined: not a deed but a *pause* after a question put by Henė's voice through Skirgaila's mouth. Book IV/V should not "confess" it in plain words before Jogaila's death (1434) unless as a scene where sound dies again.
- Žiežirba's age (born c. 1390–91) is "about twenty" in 1410–11 (Part 1 notes: 14–15 in Dec 1405).
- Ona's cough hints at the 1418 death without naming it.

## Hands on to Book IV
Samogitia Lithuanian **for two lifetimes**; the Hidden Groves cold and listening; Galminas a grove and the Root-Mother asleep; Eglė translucent, losing names, her žaltys nearly white; the flame back in the vault, Žiežirba its keeper (heir to the oath); the pebble warm; Vytautas's left ear dead; Henė at Vytautas's court with two stones; Jogaila hearing only a child and Oleśnicki clearly; Oleśnicki, 22, with a warm coin and the maps, certain "ink" will do the rest; Plauen as the Order's survivor (Grand Master from 9 Nov, deposed 1413); Ona coughing at Trakai. The wolves: Pušis, Kirmėlė, Pilkoji and Žiežirba alive; Pilkoji keeps names.

## Seam pass (continuity edit, whole book)
Decisions made when reading ch. 1-30 in order. Edits are minimal line adjustments (books/3/[0-9]*.qmd only).

- **Henė's stones:** ch. 3 (Jan 1401) showed three; now "du juodus akmenis". Two in every Book III scene (ch. 5, 8, 13, 17, 25, 27, 28); the "three" appear only as memory (ch. 13, 28).
- **Vytautas's left ear:** 1399-1409 it is nearly deaf (rings; only shouts get through: ch. 2 now says so; ch. 6, 14 match); ch. 10 "kurčią" -> "vos girdinčią"; it goes fully dead at the ch. 20 lightning. Ch. 25: Henė's line at Thorn now comes from the right (it contradicted the dead left ear). Ch. 24: Vytautas now positions Jogaila on his right.
- **Krėva confessions (escalation):** ch. 3 tomb (sound dies after "Krėvoje, kai"); ch. 6 "ar tu mane nekenti?" (no confession); ch. 13 Brest ("Aš..." / "Ne"; one line added recalling the tomb); first Wawel Franciscan attempt after Brest (ch. 27 says so); ch. 24 Vilnius (names Brest twice; new line: Vytautas does not repeat "man tai nebesvarbu", which makes "Kol kas" a softening); ch. 27 second Franciscan attempt + the dream reveal (Henė's voice). Ch. 27 now opens "Beveik vienintelis balsas" (Henė in ch. 13/27 and Oleśnicki in ch. 30 are the exceptions).
- **Oleśnicki:** "Zbignevas iš Olesnicos" in ch. 13, 24, 30 (formal Olesnickis only in notes); Part 1 never names him. No change.
- **Pebble:** cold (ch. 2, 6, 8) -> one tremor ch. 8 -> brief warmth at the naming ch. 9 -> cold but the Queen speaks through it ch. 14 ("nebe miręs") -> warm at the oath ch. 17 (light through the pouch ch. 18) -> burns ch. 20 -> warm ch. 21, 25, 28. No contradictions found.
- **Žiežirba:** 8 (Sept 1399, Book II) -> 9 (spring 1400, ch. 2) -> 14, almost 15 at Kūčios 1405 (ch. 9; born c. Oct 1390-Mar 1391) -> "about twenty" 1410-11 (ch. 17, 28). Name unknown to him; ch. 15 cost = mother's lips. Ch. 28 now says he cannot sleep under the vault roof but can keep watch inside (ch. 9's cost vs. his working inside in ch. 15, 28).
- **Wolves:** 23 in, 16 back, 7 in the bog, Rūdis the eighth (ch. 19-21). Ch. 18 "kelios dešimtys" -> "per dvidešimt". Ch. 15: "ir Kalvis" (dead since 1399) -> "ir Pušis".
- **Flame:** left c. 8 Jun 1410 (ch. 15, 22 days); opened before strangers only at ch. 17 (ch. 17 "pirmą kartą prie svetimų akių"); back after eleven months (ch. 28). The žynys's flame leaned east "23 years" (ch. 17); ch. 21 said "dvylika" (that is the ch. 1 figure for 1400): fixed to "dvidešimt trejus".
- **Galminas sap:** light green (ch. 1) -> black after the ch. 4 offering -> ch. 12 bridge sap now "tamsi, žalsvai juoda" (was "šviesi, žalsva") -> bark/root-giant ch. 16, 19 -> grove ch. 23 beside Lubienio lake.
