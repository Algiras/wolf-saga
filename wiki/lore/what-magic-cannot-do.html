What Magic Cannot Do¶

Lore of the Craft: limits, taboos and fell workings

“The sea keeps the dead. She does not return them. If she did, the shore would be crowded.”

[The saga’s invention] A Palanga amber-keeper’s reply to a mourner.

Five things it cannot do¶

The saga sets a hard ceiling: no mortal working ever exceeds a castle for a night. These five limits are firmer still (Magic rules, §10; Cosmology, §6).

Taboos and fell workings¶

Some workings are possible and forbidden. Each carries a debt that “finds its owner”.

  • Glamours and illusions. The Law of Truth forbids making anyone perceive what is not there. The Krėva fog is real fog. The Shadow-Seller can conjure illusions; no mortal caster can. His bargains still bind him to their literal wording.

  • The forbidden transfer. Moving a price into another body that did not choose it. The Lagoon witch names it in Book II ch. 9; Vytautas refuses.

  • The Five-Voice, repeatedly. Not forbidden, but each use leaves the caster “a little less present”. By Book IV an old Vytautas hears the world as through water.

  • Selling a name or shadow. Bought power is bound only by the letter of the deal, and the thread compounds.

  • The kept aitvaras. A bought spirit that brings grain and silver; the house burns if the feeding stops, and the soul is forfeit at death.

  • Leaving the flame to die while the promise stands: madness, then death.

Why history cannot be bent¶

The rule is: history is the record plus mythic gaps.

Fixed

Free

Real dates, real office-holders, real outcomes of battles, treaties, deaths and baptisms

Private conversations, motives, unrecorded minor characters

Kniprode dies on 24 June 1382; Jogaila is baptised in Kraków on 15 February 1386

How something happened where the chronicles are silent or contradictory

Kęstutis dies at Krėva in August 1382; Vytautas dies in 1430 uncrowned

Anything supernatural

Magic may explain a gap: how Kęstutis died silently, how Vytautas passed the guards. It may not contradict a fact. Even the Forces, which can nudge weather, do not cancel a battle’s outcome. The victory at Grunwald in 1410 “changes nothing for the gods”, and Vytautas dies without the crown in 1430 because that is the record.

For the sources, disputed accounts and declared alterations, see History and the Saga. The historical notes also declare a few adaptations of transmitted circumstances. An adaptation is distinct from an undocumented private conversation: it changes the novel’s version, rather than establishing a recovered historical fact. Newly found discrepancies are listed separately; they do not become deliberate alterations merely because magic exists elsewhere.

Where the chronicles disagree, the saga sometimes combines them through an explicitly fictional explanation. The Order chronicler Wigand of Marburg says Birutė was drowned at Brest in 1382; the Polish-Lithuanian delegates at Constance in 1416 defended a long life and natural death. Their testimony does not by itself date her death to 1416. In Book I ch. 11 both are true: Jogaila’s men tried to drown her and the sea refused her, and she “died” a second time by choice in 1384 (ch. 27).

The writer’s corollary¶

Every magical rescue must leave the political problem unsolved. If the working is large enough to alter the record, it is the wrong kind of working: it belongs to a Force, and Forces always want something. See The Price and A reader’s guide.
