She saw the Ship. A black, shapeless hulk that sailed not on water but above the very earth of Lithuania, tearing up meadows and forests. Its oars were not of wood but of cold steel — thousands of swords, rhythmically cutting into the land and leaving deep, bleeding wounds in the body of the country. The Ship moved slowly but surely toward the heights of Vilnius.

— Praurimė, have mercy... — she wanted to cry out, but her throat was full of salty seawater. She was drowning in her own vision.

And then, through the noise, a Voice came. It was not the voice of a man or a god. It was the vibration of the Amber Queen, coming straight from the stone in her hand.

*"Close your eyes, Birutė. This vision is not for your pain but for your strength. What is coming no longer belongs to your hands. It belongs to your son, whom you have made a Wolf. Let the old world sink, so that the new one may sail out."*

Birutė felt that behind the Voice lay something more — not one voice but a chorus of the sea, singing through the ages of everything that gods and men had shattered. She understood what was being asked of her. The Queen was suffering without hands; she needed a woman who would walk on dry land. Birutė answered neither yes nor no. She said only one thing:

— If someone must pay — let it be me, and not him.
