# Book II, Part 1 (ch. 1–10): notes for other parts

## Dates used

- Ch. 1–2: spring 1387, the same night (Skirgaila's feast at the Peninsula Castle; Vytautas on the island).
- Ch. 3: winter 1387–88, Vilnius. Ch. 4: 1 Jul 1388 (Brest charter), winter 1389 (Grodno; Grodno charter handed out).
- Ch. 5: autumn 1389 (wedding-wagon plot), Nov–Dec 1389 flight. Ch. 6: Lyck 19 Jan 1390; Rastenburg to early Aug 1390.
- Ch. 7: late Aug–Sep 1390 (Zöllner's death reaches the camp mid-siege; order of events vs. Aug 20 left loose). Ch. 8: Dec 1390–9 Jan 1391.
- Ch. 9: Rasos (Midsummer) night 1392 (forts burned); Ona arrives 9 days later; the sons die c. early July 1392. Ch. 10: 4–5 Aug 1392, Astravas.

## Names and people introduced

- Franciscan POV: **brolis Anzelmas** (German father, Lithuanian mother, speaks Lithuanian without a Polish accent); he baptised the boy **Matas** individually (Book I ch. 29). Bishop is "vyskupas Andrius" (Wasilko). Polish clerk **Mikalojus** counts shirts (frozen ink). **Stanislovas** is Skirgaila's Kraków clerk. **Ponia Vaisilienė**: ex-vaidilutė (tier 3, Fire), now Voiceless: the tier-3 loss for the ledger.
- Vytautas's sons: **Jurgis** (b. c. 1378) and **Ivanas** (b. c. 1380). Names come from the Grunau tradition (Iwan and Georg) and coincide with two real kinsmen (Jonas Alšėniškis, Jurgis Narimantaitis) on the 1390-92 hostage list; see `fact_check.md`.
- Grodno/Brest: physician **Šmuelis** of Brest (Jewish, decent, no Force link); miller **Vaitiekus** (hangs himself after selling the plan); **Pinčiukas** (green kaftan, ledger, smells of wet mill-stone; "a small office"). Moscow: **bojaras Fiodoras**, **Jevdokija** (Dmitry Donskoy's widow), **metropolitas Kiprijonas**.
- Plot of 1389: bride **Daumantė** (17), her father **Rimgaudas** (dies), Kęstutis's old captain **Butvilas**. Order: **Markvardas fon Zalcbachas**, **brolis Dietrichas** (limp from a Reise; kind), the Marshal (unnamed). English: Bolingbrokas and his squire **Edmundas**.

## Objects and states

- **Pebble:** warm (ch. 2, 5); dim but not dead in the consecrated stone at Lyck (ch. 6, he swore "honestly": to keep faith as long as the Order does); **hot** at the refusal in ch. 9 (his palm blistered). At the end of ch. 10 it is not mentioned (leave warm/glowing; Part 2 ch. 20 turns it cold).
- **Kęstutis's sword** hangs above the high seat at Trakai (Peninsula Castle); rust spreads; Skirgaila forbids cleaning it ("Tegul visi mato"). At Ostrów Skirgaila says "Raktai bus tavo… kai apsižiūrėsiu, ką pasiimti" (sets up ch. 11: keeps the sword).
- **Silver wolf** worn openly; Jogaila flinches. Vytautas dropped the burned Order mail (black cross) into the Neman after ch. 9 and wore plain mail, then a brown gold-embroidered Ruthenian robe at Ostrów (Algirdas's style).
- **Henė's stones:** three in ch. 1, 5, 10; the third always wobbles. At Ostrów he kneels to Vytautas holding all three, the wobbling one still Skirgaila's ("Aš jį laikau, kol ateis laikas").
- **Skirgaila's breath** fogs like winter since the Krėva cell (still in Aug 1392). Henė's never shows.
- The forbidden lament ("Oi tu girių senas šerne… sava ranka užsmaugė"): a blind kanklės player (unnamed) sings it in the yard and across the lake.
- Birutė's west tower at Trakai is locked, untouched.
- Sofija's amber bead: from Birutė at age 9; cold in the Kremlin chapel (consecrated stone), warm by the Moskva where she hears surf and Birutė's lullaby. She carries Ona's herb pouch (henbane, wormwood, St John's wort) and a note ("if someone suddenly falls ill, ask what he ate").

## The Last Flame

- The flame was moved from the lodge to the **south corner of the island foundations** (half-vault, fieldstone hearth roofed with planks and hides), fed with Kęstutis's rotten scaffolding oak. Wolves cannot stay under the lit roof, so they tend it in turns. Spring flooding doubles Fire's cost: Vytautas's "Ugnis tau degs" gave him soul-chill (left hand numb ~3 days, empathy gone while he judged Kalvis).
- Kalvis carried the pot (burnt right forearm, no hair on it), judged and spared; he guards the flame. Šešiapirštis: "I follow the flame, not you"; two old Kęstutis wolves leave with him. Survivors of the 1382 slaughter: Jonas (dead) and **Kirmėlė**, who shudders at the verdict (hint for ch. 11). Kirmėlė and a young wolf (white throat-mark) row the boat.
- Vytautas goes "hunting" three nights each season to Trakai; Ona guesses a fire (hot stone in his bed) and does not ask.
- Ash-bearers walk to Trakai for live ash; one gave a pinch to Anzelmas (bread for bread) which held a tanner's hearth against the Quenched.

## Wolves and Gediminid blood

- Wolves stand off at the 1389 plot, the 1390 siege and the border of Prussia (rule 2; also "we follow the flame, not you"). First estrangement at the border (ch. 5).
- **Šešiapirštis dies** (ch. 9): left Vytautas his wolf-tooth on a thong; said the wolves will choose a new Vilkų Tėvas in the bog (the seat is vacant). He had Rūdis not on stage.

## The sons (ch. 9)

- **Fact-check:** Ona was released by the Order in May 1392 (record: freed to go to Lithuania) and rode in from the Grodno side 9 days after Rasos; the laundress escape is gone. **Žygimantas stayed** as hostage. The boys had been moved to **Balga**, tutor Dietrichas with them.
- Poison: henbane, wormwood and "German sugar", slow, via the kitchen; the cook vanished the morning after the Neman news. Physician said "fever"; Henrikas reports "karštinė… kai kurie sako, kad ne": the doubt is voiced by others, not by Ona.
- A Sambian ragana (milk-white eye, hut of nets on the Pilkasis kyšulys) offered to draw the poison for the blood of an Order child (transfer of the wound; the child dies). Šešiapirštis carried it; Vytautas said "Ne." Ona said nothing, then looked away for the first time. **Eglė** called the Neman foam at dusk: red; the boys died that morning. Dietrichas gave Šešiapirštis hair locks and a small wooden cross for Vytautas (not delivered on page; Vytautas has them).
- Ona has not looked at Vytautas since; at Ostrów she wears black and looks at everyone else. (Part 2 ch. 20: her first words to him since.)

## History handled

- Skirgaila's men captured Andrius of Polotsk (spring 1387); sent to Kraków. Skirgaila holds Vilnius's Upper Castle in 1390.
- 1390: Tautvilas (Order white mantle, baptised Konrad) falls to a crossbow bolt at the burning gate of the Crooked Castle (shooter unknown; record: crossbow, Wigand); Karigaila (red cloak, axe) dies when the arch falls (16 Sep); his head is carried round the Lower Castle on a pole (record). Nothing from the sky ("blogiau, kai nėra"). Never rebuilt. Bolingbroke thinks of Richard II (Appellants of 1387).
- Brest charter (1 Jul 1388) and Grodno charter (1389) shown as policy plus a real decency ("kas pakels ranką prieš juos…").
- Sofija ⚭ Vasily I, 9 Jan 1391, Kremlin cathedral (not named); Vasily 19, held in the Horde four years, sleeps with a knife.
- Ostrów: Henrikas (Płock bishop-elect, called "Mazovijos Henrikas") mediates; terms stated only as in the outline; Jogaila carries Jadwiga's note ("Pasirašyk, Vladislovai, kad nebūtų daugiau kraujo"). Jogaila asks "Kur tavo motina?" Vytautas: "Prie jūros… gyva." Wax was warm this time (cold at the 1385 Krėva signing).

## Deviations and flags

- **Boys' timeline:** I have Vytautas bring them home from Marienburg c. autumn 1388 (Zöllner's "good will"), so in 1389–90 he leads them back as hostages (Book I ch. 28 had them left at Marienburg).
- **Žygimantas:** handed over as a hostage at Lyck in Jan 1390, at 25 (record, VLE: c. 1389/90-1398, in the Order's hands from Vytautas's second defection until after Salynas). Historical-fact pass: ch. 6 no longer says he had already spent six years with the Order (now "two years in strange hands"); ch. 19 now says eight years.
- **Eglė** appears in Vilnius (ch. 3) and at the Neman (ch. 9) as the Root-Mother's "gap filler" (persona), not only in the Dykra.
- **Kalvis** guards the flame (per D11), not the ember-carrier.
- The outline's ch. 3 friar is named; the ragana's Order-child price is stated, not enacted. Ch. 6 hostage list (fact-check, replaces earlier): Ona, Sofija, Žygimantas, Rymgailė, Jonas Alšėniškis and other nobles; the boys are not written down ("escort"). Danutė no longer appears.
- Zöllner d. 20 Aug 1390 at Christburg; siege 11 Sep – 7 Oct 1390 (checked).

## Canon questions

- Names of the two sons (Jurgis, Ivanas) are my choice from the persona variants.
- The new Vilkų Tėvas (bog-rite) is unresolved; Part 2 may stage it or leave Kalvis as acting leader.
- Whether Part 2 ch. 11 should show Skirgaila's rusted sword as the thing he "keeps" (set up in ch. 1 and 10).
