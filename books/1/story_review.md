# Book I "Vilko Tremtis": Story, Pacing, History & Magic Review

*Review date: 2026-10-10. Scope: all of `books/1/*.qmd` (prologue, ch. 1–30, appendices 31–32, orphan `00_pradzia.qmd`) checked against `world_rules/`, `persona/`, `books/series_outline.md`, `books/1/*outline*.md`, `books/1/senoves_kalba_magic_system.md`, and the outlines for Books 2–5.*

---

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

The spine works: trust, then betrayal, then a death in prison, an escape in disguise, a deal with the enemy, a second betrayal, an uneasy truce, a baptism. Birutė's arc (seer, then widow, then amber statue) is the strongest thread, and the amber pebble works well as a moral barometer (dead after the Marienburg baptism, warm again when Vytautas turns on the Order). Four things hold the book back. **(1) Length and pacing:** 28,000 words across 30 chapters averages about 750 words a chapter, which is novella length. The big set pieces (battle, siege, truce) get a single page each, while internal monologue gets repeated. **(2) Two magic systems that never meet:** the five-element *Senovės Kalba* and the "Meddling Forces" pantheon run side by side with no rules connecting them. **(3) A handful of loud historical errors** (Kniprode was already dead, Polish gold arrives three years early, Vytautas becomes Marshal of the Order, Jogaila is baptised in Vilnius in 1387). **(4) Continuity drift** in names, objects and identities (two Eglės, a golden chain that was never given, an amulet that survives after "the last noble items" were given up, Henne serving two masters).

## 1. Pacing & structure

### 1.1 Word budget by part

| Part | Chapters | Words | Story time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue + I "Nuosmukis" | P, 1–10 | ~10.3k | ~2 weeks (autumn 1382) | Slow and atmospheric. Ch. 5 and 6 repeat Jogaila's "am I a traitor?" beat. Ch. 7–8 are vignettes under 500 words. |
| II "Pabėgimas" | 11–19 | ~6.8k | ~2 months (late 1382) | Best momentum in the book. The escape, swamp, border and Marienburg sequence flows. Ch. 18→19 rushes the negotiation and the baptism into what feels like a single day. |
| III "Karas ir Tvarka" | 20–30 | ~8.5k | **~5 years (1383–1387+)** | Badly compressed. Ch. 23 says 1383 and ch. 29 says 1387, but nothing between them (the 1384 reconciliation, Krėva Union 1385, Jogaila's Kraków coronation 1386) appears on the page. Time jumps carry no signposts. |

**Recommendations**

- Pick a target of **90–120k words** (about 3–4k per chapter) if this is meant to read as an epic. Grow scenes, not adjectives.
- **Merge or differentiate ch. 5 and 6.** Both stage Jogaila's doubt plus a childhood flashback. Keep the flashback in one chapter and give the other Skirgaila's POV.
- **Write the interrogation.** The outline's ch. 8 ("Kęstutis refuses to abdicate") is missing, so the murder has no stated in-scene motive. One scene where Skirgaila demands abdication and Kęstutis refuses would fix it.
- **Add date or season headers** to Part III chapters, or one bridging chapter covering 1384–1386 (Vytautas's reconciliation and the Krėva Union).
- **Ch. 25 → 26:** Vytautas is a hunted fugitive with a dying band, then in the next chapter he is negotiating as an equal. Show the arrow message being answered, or show Jogaila's envoy finding him.

### 1.2 Repetition and copy-paste artefacts (fix these first)

- **Ch. 20 reuses Ch. 5 verbatim.** "Staiga, kaip visada, kai jo siela buvo susiskaldžiusi…", the childhood run ("bėgančią… Lėčiau, lėčiau, jūs abu esate man sūnūs!"), "Ar aš parduodu savo kraują už tuščią karūną?", and "plyšys, iš kurio pradeda tekėti šalta, juoda kraujo upė" all appear word for word. Ch. 29 uses the same template a third time.
- Ch. 1 has a duplicated sentence: "Medis nusitrynė nuo laiko… Medis per dešimtmečius nusitrynė…"
- Ch. 3 has two consecutive near-identical dialogue tags ("o jos balsas nuskambėjo lyg tūkstančiai pamirštų giesmių").
- Ch. 15 and 16 both stage Eglė silently pointing the path, as if the same scene was written twice.
- Ch. 30 repeats a paragraph ("Tai buvo ne tiesiog rytas. Tai buvo visų amžių sutrūkimas…").
- Ch. 1 has a garbled markdown artefact: `**iš rytų** — **iš Bizantijos**…`

### 1.3 Prose (noted, not the focus)

Adjective stacking ("mirtinai", "neįtikėtinai", "negailestingai" sometimes three to a sentence) flattens the intensity. Grammar slips are frequent: "Neik į jų irklas!" (ch. 2), "Vyras, kuris laivą valdo" for a guard captain (prologue), "Tai tikra žodis", "klykojo galva" (ch. 6), and gender agreement errors ("bėgančią" for Jogaila, "Vyties"). A human Lithuanian line edit is needed after the structural fixes.

## 3. History check (1377–1387)

Fantasy may bend history. The project's own conceit (`mythic_gaps.md`), though, is that **magic explains history's gaps without contradicting its record**. These items contradict the record:

| # | Text | Actual record | Severity / fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Ch. 18–19, 25: Grand Master **Winrich von Kniprode** receives Vytautas | Kniprode died **24 June 1382**, before Kęstutis's death (15 Aug 1382). Vytautas's host was **Konrad Zöllner von Rotenstein** (GM 1382–1390). | **High.** Swap the name; the characterisation (old, devout, a chess player) can stay. |
| H2 | Ch. 5, 7, 9: **Polish gold**, a Polish monk, a strangling chain "ordered from Kraków" | In 1382 Poland was ruled by Louis of Hungary (died Sept 1382). Polish courting of Jogaila began in **1384–85**. Jogaila's 1380–82 partner was the **Teutonic Order** (the Dovydiškės treaty). | **High:** it misplaces Jogaila's whole motive. Make the money and the chain **Order** silver, or Riga merchant silver via **Hanul**, the Vilnius merchant who historically opened the gates. Polish interest can arrive in Part III. |
| H3 | Ch. 1–6: Kęstutis is still in power and is lured to Vilnius | **1381:** Kęstutis learned of Dovydiškės, seized Vilnius and deposed Jogaila. **1382:** Jogaila retook Vilnius while Kęstutis was in Samogitia. Kęstutis and Vytautas came to parley at Jogaila's camp near Trakai and were seized there. | **Medium.** Skipping the 1381 coup makes Jogaila's betrayal look like pure ambition and turns Kęstutis from usurped usurper into a naive old man. At least have Vytautas mention "last year we took Vilnius from him and let him go". The mercy Kęstutis showed, *letting Jogaila live*, is the tragic irony the book is missing. |
| H4 | Ch. 10: Kęstutis is buried "like a dog, without rites" | Jogaila gave Kęstutis a **full pagan cremation** in Vilnius, with horses and hounds. | **Medium, and an opportunity:** the honoured funeral of a man he had murdered is a far more chilling Jogaila. |
| H5 | Ch. 19: Vytautas becomes the **Order's Marshal** | The Marshal was a professed monastic knight. A newly baptised foreign duke could not hold the office. | **High.** Make him a vassal or ally with knights "lent" to him. |
| H6 | Ch. 19: baptism at **Marienburg** in the same scene as the oath | Vytautas was baptised Catholic in **Tapiau (Tapiava), 21 Oct 1383**, as *Wigand*. The Treaty of Königsberg (Jan 1384) ceded Samogitia to the Nevėžis. | Low/medium. Marienburg is fine for drama; date it 1383 and name him Wigand. It is a delicious detail. |
| H7 | Ch. 22–24: Vytautas and the Order **besiege Vilnius with bombards** in 1383, and Vytautas burns their siege tower | The 1383 campaigns took **Trakai**. Vytautas defected in **July 1384**, burning the Order's castles he held (**Jurbarkas/Georgenburg, Marienwerder/Naujapilis**). The bombarded siege of Vilnius with Vytautas was **1390**, during his *second* alliance with the Order. | **High for series planning.** Book I uses up the 1390 siege, so Book II has nothing left for 1389–92. Either (a) recast ch. 22–24 as the 1384 burning of the Order's Neman castles, or (b) keep the fictional 1383 siege and drop the 1390 one in Book II. Option (a) is stronger and historically exact. |
| H8 | Ch. 28: Vytautas rebuilds **Trakai** and "splits Lithuania" with Jogaila in 1384 | In 1384 Vytautas got only Grodno, Brest and Podlasie. **Trakai went to Skirgaila**, which is Vytautas's core grievance until 1392 (Ostrów). | **High.** This grievance is the engine for Book II. Give Trakai to Skirgaila on the page. |
| H9 | Ch. 29: **Jogaila baptised in the Neris in 1387** alongside Vytautas | Jogaila was baptised in **Kraków on 15 Feb 1386** (as Władysław), married Jadwiga three days later, and was crowned on 4 Mar 1386. Vytautas was re-baptised there too (as *Aleksandras*). In **1387** in Vilnius, Jogaila (already king) presided over the mass baptism of ordinary Lithuanians and founded the cathedral and bishopric. | Medium. Keep the icy Neris scene, but have Jogaila presiding as king and Vytautas witnessing (he is already baptised twice). |
| H10 | Missing | The **Krėva Union (14 Aug 1385)** was signed *in the same castle where Kęstutis died*. | **Big missed thematic beat.** One scene: Jogaila signs away Lithuania's independence in the room above his uncle's death cell. |

## 4. Magic system and cosmology consistency

### 4.1 The core problem: two systems, no bridge

- **System A: *Senovės Kalba*** (`senoves_kalba_magic_system.md`, appendix ch. 31). Five elements with deities (Praurimė, Gabija, Bangpūtys, Žemyna, Vėjopatis), zeniths, incantations, boons, costs, three Laws and the *Senovės Sutartis*.
- **System B: the Meddling Forces** (`meddling_forces.md`, `hidden_immortals.md`). These are the Root-Mother, Perkūnas, the Shadow-Seller, the White Silence, the Amber Queen, the Iron Saint, the Steppe-Hunger and the Gold-Glutton, some walking as avatars.
- The text mixes them freely: "Eglė, Žemynos, Šaknų Motinos pasiuntinė" (ch. 16) treats Žemyna and the Root-Mother as the same being. Perkūnas acts directly three times (ch. 10, 19, 21) but has no element. The Amber Queen speaks *from* amber to Birutė (ch. 3) while other docs say Birutė *is* her.
- **Fix:** one cosmology with layers. A gods layer (old high gods), a Forces layer (what the gods become when they meddle), and a craft layer (*Senovės Kalba*, which is how mortals ask). See `world_rules/cosmology.md`.

### 4.3 What works and should become canon

- **The amber pebble as a fidelity barometer** (warm, then dead at the baptism, cold, then hot when he betrays the Order, then glowing at the 1387 baptism). Make it an explicit rule: *it answers to the bearer's truth, not his faith.*
- **The tarnaitė who stays behind** (ch. 13) as a secret Root-Mother devotee. A real historical echo (the maid Elena was executed) given mythic weight.
- **Birutė's ring returned three times by the sea** (ch. 11). A perfect folk-rule beat.
- **The amber kneeling woman on Palanga shore** (ch. 27). A physical relic for Books II–V to return to.
- **Henė juggling three black stones** (the three cousins). Keep it consistent; the docs say "balls".
