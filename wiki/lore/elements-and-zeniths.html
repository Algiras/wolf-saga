The Five Elements and Their Zeniths

Lore of the Craft: Stars, Fire, Water, Earth and Wind

lore

“The sky keeps a better calendar than the Church. Ask the stars at Kūčios and they answer for half the price. Ask them at noon and they send you the bill twice.”

[Saga] From a copyist’s marginal note in a Vilnius Franciscan psalter.

Five elements, five patrons

Senovės Kalba draws on five elements, each with a patron deity, a spoken line and a zenith: the hour at which the element listens best. This page follows Magic rules, §3.3 and §4.1, and the calendar, §3.3. For each element’s own page see Stars, Fire, Water, Earth and Wind.

[Record] Gabija (hearth fire), Žemyna (earth) and Saulė (the sun) are well attested. Praurimė, Bangpūtys and Vėjopatis rest on late or doubtful sources, and no Baltic source describes a five-element craft. The zeniths are [Saga].

The zenith table

Zeniths are astronomical, because the elements watch the sky, not the Church calendar. In the 1380s the pagan solstice night falls about 13 December, eleven days before Christian Kūčios.

Element Patron Line Zenith At zenith Off zenith
Stars Praurimė Žvaigždės tau švytės Winter-solstice midnight (Kūčios) Cost halved Doubled in daylight; impossible under overcast
Fire Gabija Ugnis tau degs Summer-solstice noon (Rasos) Halved Doubled in rain or floods
Water Bangpūtys Vanduo tau teks Full moon or high tide; strongest at the spring ice-break Halved Doubled away from living water
Earth Žemyna Žemė tau laikys Autumn-equinox dusk (Dagotuvės) Halved Impossible at sea or in the air
Wind Vėjopatis Vėjas tau neš Storms; Vėlinės Halved Doubled in caves or still air

A calendar of the zeniths

Season Julian date, 1380s Festival Element that listens
Spring Equinox about 12 March; the first full moon after the ice goes out Equinox feast Water
Summer About 13 to 14 June, at noon Rasos Fire
Autumn About 14 to 15 September, at dusk Dagotuvės Earth
Autumn Late October to 2 November, and any great storm Vėlinės Wind
Winter About 13 December, at midnight Kūčios Stars

Canon gap, flagged: the older flavour notes tie Water to the spring equinox, while Magic rules tie it to the full moon and the ice-break. The calendar notes reconcile them by a recommended rule, the first full moon after the ice-break. This page follows that recommendation; it is not yet a settled canon entry.

Minor and major workings

A minor working is one candle-length. A major working is a night, an army or a life.

Element Minor price Major price
Stars (clear sight) Hours of blindness Blind for days, or one beloved face lost from memory for good
Fire (soul armour, strength) Exhaustion Soul-chill: empathy gone for days; the caster can kill without feeling
Water (healing, life spring) Raging thirst The caster takes part of the patient’s wound
Earth (unmovable will) Stiffness Petrification for the length of the effect; permanent loss of grace
Wind (shadow speed, far-speech) Vertigo Loss of voice, with only whistling for days, permanent with repeated use

The prices as the books pay them

Stars. Birutė’s vision in Book I ch. 3 falls at Dagotuvės, off zenith, so it costs double: blindness for “two days, maybe three”. In Book II ch. 27 Vytautas, with no surety, speaks the Five-Voice, and the Stars price is a face: Elena’s, who lay in his bed at Krėva, becomes “a smooth, worn emptiness”.

Fire. In Book II ch. 2 Vytautas holds his palm to the failing flame, and the soul-chill rises through wrist and elbow until looking at the kneeling smith “was like looking at a stone”. The spring damp doubled the price. In Book II ch. 11 a one-candle working over the ash-pot costs his legs, because rain and lake doubled it.

Water. At the ice-break in Book VI ch. 10 Birutė notes that the price is smaller. In Book II ch. 9 the Lagoon witch states the rule: whoever heals takes the wound into herself. See The Price.

Earth. In Book II ch. 19 the žynys Kantaudas speaks Žemė man laikys after Dagotuvės has passed: “the earth hears only half today; the price will be double”. He turns to stone against his oak.

Wind. The Krėva fog of Book I ch. 14 is cast at Vėlinės, “when the wind is strongest, and the price was half”: even so Birutė loses her voice. Her voice already comes out as a whistle in Book I ch. 11; after the fog “there was nothing left of that sound”.

Why timing matters to the plot

A zenith turns a hopeless working into a possible one. Book I ch. 14 and the Krėva escape date are set on the eve of Vėlinės for exactly this reason: Wind’s zenith halves the fog’s price. A writer who needs a working off zenith must show the doubled bill.
