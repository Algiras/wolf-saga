# Book VI fact-check (Šventasis Miškas, 1341-1377)

Pass date: 2026-10-12. Rule: D3, history is the fixed spine; where historians disagree the saga picks a real side and says so in `istorine_pastaba.qmd`; invention only fills gaps. Format: claim -> record (source) -> action. Binding: Jogaila b. c. 1362 (ten in 1372; the 1359 armour flashback is Skirgaila's) is not undone.

## Findings (written incrementally)

1. **Kęstutis's capture, ch16** -> captured **21 Mar 1361** (Baronas, via en.wikipedia) after he and Algirdas had taken the Order's Eckersberg and Johannisburg (Masuria); a small band of knights returning from another raid fell on the camp by **Lake Ublik (Wobel)**; he was over 60 and overcome by the third knight who attacked him. VLE says "near Įsrutė". Held at Marienburg about half a year. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%99stutis ; https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/kestutis/ ; https://www.journals.vu.lt/lietuvos-istorijos-studijos/article/view/37183 (Baronas 2003) -> **corrected in ch16**: dateline "1361 m. rugpjūtis" -> March (frost, thaw); Samogitian bogs and the Šešupė ford -> Sūduva and the Masurian lakes; the Order's staged trap with 600 riders -> a chance meeting with a small returning band (the record), with Hohenstein as the third knight.
2. **Capture date** -> Wigand gives 21 Mar 1361, Hermann von Wartberge 13 Mar; place Lake Ublik (Wobel) near Johannisburg (veidas.lt summary of Baronas/Janulaitis: Ekersberg's komtur and Mikalojus of Vindekaimis pursued him; en.wikipedia: a small band of knights returning from another raid hit the camp). Ch16 uses March, no date. http://www.veidas.lt/kestucio-nelaisve-ir-pabegimas-is-marienburgo
2b. **Escape from Marienburg, ch17-18** -> **11 Nov 1361** (veidas.lt after Janulaitis; Baronas c. 11 or 17-18 Nov; Wigand: November); Alf (Adolfas), a servant of Lithuanian origin, removed bricks from the cell wall over months and carried out the rubble; Kęstutis broke through the wall, crossed the empty terrace, climbed down into the dry moat, where Alf waited with horses and a Teutonic knight's cloak; he rode out through the outer gate as a knight, went toward Liebstadt, hid in a bog, freed the horses and went on foot over the Drwęca to Mazovia. Alf's fate is not recorded. Baronas (2004, Annaberger Annalen) doubts the Order wanted him kept and notes Wigand knew details only a participant could. Sources: http://www.veidas.lt/kestucio-nelaisve-ir-pabegimas-is-marienburgo ; https://lituanistika.lt/content/3445 ; https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/kestutis/ (Nov 1361, Alfas, through Mazovia) -> **corrected in ch18**: dateline "1 lapkričio" -> "11 lapkričio"; "78 nights" -> 235 (March to November); kitchen-servant disguise, bucket and the hanged Alf -> the wall dug out by Alf, the white knight's mantle, horses, outer gate, flight toward Mazovia; Alf's fate left open (the sergeant Jonas and the wolf Žvirblis are saga inventions). ch17: Kniprodė "waited nine years" -> ten (Grand Master since 1351), and the capture is now chance, not his plan.
3. **Who warned Kęstutis of Dovydiškės (1381)** -> en.wikipedia (Civil War 1381-84, after Ivinskis 1978): **Kuno von Liebenstein**, komtur of Osterode, in Aug 1381. VLE and Wigand give no informant in the accessible text. Real **Günther von Hohenstein** (c. 1310-1380) was komtur of Osterode from 1349 and later of Brandenburg and died in 1380, so he cannot have warned anyone in 1381 (https://archaeolog.ru/media/2024/Пресс-релизы/Пресс%20релиз%20Бранденбургские%20древности.pdf is a weak source; flagged). -> **corrected in Book I ch1** (Liebenstein); in Book VI, Günter fon Hohensteinas (22 in 1353) is kept as an **invented** young Order brother who shares a name with a real, older knight; the afterword now says so, and the "warns Kęstutis in 1381" thread is dropped from `detailed_outline.md`.
4. **Gediminas's death (ch1, outline)** -> died **1341**, probably December (en.wikipedia lead "December 1341", infobox c. 1341); VLE: "1340-41 began a baptism campaign, soon died"; the pyre is dated 1342 in en.wikipedia (weak source: Jones-Bley and Huld 1996); place and cause unknown ("presumably killed in a coup" rests on a travel guide). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gediminas ; https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/gediminas/ -> **confirmed**: dateline "1341-1342 m. zima" kept; the outline's "(verify)" replaced; afterword now says "greiciausiai gruodi". Jaune is a widow in ch1 and d. c. 1344 (some historians doubt she existed): consistent.
5. **Jaunutis deposed 1345 (ch3-4)** -> 1345 by Algirdas and Kestutis, month unrecorded; Kestutis "unexpectedly captured Vilnius"; Jaunutis was imprisoned, escaped, fled to Simeon of Moscow, was baptized Ioann, later reconciled and held Zaslavl, last mentioned 1366; en.wikipedia (Rowell 1994): the Order under Ludolf Konig was "ineffective", years "quite peaceful", and a big Order reise planned for 1345 may have triggered the coup https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaunutis -> **left**: Vėlinės 1345 is invention (record silent on the month); Kestutis letting him ride out toward Moscow (Smolensk road) matches the flight, but the saga lets him leave unharmed instead of imprisoned; ch2's yearly Order raids over 1342-44 sit uneasily with "quite peaceful" and were not re-verified.
6. **Martyrs of Vilnius (ch6)** -> Orthodox tradition: Anthony hanged 14 Apr 1347, John 24 Apr, Eustathius 13 Dec 1347, on one oak, under Algirdas (Maria of Vitebsk d. 1346) https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/0203/04/14/101076-martyrs-anthony-john-and-eustathius-of-vilnius ; https://pravoslavie.ru/102854.html -> **compressed, disclosed** (ch6 shows all three hanged in one spring scene; afterword now gives the three dates). Maria's death "last year" (1346) is right.
7. **Strėva, 2 Feb 1348 (ch7-8)** -> Candlemas; the Order's raid on Aukstaitija (from 24 Jan) was led by **Grand Marshal Siegfried von Dahnenfeld and Grand Commander Winrich von Kniprode**, not the Grand Master (Dusemer); the Order's chronicles say the knights forced the Lithuanians back over the frozen river with heavy losses (Wigand's figure 18,000 is not trusted), Lithuanian historiography holds the Lithuanians won; Lithuanian commander "probably Narimantas" (some say Kestutis); **Narimantas died** (Order chronicles, VLE), Matthias of Neuenburg names two sons of Gediminas dead, the second presumed Manvydas (a guess) https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/strevos-musis/ ; https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/narimantas/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manvydas -> **corrected in ch7**: the banner is now "the Grand Master's, or only the Grand Commander's and Marshal's"; Narimantas's death kept (record); **afterword** rewritten (no longer "two sons died"); Manvydas lives (he lights the pyre in ch8). The record's "Teutonic win" and the saga's heavy Lithuanian loss agree.
8. **Narimantas (Gleb) at Pinsk** -> Narimantas, prince of Pinsk, baptized Gleb: consistent with ch7-8.
9. **Ox-oath, 15 Aug 1351 (ch13)** -> Louis I offered peace on 15 Aug 1351; Kestutis agreed to be baptized and give military aid, confirmed by a pagan rite (a bull; Dubnica chronicle; Kerbelyte 2015 "Priesaika ar aukojimas?" reads it as a sacrifice, Zinkevicius thinks the words are garbled); he ran away "on the road to Buda" about five days later, after being placed in the care of Boleslaw III of Plock https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_Lithuania ; https://www.zurnalai.vu.lt/td/article/view/28996 -> **left, disclosed**: the saga has him slip out the first night in the camp (compression); afterword says so. Casimir III (widower of Aldona d. 1339), Louis b. 1326 (25), Casimir b. 1310 (41): confirmed.
10. **Kniprode (ch14, 17)** -> Grand Master 1351-24 Jun 1382; Grand Commander before 1351 (VLE) -> confirmed. The "Table of Honour" and the English and French guests of the 1350s: **not verified**, left as flavour (outline still says "verify for the period").
11. **Kaunas, 13 Mar - 17 Apr 1362 (ch19)** -> Order under Kniprode, siege towers, trebuchets, mines, burning resin; fell on Easter (17 Apr 1362 was Easter Sunday by my Julian computus; en.wikipedia says Holy Saturday); Vaidotas commanded and was taken with 36 men; Kestutis was present and tried to negotiate (en.wikipedia; VLE says both brothers were busy against the Tatars) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Kaunas_(1362) -> **corrected in ch19**: dateline now "13 March - 17 April"; "three weeks" -> five weeks (day 11 and day 32 of the mine kept proportionate; Kestutis's promise "per penkias savaites"). Kniprode's presence is attested (afterword rewritten: was "not confirmed"). Kestutis arrives too late, which is the saga's choice.
12. **Blue Waters (ch20)** -> autumn 1362 or 1363 debated; the three Tatar beys (Kutlug Bey, Haci Bey, Demetrius in the infobox), Syniukha river, Kyiv gained, Vladimir Algirdovich put in Kyiv; sources: Stryjkowski (1582, "weak"), the Tale of Podolia (1430s propaganda), Dlugosz silent https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Blue_Waters -> **kept 1362 and disclosed** (afterword already says "1362 ar 1363"); no text change. Algirdas "66" in 1362 (b. c. 1296) fine.
13. **Butautas, 1365 (ch21)** -> summer 1365 while Algirdas and Kestutis were in Volhynia helping Liubartas; Dirsunas uncovered the plot, Survila rescued him; he and 15 followers fled to the Order; baptized Henry at Konigsberg **25 Jul 1365** after the komtur of Insterburg; went to Prague, d. **7 May 1380**; Rowell doubts the plot (late German source) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butautas -> **corrected in ch21**: dateline "1365 m. zemos pabaiga" -> summer; snow, ice, frozen laundry -> summer; Kestutis now rides back from Volhynia; baptism "Prusijoje, ankstyva pavasari" -> Konigsberg, late July, Henry after the Insterburg komtur. The saga's quiet plot and the mercy are invention. Butautas's later death (1380) is not shown in Book VI.
14. **Vaidotas and Butautas, sons of an earlier wife or of Birute (ch2, ch4, ch16, ch21, afterword, outline)** -> VLE: first (unnamed) wife bore Patrikas, Vaidotas, Butautas; Birute's sons Vytautas, Zygimantas, Tautvilas; Tegowski (1999) calls Vaidotas "eldest son of Kestutis and Birute", but many of his conclusions are conjecture (en.wikipedia Vaidotas); Birute m. c. 1349/50 (VLE "1349 or a little earlier", en wiki says c. 1344) https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/kestutis/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaidotas -> **VLE side kept, disclosed** in the afterword (the saga's brothers are 13 and 9 in 1344, 31 and 30 in 1362 and 1365). Patrikas (in the record fighting beside Kestutis in 1361) is not in the book.
15. **Moscow 1368, Lubutsk 1372 (ch22, ch25-26)** -> 1368 (Nov), 1370, 1372 campaigns; white-stone Kremlin 1366-68; Mikhail of Tver, Uliana's brother, asked for help; Kestutis is listed among the commanders (en.wikipedia, Britannica) though no source details his part; Lubutsk: standoff on the Oka, the vanguard beaten, treaty in "summer 1372", Vladimir of Serpukhov wed Elena Algirdaite https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lyubutsk ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian%E2%80%93Muscovite_War_(1368%E2%80%931372) -> **confirmed**; Dmitry 18 (1368) and 22 (1372) and Algirdas 72/76 (b. c. 1296): ok.
16. **Franciscans of Vilnius (ch23)** -> 1369 tradition; Bychowiec: Algirdas away against Moscow, seven monks killed and seven tied to crosses and sent down the Neris; source disagreement on the number (one guardian and four brothers elsewhere) https://www.ldkistorija.lt/?p=10245 ; https://lituanistika.lt/content/14886 -> **dateline corrected**: "vėlyva zima" -> "Kalėdos" (the chapter opens on Christmas morning; 1347 + 22 years = 1369); the hangings and the number 14 are the saga's; disclosed in the afterword. Algirdas is not in Vilnius in the chapter, which fits Bychowiec.
17. **Rudau, 17 Feb 1370 (ch24)** -> 17 Feb (German) / 18 (Lithuanian); Kniprode and Marshal Henning Schindekop; Schindekop speared in pursuit and **died before reaching Konigsberg**; Algirdas and Kestutis both led; ~17,000 vs ~10,000 (infobox); the Order's claims of Lithuanian dead are exaggerated https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Rudau -> **confirmed**: the chapter's marshal "zuvo" is a field rumour; the afterword says mortally wounded.
18. **Algirdas's death May 1377 and the succession (ch28-29)** -> Algirdas named Jogaila heir before his death (VLE); en.wikipedia 24 May 1377 (not re-searched). Jogaila 15 per the binding c. 1362. -> **confirmed**.
19. **Ages (binding)** -> Jogaila b. c. 1362: 6 in 1368, 10 in 1372 (ch25, ch26), 13 in 1375, 15 in 1377; Vytautas c. 1350: 11 (1361), 15 (1365), 18 (1368), 22 (1372); Kestutis c. 1297 (VLE "about 1300"): 44 (1341), 48 (1345), c. 64 (1361, "over sixty"), nearly 80 (1377); Algirdas c. 1296 (66 in 1362); Birute c. 1334 (15 in 1349, 27 in 1361); Danute c. 1358 (3 in 1361) -> **all consistent**, nothing changed.
20. **Antiquarian checks, no change needed:** Gediminas invited Franciscans c. 1323; Louis b. 1326; Kniprode GM 1351; Dmitry Donskoy b. 1350; Uliana of Tver m. Algirdas 1349/50 (the chapters say January 1350); Dusemer GM 1345-51; the Order's Kaunas fortress.

## Canon-doc conflicts (not edited)

- **`world_rules/master_chronology.md` (and wiki/codex, relation-maps, pronunciation guide, faiths-clergy, houses-lineages):** names **Gunther von Hohenstein, komtur of Osterode** as the 1381 informant; the record (en.wikipedia after Ivinskis) is **Kuno von Liebenstein**, and Book VI treats Hohenstein as an invented young brother who is NOT the informant. `houses-lineages` already lists Liebenstein "verify".
- **master_chronology** "Gediminas dies (winter 1341/42 (verify))" -> December 1341, pyre 1342; the "(verify)" can go.
- **master_chronology** "1361 captured ... escapes with a servant (Alfas) (verify)": now settled (captured 21 Mar, escaped 11 Nov); the canon line "captured in a border fight / bait" in the old outline and `part2_notes.md` is superseded (notes edited).
- **master_chronology** "1384 ... Trakai goes to Skirgaila": the record gives Skirgaila Trakai in **1382** (VLE: 1382-92); Jogaila promised it to Vytautas in spring 1384.
- **master_chronology** "Autumn 1381 ... Kestutis seizes Vilnius ... releases him": the record says Jogaila was away and was caught on his way back.
- **houses_and_lineages §1.4 / canon_decisions "earlier unnamed wife"**: matches VLE; Tegowski/Wasilewski make Vaidotas Birute's son. Flag as a deliberate pick of one historiographical side.
- **houses_and_lineages** "Dlugosz has her drowned in 1382" (Birute): the drowning is Wigand's (Dlugosz had Wigand translated into Latin; whether he repeats it is not verified); en.wikipedia places it at Brest, VLE says Kestutis sent her there.
- **houses_and_lineages** "Manvydas d. 1348 (verify circumstances)": only a guess ties him to Strėva; Book VI keeps him alive in Feb 1348.
- **houses_and_lineages** Butautas "d. 1380" matches en.wikipedia (7 May 1380, Prague); "plotted against his father" should read "against Jogaila's... / against the grand-ducal throne while Kestutis was in Volhynia" (the plot was against Algirdas and Kestutis jointly, summer 1365).
- **Jogaila's birth**: VLE (the encyclopedia the saga cites on `sources.qmd`) still gives "about 1351(?)"; the binding c. 1362 follows Tegowski 1999; the afterword and `sources.qmd` now say both.
- **wiki/ Book VI pages, `books/6/detailed_outline.md` (edited), `part2_notes.md` (edited)** used the old August 1361/Sesupe ford and 1 Nov 1361 facts; the codex pages under `wiki/` (other agent's remit) may still repeat them.
