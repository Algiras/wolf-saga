Cosmology

Source: world_rules/cosmology.md

Canonical. Reconciles the five-element Senovės Kalba (books/1/senoves_kalba_magic_system.md) with the Meddling Forces (meddling_forces.md) and Hidden Immortals (hidden_immortals.md). Mechanical rules for mortal magic are in magic_rules.md.

In the saga’s world the folklore is literally true, but the folk are only ever half right about it. Peasants who leave milk for the žaltys are correct. Priests who say the žaltys is a demon are wrong, and so are the old women who say it is harmless. This is the governing tone: the Baltic myth taken at its word, then made grey.

1. The World Tree (Pasaulio Medis)

The world is an oak (sometimes sung as a linden or a silver birch). Each layer is real and reachable, though rarely by the living.

Layer Name Who dwells there How mortals reach it
Crown Dangus (the Sky), the Stiklo Kalnas (Glass Mountain) at its summit Dievas, Saulė, Mėnuo, Aušrinė and the star-children; Perkūnas rides between Only the dead climb the Glass Mountain, and only those with long nails (hence the custom of burying nail-clippings and lynx or bear claws with the dead)
Bridge Paukščių Takas (the Birds’ Way, the Milky Way) Souls (vėlės) migrating as birds; Vėjopatis’s winds Dying. Dreaming, rarely, for seers
Trunk Žemė (the Middle World) Mortals, beasts, the spirits of place (dvasios) Born here
Roots Anapilis / Požemis (the Under-Castle), Velnias’s realm The dead who will not climb, wealth, bargains, the past Swamps, wells, river-bottoms, graves, crossroads at midnight, and under the mask of the Shadow-Seller
The waters around the tree Marios (the Baltic) and every lake, which are its eyes Bangpūtys; the drowned palace of Jūratė; the Amber Queen Drowning, which is not always death (see Birutė)

Dausos, the afterlife meadow, lies past the Glass Mountain. Cremation is the correct funeral because fire is the fastest road up. A buried pagan climbs slowly or not at all and may linger as a vėlė that must be fed at Vėlinės. This is why Kęstutis’s honoured cremation matters (H4 in the review), and why the Order’s ban on burning the dead is, to pagans, an atrocity against the soul.

2. The Three Layers of Power

This is the bridge between the two magic documents.

   LAYER I — THE GODS (Dievai)            source, ancient, mostly indifferent
          │  when a god's attention is fed by human history, it hardens into…
          ▼
   LAYER II — THE FORCES (Galios)         hungry, partisan, meddling in events
          │  both layers can be *asked* — politely, at a price — through…
          ▼
   LAYER III — THE CRAFT (Senovės Kalba)  mortal words, mortal blood, mortal cost

2.1 Gods are indifferent; Forces are partisan

A god is a fact of nature: Perkūnas is thunder and Žemyna is soil. Gods do not care who rules Vilnius. A Force forms when a god’s attention is fed long enough by human worship, war, grief or greed that it develops appetite and agenda. The Root-Mother is what Žemyna becomes after a century of watching the forests cut. Perkūno Rūstybė is Perkūnas after a thousand sworn oaths. The Shadow-Seller is a splinter of Velnias that learned to enjoy the trade.

A god can be petitioned. A Force must be bargained with, and it remembers every bargain.

2.2 The Vulture Gods are egregores

The invading Forces (Iron Saint, White Silence, Steppe-Hunger, Gold-Glutton) are not the Christian God, Tengri or any real faith’s deity. The saga never passes judgement on the Christian God. These Forces are egregores: spirits fed by institutions, by crusade, purchased baptism, conquest and ledger. They grow wherever those appetites gather, including in Lithuanian hearts (Jogaila’s ambition feeds the Gold-Glutton as surely as any Hanseatic counting-house). Honest Christians in the saga (the Franciscan martyrs of Vilnius, Jadvyga at her best) are not their servants and are often their victims.

2.3 Spirits are local

Below both layers are the dvasios: laumės, kaukai, aitvarai, žalčiai, vėlės, the spirits of particular groves, wells and households. They are bound by custom, the reciprocal courtesy of offering and respect. See bestiary.md.

3. Pantheon and Force mapping

3.1 The Gods

God Domain Element (Kalba) Force-face (when meddling) Notes
Dievas (Dievas Senelis) Sky, order, the distant father none none; walks as the Beggar Never bargains. Tests with requests (bread, coin, a story). Those who give are not saved, but seen (see §5).
Perkūnas Thunder, oaths, justice, war none (he cannot be asked) Perkūno Rūstybė Acts through claims (marks) and signs (lightning on oaks and banners). Punishes broken oaths and cowardice, including in his own.
Velnias The dead, the underworld, wealth, cattle, bargains, cleverness none Šešėlių Pardavėjas (a shard) Not evil. Hungry and fair: every deal is kept to the letter. Folk tales show him regularly cheated by clever peasants, which is the only defence.
Laima (with Dalia and Giltinė) Fate, share, death none none Spins every fate at birth. Dalia allots fortune; Giltinė, her sister, cuts the thread (see bestiary). Laima’s spinning is why Praurimė’s sight shows possibilities, never certainties.
Žemyna Earth, fertility, oaths sworn on soil Earth (“Žemė tau laikys”) Šaknų Motina (Root-Mother) Oaths sworn by kissing the earth are binding physically: the oath-breaker’s land rejects him.
Saulė The Sun, mother of life none directly none Her tears are amber. Every piece of amber is a drop of solar grief, which is why amber stores memory.
Mėnuo The Moon, warrior, faithless husband (he left Saulė for Aušrinė and Perkūnas cut him in half, hence the phases) none none Governs tides and therefore Bangpūtys’s zenith.
Gabija The hearth, sacred fire Fire (“Ugnis tau degs”) none; she does not hunger, she guards Every household fire is a small shrine. Spitting into fire or stepping over it is sacrilege.
Bangpūtys Sea, storms, waves Water (“Vanduo tau teks”) none Bound in an old feud with Perkūnas over Jūratė (§3.4).
Vėjopatis The four winds, messenger between worlds Wind (“Vėjas tau neš”) none Carries the vėlės along the Birds’ Way. Speech with the dead goes through him.
Praurimė (doubtful: known mainly from 19th-century mythographers, Narbutt; authenticity disputed) The fire of the stars, the sky-twin of the eternal flame Stars (“Žvaigždės tau švytės”) none Folk memory remembers her as a goddess of sacred fire; the saga’s seers know her as the fire above, the stars read as fate-threads lit by Laima’s spinning. Both are true.
Medeina Forests, the hunt, wild beasts none merged into the Root-Mother Medeina and Žemyna’s grief fused during the 13th-century crusades. The Root-Mother speaks with both voices.

3.2 The native Forces

  • Šaknų Motina (Root-Mother): Žemyna and Medeina’s wild grief. Wants the forest kept and the iron and plough stopped. Takes the “Amber Debt”, royal blood for land lost (D7). Servants: Pelkių Eglė, the vilkatai (iron_wolves.md), Galminas.
  • Perkūno Rūstybė: justice without mercy. Wants oaths kept and cowards punished. Takes claims on warriors. Marked Vytautas in 1382 (ch. 10).
  • Šešėlių Pardavėjas (Shadow-Seller): Velnias’s splinter. Wants deals, and through deals the dead (the past) to outweigh the living (the future). Takes “threads”, small obligations that compound. See D2.
  • Gintaro Karalienė (Amber Queen): see §3.4. Wants memory kept. Takes the vessel’s mortality.

3.3 The Vulture Forces (egregores)

  • Geležinis Šventasis (Iron Saint, Ferrum Sanctum). Fed by: crusade, the Reise season, forced baptism. Signs: furnace heat in winter, the smell of quench-water, crosses that ring like anvils. Grants: tireless marching, absence of pity.
  • Baltoji Tyla (White Silence). Fed by: purchased purity, baptisms bought or sold for land and crowns. Signs: frost flowers on blades, sound dying, lilies on frozen fields. Grants: fearlessness, early winters (the “Crusader’s Winter”). Note: it gathers wherever a baptism is being bought, which is why it settles over Jogaila’s Vilnius in 1382 (he is already pricing a crown) and over Krėva (where the 1385 union will be signed). This resolves review item M6.
  • Stepių Alkis (Steppe-Hunger, Erdene-Chuluu). Fed by: conquest-without-end and the hubris of horizon-kings. Signs: dust devils, horse-hair in wells. Grants: the vision of an endless empire, which is its lure. Book II.
  • Aukso Rijūnas (Gold-Glutton, Mercator-Rex). Fed by: ledgers, tolls, land sold by the furlong. Signs: coins that are always warm, a counting voice in quiet rooms. Grants: prosperity that empties people out. Present from Book I (Hanseatic envoys, ch. 28) and dominant in Book IV.

3.4 The Amber Queen: an office, not a goddess

  1. Jūratė, a sea goddess, lived in an amber palace beneath the Baltic. She loved Kastytis, a mortal fisherman, against the law that gods may not love the dying.
  2. Perkūnas, keeper of oaths, shattered the palace with lightning, killed Kastytis, and chained Jūratė to the ruins. The amber that washes up at Palanga after storms is her palace, and the sea’s moaning is her grief. (Folk legend; see legends_and_songs.md.)
  3. Her grief did not die. It became the Amber Queen: the sea’s memory of everything gods and men have destroyed. It needs a mortal vessel to act on land. Vessels are drawn from the Palanga amber-priestess line, and each vessel ends by giving her mortality back to the sea.
  4. Birutė is the last vessel (D1). Her transformation in 1384 (ch. 27) is the last time the Queen walks; after it, she can act only through amber already touched by Birutė (Vytautas’s pebble, the kneeling statue).
  5. Why Perkūnas “punishes” Vytautas (mystical_connections.md): he is the Queen’s vessel’s son, and the feud with Jūratė is unfinished.

4. The Silent Pact and its unwinding

4.1 The Pact (1345)

When Algirdas and Kęstutis overthrew their brother Jaunutis and divided the realm (Algirdas east from Vilnius, Kęstutis west from Trakai), the Krivis of Šventaragis bound them to the gods in the Tylioji Sutartis:

  • The gods give: Lithuania will not be conquered. The Order may raid and burn but never hold the heartland.
  • The princes give: (1) the sacred flames (Šventaragis, Palanga, the Samogitian alkai) are never extinguished; (2) no Gediminid sheds Gediminid blood; (3) the Amber Debt: for every land lost to the plough-and-cross, royal blood is owed.
  • Witnesses: every spirit of place. The Pact is what keeps the dvasios courteous. It is a treaty between the land’s people and the land’s spirits.

Historical anchor: the GDL in fact remained the last unconquered pagan state in Europe through constant crusade (1283–1387). The Pact is the saga’s reason why.

4.3 The Unbinding (Atsaistymas)

The saga’s equivalent of a world-changing cataclysm; it drives the later books’ monster-haunted, grey tone.

The Pact made the spirits courteous. As it unwinds, they become unbound:

  • Before 1382: spirits follow custom. Feed the žaltys and it guards the house; honour the laumės and they weave for you. Folk rules work as advertised.
  • 1382–1387 (Book I): the first unbound appear at the margins. Pelkių Eglė’s swamp eats Skirgaila’s trackers (ch. 16); the forest is “silent” (ch. 1, 20) because the spirits are listening for the Pact to break.
  • After 1387 (Books II–V): with no flames and no Krivis, spirits are no longer owed courtesy and no longer give it. Abandoned groves breed ravenous vėlės (the unburned dead of the crusades). Aitvarai turn from thieves to arsonists. Laumės who once swapped babies now drown them. Priests cannot see them, holy water is useless against things it does not believe in, and the peasantry turns, quietly, to whoever still knows the old ways: the last žyniai, the hedge-raganos, and the feared vilkatai (iron_wolves.md).

This is the moral engine: the baptism saves Lithuania from the Order and damns it to its own unfed dead. Neither choice was clean.

5. Signs, Whispers, and the Beggar

(Supersedes mystical_mechanics.md, which remains as a quick reference.)

  • Whispers reach mortals between waking and sleep. Velnias’s whisper sounds like your own voice (the Echo). The Root-Mother’s is leaves, the Wind-Talk. The Amber Queen’s is surf, and it comes only through amber.
  • Signs are a Force’s only direct action outside vessels: lightning (Perkūnas), sap turning red (Root-Mother, before a massacre), frost-shapes (White Silence), crows leading armies (Root-Mother or Perkūnas), coins turning to stone (Dievas: what you pay for truth is worth nothing as money).
  • The Beggar (Dievas Senelis) appears at turning points and asks a small thing. Rule: those who give are not rescued, they are seen: their path becomes clear to them, even if it leads to death. Kęstutis gave the coin (ch. 4) and walked to Krėva knowing. Those who refuse are not punished; they are left blind to their own road. (Revises hidden_immortals.md §3.)

6. What the gods cannot do

  • Act directly in the Middle World except through vessels, claims, signs, answered Kalba, or spirits they command.
  • Undo a death. Even the Amber Queen only keeps the dead (in memory, in amber).
  • Break their own word. A god’s bargain is iron; this is what makes Velnias beatable by the clever.
  • Hold a baptised land by right. They hold it only by custom, people still leaving offerings. This is why the later books’ battleground is habit, not territory.
