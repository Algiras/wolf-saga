# The Turning Year

*Canonical reference for time in the saga: how the year was counted in the 1380s, the old month names, the festival wheel, the saga's magical zeniths, the Teutonic Order's Christian calendar, and a season-by-season table for Book I. The zeniths follow `magic_rules.md` §3.3, which overrides `books/1/senoves_kalba_magic_system.md`. Dates follow `master_chronology.md`. Pronunciation of every festival name is in `pronunciation_guide.md` §12.*

**Labels:** **[Record]** is attested; for folk customs this usually means recorded by ethnographers between the 16th and 20th centuries. **[Saga invention]** was made for this series. **(verify)** is to be checked before it goes into chapter text.

> **A caution about "pagan" customs.** Almost everything we know about Lithuanian festival customs was written down **centuries after 1387**: by 16th–17th-century churchmen, and above all by 19th–20th-century folklorists recording peasants who were Catholic. What those sources preserve is **double faith**, old practice under Christian names. How any of it looked in 1382 is unrecorded. The saga is entitled to its pre-Christian versions (D3: magic and custom may fill the gaps), but it should know that it is reconstructing them.

## 1. Counting time in the 1380s [Record]

### 1.1 The Julian calendar and the eight-day gap

Everyone who wrote dates in 1382 used the **Julian** calendar. In the 14th century it ran **8 days behind** the sun as the modern (Gregorian) calendar measures it (for dates between 1 Mar 1300 and 28 Feb 1500, Julian + 8 = proleptic Gregorian). All dates in this file and in `master_chronology.md` are Julian, as in the chronicles.

The astronomical turning points of the 1380s therefore fell on these **Julian** dates (computed with Meeus's formulas, ±1 day):

| Event | Julian date, 1382–87 | Old saying |
|---|---|---|
| Spring equinox | **12–13 March** | — |
| Summer solstice | **13–14 June** | — |
| Autumn equinox | **14–15 September** | — |
| Winter solstice | **13–14 December** | St Lucy's Day (13 Dec) was "the shortest day" in Julian-era lore across Europe |

**Consequence for the saga.** Christmas Eve (*Kūčios*, 24 Dec) fell about **11 days after** the winter solstice, and St John's Eve (*Joninės*, 23 June) about **10 days after** the summer solstice. The folk equation "Kūčios = the solstice, Rasos = midsummer" is right in spirit, because the Church set its feasts on the old solstice festivals, but in the 1380s the days no longer matched.

**Saga rule [Saga invention]:** the *Kalba* zeniths are **astronomical**, because the elements watch the sky, not the Church calendar. In 1382 the pagan solstice night falls on about **13 December**. Christianised households keep *Kūčios* on 24 December. After 1387 the feast drifts away from the zenith, and the night that families keep sacred is no longer the night the stars are strongest. It is a quiet image of the Unbinding, and Books II–V can use it.

### 1.2 How documents dated

- **Latin and German documents** dated by **feast days**: *in die Assumptionis beatae Mariae virginis* (15 Aug), *in vigilia Assumptionis* (14 Aug), *feria secunda post Michaelis* (the Monday after Michaelmas). The year was Anno Domini. Chanceries in the Empire often began the year at Christmas; the Order's practice should be checked *(verify)*.
- **Ruthenian documents** used the Byzantine era **from the Creation**. AD 1382 is **6890** (in the March-to-February reckoning common in Rus' chronicles; *verify which year-start a given source uses*). A Ruthenian chronicle entry for Kęstutis's death reads "in the year 6890".
- **The Krėva Act** is dated **14 August 1385**, the **eve of the Assumption**, and so the eve of the third anniversary of Kęstutis's death in the same castle (master_chronology).

### 1.3 How Lithuanians counted

Pagan Lithuania wrote nothing down. It counted by **moons** (*mėnuo* means both "moon" and "month"), by seasons, by feasts and by **work**: *rugiapjūtė* (the rye harvest), *linarūtė* (flax-pulling), *sėja* (sowing). Whether a seven-day week was in general use among pagan Lithuanians is unknown *(verify)*. Where the week appears in older Lithuanian and in the dialects, its day names are **Slavic loans** [Record]: *panedėlis* (Monday), *utarninkas* (Tuesday), *sereda* (Wednesday), *četvergas* (Thursday), *pėtnyčia* (Friday), *subata* (Saturday), *nedėlia* (Sunday). The modern names (*pirmadienis* "first day" and so on) are 20th-century coinages. Kęstutis should not say *penktadienis*. If he names a day at all, it is a feast or a moon.

The modern Lithuanian month names are old folk names, but they were **standardised only around 1900**. Before that, regions used different names, and some names sat a month earlier or later. **None is attested from the 14th century.** The saga may use them as the moon-names of the pagan year (**[Saga convention]**), knowing they are attested later.

## 3. Moon and sun

### 3.1 The moon [Record]

Lithuanian names the phases: **jaunatis** (new moon, "the young one"), **priešpilnis** (waxing, "before full"), **pilnatis** (full moon), **delčia** (waning). Folk practice recorded in the 19th–20th centuries sows in the waxing moon, fells timber and cuts hair in the waning moon, and does not start anything at the new moon. The Moon (*Mėnuo*) is a warrior and a faithless husband whom Perkūnas cut in half, which is why he wanes (cosmology §3.1).

### 3.2 The saga's reckoning [Saga invention]

- **The krivis of Šventaragis keeps the year.** The year turns at the **winter solstice**, read from the sun's rising point against the hills of the Neris valley. The year counts twelve moons from there.
- **The blind moon.** Twelve lunar months fall about 11 days short of a solar year. When the twelfth moon dies too early, the krivis declares a **thirteenth moon**, *aklasis mėnuo* ("the blind moon"), in which no oath is sworn and no marriage made. (Name and rule are inventions. They give the saga a real-sounding lunisolar mechanism, since medieval Lithuania must have reconciled moons and seasons somehow, and nothing is recorded.)
- **After 1387** there is no krivis and no one to declare the blind moon. Peasants slowly adopt the Church calendar, and the old moon-year drifts. Another sign of the Unbinding.
- **The zeniths are astronomical** (§1.1). The calendar of feasts is how people *keep* them, and the two need not coincide.

### 3.3 The zeniths (from `magic_rules.md` §3.3)

| Element (deity) | Zenith | Festival that keeps it | Julian date in the 1380s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stars (**Praurimė**) | Winter-solstice **midnight** | **Kūčios** | ~13 Dec (Christian Kūčios 24 Dec) |
| Fire (**Gabija**) | Summer-solstice **noon** | **Rasos** | ~13–14 June (Christian Joninės 23–24 June) |
| Water (**Bangpūtys**) | Full moon or high tide; strongest at the **spring ice-break** | The spring equinox feast (the magic doc's "Pusiaukojis") | Equinox ~12 Mar; the ice usually goes out late Mar–Apr. **Recommended rule:** the first full moon after the ice-break |
| Earth (**Žemyna**) | Autumn-equinox **dusk** | **Dagotuvės** | ~14–15 Sept |

*Note on the Water zenith:* `senoves_kalba_magic_system.md` ties Bangpūtys to the spring equinox ("Pusiaukojis"), while `magic_rules.md` ties it to the full moon and the ice-break. The two agree if the spring zenith is **the first full moon after the ice goes out**. The Church fixes Easter by the same full moon (the first Sunday after the first full moon of spring), so Bangpūtys and Easter compete for the same night. That is good material for 1387.

## 5. The Christian calendar overlay: the Order's year [Record]

- **Two Reise seasons.** The Order raided Lithuania mostly in **winter** (December–February, when swamps and rivers froze into roads) and in **late summer** (August–September, after the harvest). Western "guests" (French, English, Bohemian knights) came for them. By custom the Reisen were tied to the great Marian feasts: the **Purification (2 Feb)** for the winter season and the **Assumption (15 Aug)** for the summer *(verify for the 1380s)*. A banquet of honour, the **Ehrentisch** (Table of Honour), was sometimes held before a Reise.
- **Mud seasons.** Spring thaw (March–April) and autumn rains (October–November) stopped armies. The "two muddy seasons" are why Book I's set pieces cluster in high summer and deep winter (`geography_gazetteer.md`).
- **Marian feasts** (the Order's patroness is Mary): Purification 2 Feb, Annunciation 25 Mar, **Assumption 15 Aug**, Nativity of Mary 8 Sept. *(The Visitation, 2 July, was only instituted in 1389; no Order knight keeps it in Book I.)*
- **Dates that fall on feasts:**
  - **15 Aug 1382:** Kęstutis found dead at Krėva on the **Assumption**, the feast of the Order's own patroness. The Lithuanian name *Žolinė* belongs to the Christian centuries; in the saga, the peasants who later keep Žolinė half-remember it as "the day the old duke died". *(15 Aug is the traditional date used in `master_chronology.md`; some accounts give an earlier August date. Verify.)*
  - **21 Oct 1383:** Vytautas baptised *Wigand* at Tapiau on **St Ursula's Day**.
  - **14 Aug 1385:** the Krėva Act, on the **eve of the Assumption**.
  - **18 Feb and 4 Mar 1386:** Jogaila's wedding and coronation both fall in **Shrovetide**, the last days before Lent (Ash Wednesday was 7 Mar 1386), when weddings were still allowed. The haste of the Kraków calendar is visible in the dates.
  - **Feb–Mar 1387:** the baptism of Lithuania falls in **Lent** (Ash Wednesday 20 Feb 1387; Easter 7 Apr). Jogaila's privilege to the Catholic boyars is usually dated **20 Feb 1387**, which would be Ash Wednesday itself *(verify the date)*.
  - **15 Jul 1410:** Žalgiris, on the feast of the **Dispersion of the Apostles** (Długosz notes it).

## 7. Where the chapters diverge, and the cheapest fixes

1. **Ch. 1–3 are set at an autumn Dagotuvės; the arrest was in early August.**
   - Where: `01_senasis_sernas.qmd:3` ("Ruduo šiemet… atslinko anksti") and `:5` (the first Dagotuvės, harvest end); `03_gintaro_sapnas.qmd:7` (Dagotuvės songs) and `:13` ("nors dar ne žiema").
   - **Fix:** make the feast the **rye harvest-home** (*pabaigtuvės*) of late July. It is still a harvest feast with Žemyna's thanks, and it keeps every image (new bread, the women thanking Žemyna). If the author wants the name, Dagotuvės can be the saga's name for the rye harvest-home rather than the equinox feast. In that case Žemyna's **zenith** stays at the equinox, and Birutė's ch. 3 working is off-zenith either way (magic_rules §3.3 already doubles its cost).
2. **Ch. 2 has snow at harvest time.**
   - Where: `02_vilko_perspejimas.qmd:81` ("šešėliai ant sniego").
   - **Fix:** use dew, stubble or moonlight (review C13).
3. **Ch. 6 says autumn in Vilnius.**
   - Where: `06_geleziniai_vartai.qmd:3` ("rudens pavakarę").
   - **Fix:** a late-summer evening, with dust and harvest smoke.
4. **Ch. 8 says winter at Krėva, but the death on 15 Aug is a fixed date (D3).**
   - Where: `08_krevos_pozemiai.qmd:3` ("prasidėjo tikroji pilka žiema"), with summer remembered in `:9`.
   - **Fix:** keep the *cold* and lose the *season*. The dungeon is cold in August: stone that never warms, "a winter that lives under the castle". Then the ch. 8 memory of green summer becomes the summer just outside the wall.
   - **The irony to use:** Kęstutis dies on the **Assumption**, the great feast of the Order's patroness, in a castle where three years later (on the eve of the same feast) Jogaila will sign the union.
5. **Ch. 10 has autumn ice on the morning after the death.**
   - Where: `10_saltas_rytas.qmd:7` ("rudeninį ledą").
   - **Fix:** use a metal or stone simile, or dew. And show the cremation (H4).
6. **Ch. 19 compresses about ten months.**
   - The baptism (21 Oct 1383) comes in what reads as the same scene as the winter 1382 arrival.
   - **Fix:** add a date header or a time-skip line. Give the baptism its autumn (and St Ursula's Day, if the author wants a detail the Order would note).
7. **Ch. 20–21 are set in spring.**
   - Where: `20_svetimi_dieviai.qmd:17`, `21_pirmoji_kova.qmd:15, 29`.
   - These chapters come after the October 1383 baptism, so they can only be **spring 1384**, when Vytautas was the Order's vassal (Königsberg, Jan 1384). That works with the review's recommended recast of ch. 22–25 as the **July 1384** burnings (H7).
   - **Fix:** date them spring 1384, and move the "first battle" to a raid of that spring.
8. **Ch. 25 is set in spring ("last year's leaves").**
   - Where: `25_isdaviku_isdavikas.qmd:9`.
   - If ch. 22–25 become the July 1384 defection, change it to midsummer.
9. **Ch. 27 has a November simile in late summer.**
   - Where: `27_gintaro_auka.qmd:5`.
   - The full moon after the July 1384 reconciliation is ~2 Aug 1384 (or ~31 Aug). The simile "the first November ice on a lake" is fine as a simile, but a reader may take it as the season. Optional.
10. **Escape timing (an opportunity, not an error).** The record says only "autumn 1382". Setting the escape on **Vėlinės night** (around 2 Nov 1382, in a moonless week: new moon ~6 Nov) puts Birutė's wind and fog working at **Vėjopatis's zenith**, when its cost is halved. That explains how she could reach Krėva from Palanga at all, and makes the fog the dead's own weather. *(Saga suggestion.)*

| Year | Bangpūtys (equinox; first spring full moon) | Gabija (solstice noon) | Žemyna (equinox dusk) | Praurimė (solstice midnight; moon) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1382 | 12 Mar; full moon 30 Mar | 13–14 Jun | 15 Sep | 13 Dec (waxing; full 21 Dec) |
| 1383 | 12–13 Mar; full moon 19–20 Mar | 14 Jun | 15 Sep | 13–14 Dec (waning; full 10–11 Dec) |
| 1384 | 12 Mar; full moon 6–7 Apr | 13 Jun | 14–15 Sep | 13 Dec (**new moon**: the darkest, clearest star-night of the decade) |
| 1385 | 12 Mar; full moon 27 Mar | 13–14 Jun | 15 Sep | 13 Dec (waxing; full 17 Dec) |
| 1386 | 12–13 Mar; full moon 16–17 Mar | 13–14 Jun | 15 Sep | 13–14 Dec (waning; full 6–7 Dec) |
| 1387 | 12–13 Mar; full moon 4–5 Apr | 14 Jun | 15 Sep | 13–14 Dec (new moon 11–12 Dec) |

*The first spring full moon is the earliest Bangpūtys date. If the ice has not yet gone out on the Neris, the zenith waits for the next full moon (§3.3).*
