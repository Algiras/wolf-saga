Giminės¶

House

Description

Alšėniškiai (Holszański, princes of Halshany)

Lithuanian-Ruthenian princes; Julijona married Vytautas (1418), her niece Sophia married Jogaila (1422).

Gediminaičiai (House of Gediminas)

Dynasty of Grand Duke Gediminas (c.1275–1341/42). The name is a 19th-century label; the Vytis and the Columns are its arms.

Goštautai (Gasztołd)

Boyar family risen under Vytautas; Jonas Goštautas (d. 1458) led the council that elected Casimir in 1440.

House of Luxembourg

Sigismund (1368–1437): King of Hungary, of the Romans, of Bohemia, Emperor; offered Vytautas a crown at Lutsk, 1429.

Jogailaičiai (Jagiellonians)

Jogaila’s line: Grand Dukes of Lithuania and, from 1386, kings of Poland; Vytis plus the double cross.

Kęsgailos (Kieżgajło)

Samogitian magnates; Mykolas Kęsgaila (d. 1450) was elder of Samogitia.

Masovian Piasts

Piast cadet line at Warsaw, Czersk and Płock; married into both Gediminid branches.

Palanga amber-priestess line (Gintaro vaidilutės)

The saga’s invention: the maternal line of Birutė, vessels of the Amber Queen, keepers of the shore flame at Palanga.

Rurikids of Moscow

Moscow’s ruling dynasty: Dmitry Donskoy, Vasily I (Vytautas’s son-in-law), Vasily II (his grandson).

Čartoriskiai (Czartoryski)

Volhynian princes, probably descended from Konstantinas; the killers of Žygimantas in 1440.
