# Book IV end state, hand-off to Book V

Source: Book IV ch. 21–30 as written (ch. 30 = `30_epilogas_nematoma_karuna.qmd`). Facts below are on the page; "verify" marks gaps or tensions.

## Timeline of the end (1430)
- **15 Aug (Žolinė):** crown on the road in a gold-painted oak chest (ch. 21); Henė's last offer in the vault the same night (ch. 22).
- **Late Aug:** at the Noteć ford on the Greater Poland border, the Polish border guard (**Jan Czarnkowski**, formerly "kaštelionas Jakub of Gorlice"; Oleśnicki's envoy **Baltramiejus Rogala**, formerly Stanislovas, ch. 23) find the chest full of dry leaves. The Hungarian goldsmith's apprentice **Pál** (19) swears he held real gold. Henė tells Pilkoji: "I never offered a lie; it was my most honest bargain, and I lost it."
- **Early Oct (Jogaila has arrived with the Poles; the 8 Sep date had already slipped to 29 Sep):** Vilnius, the coronation that does not happen; Vytautas rages, then adjourns "until St Martin" (11 Nov) (ch. 24, re-dated in the fact-check pass; it was "8 Sep"). **Mid-Oct (c. 15-16 Oct, was "9 Sep"):** his fall on the Trakai road while riding out of Vilnius behind Jogaila (three ribs and an old boil between the shoulder blades; the black root and fresh moss that Eglė's forest leaves; the "washerwoman" Giltinė seen at the stream). He is carried to Trakai and the bed is moved onto the warm floor.
- **Mid-Oct:** Jogaila's visit (ch. 26): the Krėva secret is **said aloud** (Henė's voice through Skirgaila's mouth: "Ar jis turi gyventi, Vladislovai?"; both men were silent). Vytautas lays his hand on Jogaila's wrist and the White Silence lifts for him. Švitrigaila waits in the corridor and is not mentioned.
- **19–20 Oct (night):** Eglė's farewell (ch. 27). **20 Oct:** Giltinė stands at the bed's head; Vytautas has the bed turned and buys **seven nights**; she says she will take him "where you think you have another hour" (ch. 28).
- **27 Oct, dusk:** Vytautas dies (ch. 29). Jogaila is in the castle, sitting in the corridor (noon line in ch. 29); only Julijona is in the room. "Neužgeso" is his last word. Giltinė takes him at "another hour".
- **27 Oct night – 1 Nov:** ch. 30 (below).

## State at the moment of death (ch. 29)
- The flame in the vault **goes out at once**. The pebble in its pouch **cracks** ("like thin ice"). The warmth leaves him "in one sigh". Both his ears open for a moment; he hears a small hard round thing fall and roll (Henė's stone). Birutė's whispered name for him is said once more (never written out in the text). **No thunder**: Perkūnas holds his storm "out of respect".
- Julijona is reading him **Ona's letter** (Ona knew about the vault; "Grįžk, jei gali. Jei negali, žinok, kad aš žinau"). Julijona also knows. Elijas (the Karaim) is long dead.
- **Wishes (ch. 28):** buried in **Vilnius cathedral** ("where a fire once burned"). **Silver wolf and pebble** to **Žiežirba, via Pilkoji, handed over at Kūčios (24 Dec 1430)**, not at once. Pilkoji is to tell him: show the wolf in the Dykra to his wolves ("jie pažins"). Julijona is asked that **no one go down to the vault for three days**; Žiežirba decides for himself. Franciscan **father Benediktas** (Samogitian, Kražiai) took the confession; Vytautas withheld one thing, so absolution covers what it can.
- Kęstutis's **sword** (silver line unrusted) stands by the door; no fate given. **Silver wolf** is on Vytautas's neck. The pebble stays in the pouch on his chest until Pilkoji takes both.
- Švitrigaila sent greetings at noon on the 27th; Vytautas: "Pasakyk jam, kad palauktų."

## Characters (alive, where, what they carry)
- **Žiežirba**: ~40, throat-scar, grey at the temples; in the vault with a pot of cold ash. True name still forgotten. He fed the flame from Kalvis's ash to the last. Eglė left the small **brass buckle** (Vytautas's, which she wore under the žaltys, Book III ch. 23/Book I ch. 16) on the black ring's edge, and asked the flame to give Pilkoji her names; so the buckle lies by the cold ring (verify in V ch. 1).
- **Pilkoji** (*Vilkų Motina*, 26 years of carried names): lost the face of her sister Dalia (ch. 23); keeps a rowan leaf under her coat; carried the feast-day bog-iron knife and nearly "entered" at the vault door (a second burn on her palm, ch. 22); she keeps **Eglė's lost names** (Šventaragis valley, Mantas, Vykintė, the pine Žalioji). Wolves with her in 1430: **Kirmėlė** (old, white beard), **Uosis** (white-muzzled).
- **Eglė**: no žaltys since 1417; at the end of ch. 27 she walks into the pines with only her name left, which she remembers at dawn; the forest draws back from the lake. She says the Root-Mother **sleeps** (will wake when the axes are gone). Her cost this time: her last names. **Pilkoji carries her.**
- **Julijona**: widow, no child; clever; knows. **Danila**: Vytautas's servant. **Karaim captain** (Elijas's younger son) keeps the door; he is the Book V ch. 4 guard captain.
- **Jogaila**: ~68, in Lithuania (Vilnius area), approves Švitrigaila (verify). Silence lifted by Vytautas's touch; **the nightingale** (V ch. 12) is "the first sound without holes" after that October day. **Henė's hold on him ended** ("Henė su juo baigė"); he still has Jogaila's stone.
- **Švitrigaila**: raised as Grand Duke by the Vilnius lords within days (the epilogue only shows rumour on ~31 Oct; the date is **verify**). **Žygimantas Kęstutaitis** (Vytautas's quiet brother) not on page in 21–30.
- **Oleśnicki**: has won; his envoy Baltramiejus Rogala (formerly Stanislovas) wears Henė's **golden chain**, which "has not yet learned to exhale" (ch. 20). Oleśnicki told him to wear it to September and then give it to the cathedral with a Mass "for the one who wore it". Whether it has been handed over by Oct 1430 is **verify**; ch. 30 says only that it "remained in Kraków with the bishop's people".
- **Sigismund**: ~62, in Buda; withdrawn. The Gold-Glutton's **Counter** (*Skaičiuotojas*) goes silent on the evening of the death (ch. 30).
- Ona's letter box with the old letters is with Julijona.

## Magic and objects ledger
- Pebble **cracked**. Flame **out**; there will be no more krikštas. Unbinding **5** (by book end); tier 4/3/2 = 0/9/90. Perkūnas's storm is **held for the funeral** (V ch. 2, Vilnius). Iron Wolf seen **dissolving into mist above the Duke's tower at dusk** (also glimpsed from the south shore in ch. 30; V ch. 1 should show it from the island).
- **Henė's stones: one**, Jogaila's (light, floating, old crack that "grew a hair's width overnight" in ch. 30). Vytautas's stone fell in the death chamber, rolled to where Giltinė had stood by the bed's foot, and stopped.
- **Henė's golden chain**: with Oleśnicki's people (V ch. 19 returns it to Henė, then Žygimantas). His **yellow Hungarian bell** (left on Vytautas's knees in ch. 17) is on the sill of the death room; Henė left it there. His cap's bells are stuffed with wool.
- Henė's cost on stairs: each stroke of the death-bell shaved the edge of his shadow (shorter by a hand); it grew back away from the blessed walls.

## What ch. 30 establishes (new facts)
- Henė watched the dying from an **overturned boat on the south shore of Galvė** (folk belief: lightning doesn't look under a boat). He doffed his cap to the dissolving Iron Wolf ("Nieko asmeniško").
- That night he **climbed the stairs** to the death room (the Karaim captain at the door could not see him). Julijona received him. She told him that **Vytautas laughed once alone when Henė left in 1428**, with the bell on his knees: Henė silently counts "three" (ch. 17: Vytautas had counted two). Julijona asked him for nothing and offered nothing.
- At dawn on **28 Oct** he threw **Vytautas's stone into the lake** (no splash; a film of ice parted and closed). It is the **only stone gone from play**; Jogaila's remains.
- Henė's reasoning: the flame went out by death, not by Vytautas's word, so it belongs to **what will be told**, not to what he can buy (ch. 22: "bought things the dead give me; told things they keep"). The tale has begun: on **Vėlinių išvakarės** (~1 Nov) an old woman tells her grandson at the south shore: the Duke died *with* a crown, "only an invisible one"; the golden one turned to leaves; a jester sat by the road and "did not laugh that day"; "there was a fire under the castle that no one saw". She also says the Vilnius lords have already raised Švitrigaila. Henė: "Vaidinimas baigtas. Mitas lieka."
- His next move (for V ch. 6, 8): he reckons the old king has "four years, maybe five", and thinks of the **quiet brother (Žygimantas Kęstutaitis)** as the cheap, patient buy. He does not yet go.

## Historical fact-check

Pass date: 2026-10-12. Rule applied: D3, history is the fixed spine; where historians disagree the majority view is taken; the saga invents only where the record is silent. Format per item: claim -> record (source) -> action (confirmed / corrected in chNN / left as invention because the record is silent). "Fixed" chapter numbers are Book IV.

- **Jogaila's birth year (decided first, for Book V):** born **c. 1362** (the post-1990 revision by Tęgowski 1999 and Wasilewski, now the prevailing view in Polish and recent English scholarship; the older c. 1352 is still defended by some; sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_II_Jagie%C5%82%C5%82o and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogaila). Jogaila is therefore **62 in Oct 1424, 65 in Nov 1427, c. 68 in Oct 1430** (Vytautas, b. c. 1350, is about twelve years older). Book IV ages adjusted in ch14 (72 -> 62, 75 -> 65), ch26 and ch30 ("per septyniasdešimt" -> "beveik septyniasdešimt"). Book V should call him about 68 in 1430.

The "verify sums" in the outline is cleared (10,000 roubles).
11. **Lutsk congress, Jan 1429** -> held at Lutsk castle, 9-29 Jan (6 Jan in other accounts); Jogaila arrived first with the Poles, **Sigismund arrived late (20 Jan)** with Queen Barbara; guests included Eric of Denmark, the papal legate Andrew (a Dominican), Byzantine envoys, Moscow (Vasily II, Metropolitan Photius), Tver, Ryazan, Mazovian princes, Livonian and Prussian Order envoys, the Moldavian hospodar Alexander the Good, Tatar khans' men; Oleśnicki, Jan of Tarnów and Wojciech Jastrzębiec on the Polish side; **Švitrigaila is mentioned by no source**; https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucko_suva%C5%BEiavimas ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Lutsk ; https://pl.wikisource.org/wiki/Litwa_za_Witolda/1429 ; https://historia.dorzeczy.pl/288016/zjazd-lucki-proba-sklocenia-jagielly-i-witolda-i-dyplomatyczna-gra.html -> **corrected in ch18**: arrival order (Jogaila with the Poles first, Sigismund "tik dvidešimtą dieną"; Jogaila "jau dvidešimt dienų gyveno Lucke"; "per tris savaites"), "du kardinolai" -> "du vengrų vyskupai". Guest list otherwise confirmed; the outline's "verify the guest list" is cleared. Švitrigaila's absence in ch18: the record is silent (flag for Book V ch15).
12. **Sigismund's offer; Jogaila's agreement, then withdrawal** -> Sigismund proposed the crown; Jogaila agreed at first (answering that he must consult his council), then withdrew after the Polish council, where Oleśnicki, Oporowski and Tarnów/Szafraniec opposed; Sigismund wanted a Lithuanian kingdom to weaken the union; same sources as 11 plus https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witold_Kiejstutowicz -> **confirmed** (ch19; the sleigh ride on the Styr and "Taip" are invention). Sigismund is King of the Romans and Hungary, emperor only from 1433, b. 1368: confirmed (ch17 "šešiasdešimt", ch19 "beveik dvidešimčia metų" younger than Vytautas).
13. **Planned coronation date and place** -> first set for 15 Aug 1430, moved to **8 Sep 1430** (Nativity of the Virgin; Sigismund's letters of Jul 1430), then moved by Vytautas in early Sept to **29 Sep** (Michaelmas); place **Vilnius** (crowns to be made/assigned "in Vilna", anointing by the ordinary, Bishop Matthias of Vilnius); https://www.15min.lt/naujiena/aktualu/istorija/ldk-istorija-neivykusi-vytauto-karunacija-ir-bergzdzios-karunos-paieskos-582-376128 ; Bar 2017, https://apcz.umk.pl/RH/article/download/RH.2017.02/14433/39303 (nn. 63, 86) ; Szybkowski 2010, https://bazhum.muzhp.pl/media/texts/sredniowiecze-polskie-i-powszechne/2010-tom-2-6/sredniowiecze_polskie_i_powszechne-r2010-t2_6-s142-156.pdf -> **confirmed** 8 Sep and Vilnius (ch19, ch21, ch22 unchanged; the outline's "8 Sep, verify" is cleared). Not Trakai. The saga's "adjourned until St Martin" (ch24) is invention on top of the real 8 Sep -> 29 Sep postponements; ch24 now says the day had already been moved from Marijos Gimimas to Mykolinės.
14. **How the crown was intercepted; who guarded the roads; "Jakub of Gorlice"** -> the crown was **never physically intercepted**. Sigismund sent two courtiers ahead, the jurist **Baptista Cigala and the Silesian Sigismund Roth** (left Vienna 4 Jul); they were robbed and beaten before 19 Aug on the Neumark / Greater Poland border by the royal guards under **Jan Czarnkowski** (later podkomorzy of Poznań; in 1430 not a castellan), who took their letters, horses and c. 1,000 ducats' worth of valuables (Komtur of Schlochau, 19 Aug: "In ist die crone nicht genomen, si haben ir nicht gehad"). Jogaila read the captured letters (at Jędrzejów, after 15 Aug) and ordered every road from the Neumark watched; the main crown embassy (Archbishop Günther of Magdeburg, Dukes Přemek of Opava and Ludwig of Brzeg-Liegnitz, Hungarian and Bohemian lords; crowns by Nuremberg goldsmiths) waited c. 1.5 months at Frankfurt an der Oder (9 Sep report) and turned back; the "crown seized and fused with the Kraków bishop's crown" is a later Lithuanian-chronicle legend; Oleśnicki is the tradition's hero (Długosz). "Jakub of Gorlice" is **not attested** for 1430; sources: Bar 2017 (nn. 65-66, citing Długosz Annales XI), Szybkowski 2010, 15min/Petrauskas (above), https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witold_Kiejstutowicz -> **corrected in ch20, ch21, ch23**: Jakub of Gorlice -> **Janas Čarnkovskis** (Greater Poland border-guard commander); the route Buda-Carpathians-southern ford -> Silesia / Neumark / **Noteć ford** (ch20 Lászlo's tip; ch21 dateline "kelias per Naująją Marką į Lenkijos sieną", Brandenburg sands instead of mountains, "rytuose" for Trakai; ch23 dateline "Noteties brastas, Didžiosios Lenkijos siena", the wolves run from the west and go home east); the crown made in **Nuremberg** from Kremnica gold, Pál travelling there with the gold (ch21). Left as invention (record silent): the gold-painted chest, Ebersdorf, Pál, the leaves, Oleśnicki's informant Rogala. Ch24 now has Jogaila admit he read the captured letters and ordered the roads watched ("kas atsitiko su karūna, kuri keliavo iš Budos ... nuspręsta dar rugpjūtį").
15. **Oleśnicki** -> leading opponent of the coronation; present at Lutsk; Bishop of Kraków appointed 1 Jul 1423, consecrated 19 Dec 1423; sent with Mikołaj Białucha to Vytautas (Grodno) in autumn 1429; came to Lithuania with Jogaila in Oct 1430; Szybkowski: not at the Łęczyca council of Aug 1430; https://www.catholic-hierarchy.org/bishop/bolesn ; https://pl.wikisource.org/wiki/Litwa_za_Witolda/1429 ; https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/zbigniew-olesnicki/ -> **confirmed** (ch14, ch18-20, ch24). **Spelling: the standard Lithuanian form is "Zbignevas Olesnickis" (VLE headword, lt.wikipedia); no change**, the book already uses it; ch2's formal "Zbignevas iš Olesnicos" is a designation. Ch14 dateline Oct 1424 "vyskupo žiedas" fits (bishop since Dec 1423).
16. **Vytautas's last illness** -> no chronic illness before 1430; a sudden decline after a **fall from his horse on the road from Vilnius to Trakai, about 15-16 Oct 1430**, while escorting Jogaila (Długosz-based, written c. 30 years later; Petrauskas doubts details; Kraszewski adds an ulcer between the shoulder blades); on 21 Oct he issued a testamentary donation to Vilnius cathedral naming his burial place (St Michael's altar, next to Ona); bedridden about eleven days; https://www.15min.lt/gyvenimas/naujiena/ar-zinai/samokslas-ar-kritimas-nuo-zirgo-vu-rektorius-atskleide-ka-dabar-zinome-apie-netiketa-vytauto-mirti-1634-1398410 ; https://pl.wikisource.org/wiki/Litwa_za_Witolda/1430 -> **corrected in ch24, ch25, ch26-29**: the fall is moved from "9 Sep, riding alone" to **mid-Oct** (ch25 dateline "spalio vidurys"), Vytautas rides out of Vilnius behind Jogaila and the Polish lords (the tower-window image is gone), the physician also finds the old boil between the shoulder blades, the note to Jogaila reads "Ateik į salą" (he rode ahead to Trakai town); "prieš kelias savaites" for Pilkoji's visit; ch28 adds the cathedral donation "vakar" and burial "prie šv. Mykolo altoriaus, šalia Onos". The road-side Giltinė, the root and moss are myth explaining *how*, not *whether*.
17. **Vytautas's death, 27 Oct 1430, Trakai; who was present; burial** -> died 27 Oct 1430 at Trakai Island Castle, "in Jogaila's arms" (pl.wikipedia; Kraszewski), with Julijona (Kraszewski) and Bishop Matthias hearing confessions; Švitrigaila was already acting as lord at Trakai (Kraszewski, a 19th-century source); buried in Vilnius cathedral about eight days later (Kraszewski: 7 Nov); modern Lithuanian accounts are silent on the deathbed; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vytautas ; https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witold_Kiejstutowicz ; https://pl.wikisource.org/wiki/Litwa_za_Witolda/1430 -> **confirmed** date, place, burial (ch29, ch28, ch30); **corrected in ch29**: Jogaila is in the castle, sitting in the corridor ("karalius Jogaila vėl atplaukė iš miesto ir sėdi koridoriuje"), Vytautas: "Švitrigailai pasakyk, kad palauktų ... ### C. Conflicts in the canon docs (not edited)
