# Book V, ch. 23-26: notes (end state and established facts)

Files: `23_vaikas_soste.qmd` (1,658 w), `24_seseliu_pardavejo_veidas.qmd` (1,946 w), `25_gintaro_karalienes_tylejimas.qmd` (1,569 w), `26_rastas_pries_burtus.qmd` (1,584 w).

## Established per chapter

**Ch 23, 29 Jun 1440, Vilnius (POV Kazimieras, 12).**

- Sonka let him out of Kraków at the Florian Gate ("eik, kol jie nesusigriebė"), ten days before. Zbigniew Oleśnicki's sealed letter (Kraków council: keep him there, rule Vilnius by appointee) overtakes the party near Trakai. Goštautas pockets it unopened: "Rytoj. Kai atsakyti jau nebebus kam." Kazimieras vows to read every line from then on.
- Escort: Jonas Goštautas, Kristinas Astikas, Kęsgaila, Daugirdas. Both Čartoriskiai ride along: Ivanas (tall) has shaking hands; Aleksandras (short) is calm and nods (matches ch 21/22).
- Mykolas Žygimantaitis has fled (rumours: Moldavia / Moscow / still hiding). Goštautas explains the Prussian Confederation (Mar 1440) and says the Germans will ask for a Hanseatic office at Kaunas "next year or the one after".
- Investiture in the cathedral (sword too heavy, ducal cap too big, padded with cloth); banquet in the Lower Castle. The **Counter** sounds under the hall floor. It lists goods and "villages given for silence"; then **"Kazimieras, Jogailos sūnus: penkiasdešimt du"** (his remaining years; he does not understand). He gives the Danzig envoy's silver cup to a kitchen boy with a cut forearm, **without looking at its worth**. The Counter breaks off in that room (unmeasured gift, bestiary §28). Goštautas, who knows the Counter (ch 17), says "Taip nedaroma" and then "Gal ir gerai".
- Kazimieras speaks Ruthenian well and Lithuanian only by half (from his nurse).

**Ch 24, Jan 1441 (full moon), ice of Lake Galvė (POV Žiežirba, 51).**

- The ice has been **mute since Palm Sunday 1440**. He sits each full moon, 200 paces from the island at the south arch, on a fishermen's log beside the ice-hole, with the horn lantern (white lightning-fire, no names) and a crust of bread left out for the Iron Wolf. The Iron Wolf has not returned. A fox takes the bread.
- **Henė comes with no stones**: his hands still mime juggling. He ticks off the stones: Skirgaila collected; Jogaila died holding his; Vytautas said no. Henė brings **no Breath of the Void** (the sounds stay).
- **Unmasking:** he peels off his face like a glove. Beneath it is *tuštuma*, not skull or darkness: a depth that gives off warmth like a banked oven. All of the past stands in it at once (Žygimantas at the pillar listening, the two brothers in the valley, Pušis, Kalvis, Šešiapirštis, Rūdis, the boy with the clay pot). "Ne Dausose ir ne po akmeniu. Pas mane." In the hollow he speaks **in the keeper's own voice** (the Echo).
- **The last deal:** he will "stand the dead round the flame" and let them swear for the living (the Pact needs many mortals; he has more of them than the living). Price: the Amber Debt, **royal blood**. The only fitting blood left is Kazimieras's. The keeper need only bring the fire and stand there "as in February" (his ch 21 non-act). Offered gifts: Pilkoji hears wolves again, the unburned rest, names return, and "the beekeeper at Šiluva has forgotten half the words". The cracked pebble stirs once at "Gintaro skola": Birutė paid her share by walking into the sea of her own will.
- **Refusal** (grey, not triumphant): "Aš jau kartą nusprendžiau nieko nedaryti... Gal tai ir buvo pirma tavo dalis. Antros nebus." Reasons: (1) Žygimantas's question "can it be used?" is Henė's whole trade; (2) the Pact's flaw was that nobody asked the people, and the boy is not asked either; (3) a Pact sworn by the past for the future is the past ruling the living. He declines the offer to retrieve his lost true name from Henė: a name brought by Henė is a debt. He cannot remember what his mother called him. He keeps the lightning-fire: "Ji nieko nežada, todėl nieko neskolinga."
- Henė replaces the face, says "klausti yra mano sąžiningumas", and leaves: he will keep walking among those who cannot say no. The ice **sings again** as he goes. The keeper doesn't touch the bread.
- Count of wolves stated: Pilkoji **76**, plus **three** other wolves besides the keeper (so five in all, as ch 22 and ch 16 give: Uosis, Spyglys, Dobilas).

**Ch 25, autumn 1445 to Lent 1446, Palanga (POV the fisherman Dargis; Birutė frame coda in italics).**

- The kneeling statue **no longer glows**: grey, cool to the touch (not cold), still standing and never moved. Dargis's wife **Rimgailė**; the old midwife **Gedvilė** ("ją reikia prisiminti"); the young fisherman **Gaudas**, who takes shore amber without an offering (it is cold in the hand). Danzig cog; Varna rumour (king's body never found; "lenkai jau antri metai laukia"); Kazimieras still hesitating over the Polish crown. A Riga pedlar brings, in Lent 1446, the blinding of Vasilijus ("Tamsusis", Sofija's son, Vytautas's grandson).
- Rimgailė kneels at the statue; the sea withdraws five paces and returns. The statue stays grey but seems to lean toward her.
- Frame: the Queen says the light was always the people's (bread, candle, a name told to a child). The voice through the cracked pebble now runs "like a spring-vein under snow: felt, not heard". The sea "is learning her shore by heart", an early hint of 1450. She saw her great-grandson blinded through the amber bead that Sofija's envoy left on Vytautas's tomb (ch 2).

**Ch 26, May and 25 Jun 1447, Vilnius then Kraków (POV Kazimieras, 19).**

- The privilege is read in the Lower Castle by the old clerk **Mikalojus** (grandfather wrote for Algirdas, father for Vytautas). Clauses shown: no noble arrested or punished without a court; inherited lands not taken; offices not to foreigners; court rulings carried out without the duke's grace. "Pasitikėjimas pasenta. Raštas pasenta lėčiau." Clerk: the fire "knew whether he would hold"; now "vaškas nežino. Žino tik tas, kuris paskui skaito." Kazimieras signs; Goštautas seals. Goštautas privately says he counted the cup (eleven pounds of silver) and that he too once gave a coin away unmeasured (ch 17).
- Coronation at Wawel on 25 Jun 1447; the crown weighs less than the Vilnius cap. At the feast **Henė appears as a jester** (black and red, silent bells, empty juggling hands). Kazimieras, who has learned to look just behind a speaker, hears only scraps: "brolis... parodysiu... kur jis guli". He asks Sonka whether anyone spoke of Vladislovas; says aloud: "Jei kas turi žinių apie mano brolį, tegul parašo man laišką. Aš perskaitysiu." Henė sighs and goes; his steps down the stairs make no bell-sound. Kazimieras compares the Lithuanian and Polish privileges that night and finds two clauses that contradict each other.
- Henė is **finished as an actor**: stoneless, refused by keeper (ch 24) and unseen by Kazimieras (ch 26). Do **not** bring him back on stage except at most as an unseen presence in ch 30.

## End state for ch 27-30 (as I left it)

- **The keeper (Žiežirba):** age about 57 in 1447. He carries the horn lantern (lightning-fire, no names), the cracked pebble on a leather thong, Kęstutis's silver wolf, a knife. Since ch 24 he knows the truth of the Pact and the temptation of the "deal". He told Pilkoji nothing of the offer (not shown). He cannot recall his own name. He still leaves bread for the Iron Wolf at full moons (a habit, no longer expected to work).
- **Pilkoji:** alive, last *Vilkų Motina*; 76 in Jan 1441 (84 in 1449, as ch 28 has it). Three other wolves in 1441 (Uosis, Spyglys, Dobilas), all gone by 1449 (ch 28).
- **The last žynys:** the one-eyed beekeeper at Šiluva (wax in the right socket), ~71 in 1433. Already forgetting half the words in 1441 (Henė says so). Ch 27 has him die in Sep 1448; nothing in my chapters contradicts that.
- **The Amber Queen statue:** dull, cool, standing at Palanga shore; never moved; no glow since autumn 1445; fishing families still leave fish at her knees (the old rule: shore amber taken without an offering stays cold). Birutė's voice via the pebble is thin but works while the statue stands; the sea "is learning the shore". Palanga hooks if ch 29 wants them: **Rimgailė** (the fisherwoman who knelt in 1446; about 60 in 1450) with bread, her husband Dargis, old Gedvilė (the midwife), young Gaudas.
- **Casimir/Kazimieras:** crowned King of Poland 25 Jun 1447; the Lithuanian lords hold his privilege; two clauses in the privileges still contradict each other.

## Verify items

1. Date, venue and clause content of the **1447 privilege** (kept to "early May" and four paraphrased guarantees).
2. Kazimieras's proclamation in Vilnius on **29 Jun 1440**, its venue (cathedral then Lower Castle), and the role of Sonka and the Kraków council (invented: Sonka lets him go; Oleśnicki's letter comes too late).
3. **Goštautas's office** (kept to "ponas", no title in 1440/1447); **Kristinas Astikas** alive in 1440 (absent from ch 26 on purpose in case he died c. 1442); **Prussian Confederation** (14 Mar 1440); **Hanseatic office at Kaunas** (only "next year or the one after").
4. **Mykolas Žygimantaitis's flight**: three rumoured destinations left open.
5. **Vasily II blinded Feb 1446**: ch 25 puts the news in Palanga "per gavėnią". Varna 10 Nov 1444 ("praėjusį lapkritį"); Poles' wait for Vladislaus's body.
6. Sofija's amber bead on Vytautas's tomb (outline ch 2, "verify burial"): ch 25 frame leans on it ("prieš penkiolika metų"). Check ch 2 uses it.
7. **Casimir's remaining years**: "penkiasdešimt du" (d. 1492; born Nov 1427, so 12 in Jun 1440).
8. Oleśnicki's presence at the coronation feast and whether he'd be named "vyskupas Zbignevas" in 1447 (he became cardinal in 1449).
