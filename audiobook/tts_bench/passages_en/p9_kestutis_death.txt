— Do you recognise the gift, prince? — said the mercenary, aiming the chain at Kęstutis's neck. — Hanulas said it was worth your honour.

The chain tightened, and the light of Gabija's torch in the corner flickered and faded, giving way to the icy night of Praurimė's vaults. The world began to fade, turning to grey mist.

And then, at the very edge of his suffocating consciousness, Kęstutis saw Her.

She reached out a hand to him, and it smelled of resin.

— Come, my prince, — she said in a voice that sounded not like sound but like the roar of the sea. — The sea is already waiting for your soul. Your fight is over.

Kęstutis wanted to tell her that he could not leave. That the forest was still burning. That Vytautas had been left alone in this forest of kin-slayers. He needed at least one more moment, one more blow. But Birutė only gently shook her head, and in her gaze there was peace.

— He is going his own way, Kęstutis. Now he has only himself, his anger and his wisdom. That will help him more than your honour. The forest will look after its own wolf.

The chain closed for good. The last thought that struck against Kęstutis's head was about a dream. He saw a great oak falling in a field. The tree fell because the gods had decided that its roots had no place in this new land.
