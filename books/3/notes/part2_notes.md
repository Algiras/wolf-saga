# Book III, Part 2 (ch. 11–20): notes for other parts

## Dates and places used
- **Ch. 11** (late Jun 1407, Marienburg): Konrad died "in spring" (no date given); Ulrich elected on a Sunday (26 Jun) after a three-month interregnum. Konrad lies in St Anne's chapel. The Grand Master's hall beam of Samogitian oak creaks (Galminas hears it, Part 1 ch. 1).
- **Ch. 12** (20 May – Oct 1409, Samogitia): spark at an oak near **Ąžuolupiai** (invented village, Kražantė ford; "Naujas Ordino bokštas", not the Dubysa fort of 1401). Vytautas's riders come in early June; the Order's relief column dies on the old log bridge over the **Dubysa bog** in mid-July. Rūdis appears in Oct 1409 (declaration of war on Poland 6 Aug; truce "iki Rasos").
- **Ch. 13** (Dec 1409, Brest on the Bug): three days; plan = one united host, meeting at Červinskas (Czerwińsk) on the Vistula, then straight to Marienburg; winter feints (Samogitians from the east, Poles in Pomerania).
- **Ch. 14** (March 1410, Palanga): ice-break; three days after the ice broke.
- **Ch. 15** (30 Jun – 2 Jul 1410, Czerwińsk): the wolves reach the right bank on 30 Jun; the Polish host crosses the pontoon bridge; Jogaila crosses on 2 Jul and meets Vytautas at the bridgehead on the right bank (the Lithuanians themselves do not cross: record). Flame left Trakai about 22 days earlier (c. 8 Jun).
- **Ch. 16** (early Jul 1410): border woods (Mazovia/Prussia); Rūdis comes from the Order's camp at Kauernik on the Drwęca; "Gilgenburg road" is the one the Order's maps call impassable.
- **Ch. 17** (night 14–15 Jul 1410): the allied camp by the **burnt Gilgenburg** (not at Grunwald itself, which is a few hours' march; see deviations). Thunderstorm all night (record: rain); lightning splits an oak at the camp's edge before dusk.
- **Ch. 18** (morning 15 Jul 1410): field between Grunvaldas, Stembarkas and Liudvikavas; three Masses; heralds with two swords before noon.
- **Ch. 19, 20** (15 Jul 1410, midday–evening): alder bog beside **Lubienio ežeras** (ch. 23 of Part 3 uses the same place).

## Facts established (please respect)
**Ulrich (ch. 11, 19, 20).** About 47 in 1407, "apie penkiasdešimt" in 1410. White cloak always clean. Feels the Iron Saint as a **metal taste, furnace-heat and a hand on his shoulder**; in June 1407 the Marienburg firing chambers under the Great Refectory are cold stoves that radiate heat, an anvil-note rings beneath the castle, and a lantern flame bends towards him. He says nothing aloud to the Saint except "Aš čia." He welcomes the Riders of the Reise as "mūsų broliai" and wants the roads kept in memory. He pays Rūdis 20 Prussian marks for a written survey of all Reise roads, **including those the guides fear**. On the day, Ulrich himself orders a white cloak pinned on Rūdis. He is not shown speaking Lithuanian (Part 1 notes allow it). He is killed at the Polish line in his reserve charge (sixteen white banners), on foot, **no one knows whose hand**; in ch. 20 he looks at Vytautas from five steps, with weariness, not hatred.
**The Iron Saint (ch. 20).** Seen only by Vytautas, as a standing heaviness in the air a few paces behind Ulrich, much taller than a man, hands folded, head bowed; when Ulrich falls it does not move; the furnace heat of the whole day leaves at once; a high thin ringing like a splitting bell runs through the earth and "tūkstantis balsų, kurie staiga prisiminė, kad bijo" screams from every Reise road (heard in his right ear). **The surviving Order knights weep** (the Saint's gift: no pity, no tears) in the field. The lightning is a **silent white column**, a man's width, from a clear sky onto the place where Ulrich fell, for three breaths, then thunder (a Force's sign; no mortal working; it kills no one on the page).
**Rūdis (ch. 11, 12, 16, 19).** Sambian, 46 in 1410 (so b. c. 1364; the "jauniausias, kurį kiti vadino Rūdžiu" of Book I ch. 25, aged 20 in 1384). Order clerks write "Rudde". Thin, wind-dark, silver beard, the **throat scar** (the Order thinks "pagonių peilis, berniukas"). With the Order twenty years and a sworn wolf nineteen. He wrote the Order's road-maps for Ulrich in 1407 and marked the **Gilgenburg road "nepravažiuojamas. Pelkė. Reisių mirusieji"**; Ulrich "tiki juo kaip evangelija" (this is why the Order waits at Kauernik). He tells Rumbaudas "Tiems, kurie dar neprašė", Eglė "Jei neišlaikysiu iki pabaigos, paprašyk, kad broliai mane nuneštų į pelkę, net jei būsiu apsivilkęs jų spalvomis." In ch. 19, as the Marshal's guide, he follows **wolf trail-marks on birches** (three cuts, two slanted, one straight), leads **300 knights** of the left wing into the alder bog; he cannot tell whether the Lithuanian withdrawal was ordered (**left open**: "vilkai bėgo prieš lietuvius, ne paskui"). The Root-Mother's roots take the knights, the 400 Samogitians finish them; a Samogitian youth (about 20, unnamed, with an axe) kills Rūdis, who shouts "aš savas" and wears the white Order cloak; the youth says "Dėkui, Perkūne" and never knows. Rūdis hears the **chorus of the Iron Wolf's pack** at the roots before the bog closes (Part 3 ch. 21: Pilkoji recovers the body in its cloak).
**Rumbaudas Valimantaitis (ch. 12).** Invented details around the name: 52 in 1409; elder of Ąžuolupiai 22 years; baptised "Mikalojus" at Salynas; wife **Gedutė**, eldest son **Skirmantas** (hostage in Königsberg since 1400, aged 10 then); he kills the Order bailiff **Gotfridas** (a decent man) with an axe at the felling of the oak; the young priest **Albertas** dies of "the place". Rūdis reports (Oct 1409) that Skirmantas is alive: "Ulrichas neliepė jų liesti; su vaikais Ordinas nekariauja". Vytautas's letter ends: **"Daugiau neparduosiu"**; nobody believes it. He sends 300 horses and 20 wagons of arms; Kirmėlė comes with the riders.
**Galminas (ch. 12, 16, 19).** At the bog bridge (Jul 1409) he walks alone onto the log bridge under 30+30 bolts, which do not stop him (green sap), and the Root-Mother takes **73 Order men and 12 Samogitians** (Jonas of Šaukėnai among them); a young oak grows where the bridge began and keeps its leaves into autumn. The Order's knights **stand and call their unit numbers** rather than flee. Bark creeps from his collar to his jaw (Jul 1410) and "kopia jam iki žandikaulio" by ch. 16; he does not remember his tattoos' meaning; says "Vilką galima nuplauti, ir jis liks vilku", will not go to Vytautas, marches beside him with **400 Samogitians** (hidden in the alders). In ch. 19 he stands at the bog edge as a **bark-skinned giant, branch-fingers, two green eyes** (the transformation); the **fall into the grove** is Part 3 ch. 23.
**The Medvėgalis žynys (ch. 17, 20).** Still unnamed. 23 years of the flame leaning east. At the oath he is the officiant (left hand on the sword hilt, right hand on the earth); **cost: all his teeth** loosen and he spits them into his clenched fist ("Rytoj užteks visko, ko man liko"). He dies on his cart by the bog at the moment of the lightning ("dabar", his pot-flame goes out) as Žiežirba reports in ch. 20 (Part 3 ch. 21 matches).
**The oath (ch. 17).** Under a lightning-split oak, on Kęstutis's sword with Vytautas's blood on the *perkūnėlis*. Wording: *Perkūne, tu pažymėjai mane Krėvoje... Daug kartų sulaužiau tau žodį... Prisiekiu savo vardu, kuris yra tavo. **Nė vieno žingsnio atgal, iš kurio negrįšiu. Nė karto nenusuksiu nugaros savo pusbroliui, kol šis laukas nepasakys, kas jame stovi. Ir sumokėsiu, ką laukas paims.** Jei sulaužysiu, imk vardą. Jei tesėsiu, laikyk savąjį. Tavo ženklas ant manęs. Mano žodis ant tavęs.* The žynys states the rule: swearing by a name the Force has marked binds the Force ("Ne tik tave"). The **"iš kurio negrįšiu"** clause is why the retreat can be *feigned* (he returns) and why the Order's sword planted at his feet (ch. 18) is "Atsiminsiu, kur". The rust "nustojo plisti"; the thin bright line appears in ch. 20 after the lightning. **Henė** watched from the edge of the lantern light, did not enter ("Aš nesikišu... vyno pilstytojas"); the žynys read his smile as perhaps envy. Seen by: the žynys (the only mortal who understood the flame), Vytautas, Žiežirba, Kirmėlė at a distance.
**The flame (ch. 15, 17, 20).** It left Trakai about 8 Jun 1410 in an iron-framed **horn lantern**, lit from a sliver of oak charcoal on a bed of Kalvis's ash; Spyglys's hands shake and he stays behind; Elijas stays at the empty door ("Kas nors turi stovėti prie durų, už kurių nieko nėra"); the black ring on the shelf goes cold for the first time in 23 years. Žiežirba carries it in a sheepskin among the wolves; it runs with the wolves in ch. 19–20 and survives. At the bridge it turns blue then frost-white near Jogaila (White Silence); Žiežirba **loses the shape of his mother's lips** (he never knew his name: Part 1) until Kalvis's ash revives it. In the field the flame is "gyva" at the end (ch. 20).
**Pebble.** Cold, "nebe miręs" after the Amber Queen at Palanga (ch. 14); **warm at the oath (ch. 17)**; **burning at the lightning (ch. 20)** and then *remembering for him*: the faces of Ivanas and Jurgis (the boy asking if one can drown without noticing), Kęstutis looking back over his shoulder, Birutė, young Ona laughing ("vyras per daug galvoja"). Left ear dead after the thunder (Part 3 uses it).
**The Amber Queen (ch. 14).** The statue is life-size, head bowed, skirts of amber like water, insects and a winged seed inside, **faceless**. An old fisher-woman sweeps the sand around it each dawn, **a priest from Memel** tried six years ago to set a cross on the stone and the cross fell the other way four times; the Memel cog turned back in a fog. She speaks to Vytautas (through the cold pebble, "Vyti") after 26 years: she is memory, not prophecy; price of the victory: "Mokės miškas... Mokės jos mergaitė su gyvate... Mokės senis su ugnimi ant kalvos... Ir tu sumokėsi, bet ne tuo, kuo tikiesi. Tuo, kas sušils." She names **Markvardas** (the toast at Marienburg, ch. 6 of Book II; "Aš to neprašau. Aš tik prisimenu"); and on Jogaila: "Tu neatleidai. Aš taip pat ne... Prisimink ir stovėk šalia. Vienas be kito jūs sudužsite." Her cost: the statue **pales**, one lighter streak down the amber like a tear-track.
**Jogaila (ch. 13, 15, 18, 20).** Sound dies within ten paces of him; in Brest only Henė's voice reaches him whole. Frost forms on windows, on candles at the Mass (Zawisza sees frost on the altar in July), a frost path under his steps. Speaks Lithuanian "pusbroli". He **cannot look at the silver wolf**; at Brest he sits next to Vytautas and starts "Aš..." and is stopped with "Ne" (Vytautas: "Aš tau neatleidžiu... Man reikia tavo vyrų, tavo ietininkų ir tavo žodžio, kad nepasitrauksi"). He gives one of the two Order swords to Vytautas by Zawisza: "Pasakyk, kad nebelaukiu... daugiau jo nesulaikau." In ch. 20 he **holds Vytautas's gaze** from a few paces and they nod: no words about Krėva (Part 3 ch. 24 is their first conversation of it).
**Vytautas.** Needs lips and the right ear; **cannot read** (he recites Sofija's letter from memory); beyond a few steps sees masses and movement, not faces. In ch. 20 **Gedvydas** (35, Samogitian guard from the Vorskla flight, Book II) narrates the king's rescue into his right ear. Carries no new wounds. He plants the Order's sword (ch. 18) "tegul čia, atsiminsiu, kur" and rides back to it (ch. 20) when he decides to return. The wing's withdrawal is deliberately **neither confirmed nor denied** (chroniclers will differ; he laughs and says nothing). Sofija's letter: "Aš prisiekiau jam tai kaip duktė, o ne kaip žmona" (Moscow will not move while he is at war); Vytautas: "Ji prisiekė savo vyrui. Ne man."
**Henė.** Two stones, golden chain on his neck (not remarked by Jogaila), serves wine to both cousins at Brest ("Abiem tas pats... Tik jūs ne visada gėrėte"); to Jogaila alone: "Aš kalbu abiem vienodai, didenybe. Tik jūs vienintelis girdite, kad aš tai sakau." Stones fly in the same track "kaip du žirgai prie vieno vežimo".
**Oleśnicki.** First seen at Brest as the youngest chancery secretary, 20, big nose, ink-black fingers; at the Grunwald Masses with the royal seal-case, counting; in ch. 20 he takes a broken spear off the ground, holds it wrongly, blocks the German knight's horse (which falls); coins spill from the knight's slashed purse (Part 3 ch. 30 takes the warm coin).
**Zawisza Czarny (ch. 18).** 31, black armour, no blazon, tournament fame (Prague, Buda, Paris); decent, devout, ironical; believes the frost of the royal tent is the dead queen Jadwiga praying; carries the second sword to Vytautas and notes his ash-grey hand, the rust-brown sword, the silver wolf and a **warm light through the pebble pouch**. Vytautas: "Mačiau tave Budoje. Prieš dešimt metų."
**Wolves at Grunwald (ch. 18–20).** 23 go in; run on foot ahead of the horse (geležinė valanda); 16 return, 7 stay in the bog; Kirmėlė and Pilkoji named alive. The Samogitian youth who killed Rūdis is among Galminas's 400.
**Smolensk banners:** three, hold in the centre until Vytautas returns; "kelios dešimtys vyrų" left (ch. 20).

## Magic ledger (Part 2)
- Costs on the page: **Root-Mother** (ch. 12, 19): 73 + 12 dead; Galminas's bark; Rūdis (life). **Amber Queen** (ch. 14): the statue pales, her voice will be quieter. **Flame/Žiežirba** (ch. 15): the memory of his mother's lips for a moment. **Eglė** (ch. 16): translucent to the second joint, forgets to name birds, žalčiai halved. **Žynys** (ch. 17): teeth; life. **Vytautas** (ch. 17, 20): blood, left ear, the flood of memory.
- Scale: the oath is **Priesaika/Claim** (asked power bound by the Force's nature), not a mortal working; the lightning, the bog harvest, the Iron Saint's break are **Forces**; the žynys's "castle-night" ceiling is not exceeded. The Saint's break is deniable as a **tactical** event (Order's cohesion collapses when its commander falls).
- Pebble: cold (ch. 11–14) → warm (ch. 17) → burning (ch. 20) → warm (ch. 21 onwards). Henė's stones: two. Tier 4: 1 → 0 (ch. 20). Unbinding 3.

## Deviations from the outline (and why)
- **Ch. 11:** the title is **"Marienburgo Šešėliai"** (outline: "Malborko"), following Book II's seam pass (Book II ch. 30 is "Marienburgo Žemėlapis"); the file slug keeps `malborko`.
- **Ch. 12:** the dateline runs **May – October 1409**: the outline's truce to midsummer 1410 dates from 8 Oct 1409, so Rūdis brings the news in October. The Marienburg scene is **reported** by Rūdis (the declaration of war and Ulrich's refusal to touch the hostages), not shown; Rumbaudas is a local elder, not an Order court figure. The names of the 1409 leaders are left vague (outline: verify).
- **Ch. 15:** the record has the Lithuanian host meeting the Poles on the **right bank** at Czerwińsk after the Poles crossed; the wolves therefore watch the crossing and the cousins meet at the bridgehead (the Lithuanians do not cross).
- **Ch. 17:** the camp is by **burnt Gilgenburg**, not literally "by Grunwald" (the allied army marched a few hours on the morning of 15 Jul). The nameless Šatrija žynys of Part 1 does not appear.
- **Ch. 18:** the outline says Jogaila hears "Mass after Mass" and the Lithuanian light horse and Tatars charge first; shown from Zawisza (Polish) with **one sword carried by him to Vytautas**, who plants it. Banner counts are avoided (outline: verify Długosz).
- **Ch. 19:** the "retreat?" is shown with a pre-planned wolf trail through the bog (so the wolves "lead pursuing knights into the woods and bogs") but the narration does **not** say whether the wing's flight was feigned; the Order's pursuers are about 300 of the Marshal's banners (their commander unnamed); Marquard von Salzbach is not named on the field (Part 3 ch. 21 handles his capture).
- **Ch. 20:** the Polish line, the king's rescue and Ulrich's fall follow the record; the **lightning column** and the **knights' tears** are Saga. The sword's bright line (Part 3 ch. 28) first appears here.
- **Word counts** run 1,700–2,500 (ch. 13 and 14 near the lower end).

## Canon questions
- **Henė's stones** in Part 1 ch. 3 (Jan 1401) read as three; canon (D2, Book II) has two from Skirgaila's death in 1397. Ch. 13 treats two as normal ("Anksčiau jų buvo trys" is a memory of before 1397).
- Whether a Samogitian leader named **Rumbaudas Valimantaitis** is attested for 1409 (the saga uses the outline's POV name).
- **"Zbignevas Olesnickis"** (table name) vs **"Zbignevas iš Olesnicos"** (used in ch. 13); the latter reads better as a first mention.
- Zawisza's presence with the royal household at Grunwald is a plausible placement, not a recorded one (verify).
- The lightning column "stands" and the weeping knights are **not** a mortal working; if a later book needs to be stricter about "magic never wins the battle", note that Ulrich's death and the Order's collapse are shown as men's deeds first.
