# Book V, ch. 27-30: notes (end state, series close)

Files: `27_paskutine_zynio_giesme.qmd` (~1,840 w), `28_pilkoji_atsigula.qmd` (~2,040 w), `29_vardas_prie_ugnies.qmd` (~2,820 w), `30_epilogas.qmd` (~1,500 w).

## What each chapter established

**Ch 27 (Sep 1448, Šiluva stumps; Žiežirba, 58).** 23 hives on stumps. The one-eyed beekeeper-žynys (~86) dies on a sheepskin at the fifth hive, singing the Silent Pact song. He has been "no longer a žynys" since he gave away the words in autumn 1433 (ch 11): "Žynys miršta kartu su žodžiais. Dainininkas gali mirti ir be jų." Count: eight when the keeper came, six with him now, **five after him** (Šeduva woman d. 1447, the old Latvian-side one d. 1446; the five left are scattered: Dubysa, Dzūkija forest, a woman at the Nemunas, two unknown; none knows another). Song (wrong details): the whole people kneels and swears; "niekas jos žodžių neištarė, broliai nusuko akis"; "amžinai, kol ugnis dega". Truth he carries from ch 5: elder brother hesitated; the younger spoke the fire's clause aloud; Algirdas never forgave; the people were never asked. The keeper corrects nothing: asked "ar teisingai?" he answers "ji dainuojama taip, kaip jai reikėjo būti dainuojama" (the old man: "pirmas teisingas atsakymas, kurį iš vilko girdžiu"). He hums the missing ending in the old man's version. The song stops at its silent gap; the death falls in the silence. The keeper tells the bees (custom), buries him at the sixth hive under a live stump (earth, not bog, not fire); bees settle on his dead left arm (dead to the elbow since the Unburned winter, ch 16): he feels the weight. Thesis line: "Tiksli daina būtų mirusi su kunigaikščiais. Ši turėjo klaidų, už kurių galima laikytis."

**Ch 28 (2 Nov 1449, Vėlinės, oldest bog; Žiežirba).** Echo of Book III ch 7 (Pilkoji lays a Vėlinės table; four places, the fourth for **Uosis**, who died last winter, found first by a magpie, last of the old wolves). Pilkoji is 84, "šaknis". Carries the Iron Wolf's skull in both arms; the keeper lights the way. On the kūlgrinda she names the dead in the bog: Spyglys, Šiurpas, Šermukšnis, Dobilas, Kirmėlė (Dobilas "vieną rudenį nebepabudo", between 1441 and 1446, left undated). She runs the nine Words with him; at the ninth ("Gauja atsiliepia ugniai, ne žmogui") she releases the pack: "Tada gauja nebeatsiliepia niekam." She whispers all the names she carries (the dead's, her husband, mother, daughter, Eglė's four from ch 1) into the **cracked pebble** against his chest while he covers his ears (rule 4 kept by choice); not rendered. She tells him the folk tale (a wolf whose name someone remembers can come home: bread on a knife, name at a fire); she does not believe it; "Aš jau žinau, kur grįžtu." She lies down with the skull on her chest in her wolf-hide; he presses her under with both hands (the krikštas reversed); she does not struggle; he does not see her rise. Dawn: the chorus at his throat-scar (the Iron Wolf's pack) falls silent, last voice Pilkoji's. The **Root-Mother** returns to the abandoned groves: Šiluva stump (the beekeeper's) puts out a green shoot in November, thirty-fifty stumps with it; "po penkiasdešimties metų žmonės sakys, kad miškas sugrįžo pats, ir bus teisūs". He **leaves the lightning-fire lantern** burning on the Vėlinės stump beside the four cups ("Čia tamsu"). He keeps pebble (now warm, "kaip delnas, kuris ką tik paleido": the Queen calling), silver wolf, knife, a crust. Last sentence: he is the last wolf.

**Ch 29 (12 Dec 1449 – early Mar 1450, Palanga; frame Birutė, plain first person like ch 1, her voice in italics inside the scene).** Opens "Vanduo pasiekė pečius, kaip žadėjau" (ch 1: knees, "pasakysiu paskutinį dalyką, kai vanduo pasieks pečius"); closes "Vanduo siekia smakrą. / Tai paskutinis dalykas, kurį atsimenu." He walks five weeks to Palanga (never saw the sea). On the solstice night (calendar has drifted from Kūčios by 12 days; Praurimė's zenith, so Birutė's cost is **halved**) he lights an ordinary **driftwood fire** (first fire of his own in 44 years; salt makes it green-blue). The Queen speaks through the pebble (her last): she kept one name of those the sea carried (ch 1, "Vieną aš pasilikau"), because Vytautas's hand rose to his chest when it was spoken (Book III ch 9). She states the cost truthfully: all gifts, the part left in the bog stays, the sworn years fall in one winter, he will die a man. **Rimgailė** (greyed, ~60) comes with bread on a (smith's iron) kitchen knife-point; the name is spoken (never written: "kaip kvėpavimas po šalčio, kaip lašas ... ant akmens", as Book III ch 9); his mother's face returns whole (round, scar on chin, laughing); he takes the bread with his lips; the iron burns; **his blood is red**. After: night-sight gone; first warmth of fire; he sleeps under a lit roof (Dargis's hut) for a day; hair white in three days; cold, illness; "Kaip kad kažkas prisimena, jog tu esi." **Kūčios**: he says the skin-names at the table (Pušis, Šešiapirštis, Kalvis, Jonas, Kirmėlė, Rūdis, Spyglys, Šiurpas, Šermukšnis, Dobilas, Uosis, Pilkoji) and "Vilkų kunigaikštis, Vytautas" (echo of ch 7's fifteenth bowl), then a place for his mother (name unknown; "Žiežirba" was her pet name: she put a coal on a clay shard: "štai tau žiežirba, saugok"); **first tears** ("Tau tekėjo vanduo" says the granddaughter Gedvilė, 8). Evenings he tells stories to village children and then parents; the **statue starts to glow weakly** again because people remember (ch 25: "ją reikia prisiminti"); Dargis stops lighting the candle. He asks to be **burned** (no wolf ever was), gives the silver wolf to Rimgailė ("Duona už duoną"; she hides it under the threshold). Dies early March before dawn, last sound his own name (Birutė keeps it). Pyre of driftwood beside the statue; the amber pebble on his chest burns with pine-resin smell (echo of the Book I prologue); the smoke does **not** hesitate (contrast ch 1): body and name rise together; "nuo to ryto žmonės sakė, kad kelias yra". Evening: ice breaks, sea rises to Birutė's shoulders. Coda: bead on Vytautas's tomb in Vilnius is warmest; blinded great-grandson in Moscow; Henė did not come (nothing to bring); Gedvilė will sweep the stone after the water goes. Birutė did **not** write the name.

**Ch 30 (Christmas night 1553, farmstead near Palanga; the grandfather, 94, b. ~1459).** His mother is **Gedvilė** (the girl of ch 29, b. ~1442) and her grandmother Rimgailė. He wears the silver wolf under his shirt; a piece of warm amber sits on the sill. He recites the keeper's wolves' skin-names at Kūčios as "svečiai" (family litany; nobody knows who they are). Boy **Jurgutis** (8). The grandfather remembers 1466 (age 7; the Order a Polish vassal; "Taip? Ir kas dabar?"), 1529 (age 70; the clerk reads the Statute: whose forest, whose bee-tree; the Counter under the floor), timber carts to Danzig, the forest "ceased to be dusk". **Vilkaitis** (descendant of half-made wolves, thin white line at the throat) drinks the Christmas beer in the cellar and hums the Pact song wrongly ("tylėk, tylėk, Tylioji Sutartie" the only intact line). Omniscient aside: Olaus Magnus (ex-archbishop of Uppsala, exiled in Rome; werewolves in Prussia/Livonia/Lithuania drink Christmas beer); Henė (black and red, empty hands) unseen on the window-frame. Bread crumb into the fire ("Duona už duoną"). Last line exactly: "Kai miškai buvo žali, jis buvo. Dabar jis tik tavo sapnuose."

## Outline threads A-J: how each closed

- **A, Silent Pact:** a song sung wrong (ch 27), kept as folk custom (bread to the fire, empty places at Kūčios, "Tylėk, tylėk" hummed drunk, ch 30). The true oath survives only in the pebble/sea; the keeper chooses not to correct it. "The Pact was the people's, and its last keeping is memory" is the keeper's insight in ch 27.
- **B, Last Flame:** out in ch 1; relighting fails in ch 20; the lightning-fire (no names) is left burning at Pilkoji's Vėlinės table (ch 28); the keeper is named by an ordinary driftwood fire (ch 29); the first crumb to the hearth is all that is left of the flame (ch 30).
- **C, Amber Queen:** dims 1445 (ch 25); speaks his name through the pebble and spends her last (ch 29); the statue glows once more with remembering, then the sea takes it; she says "Tai paskutinis dalykas, kurį atsimenu" (frame closes). Sweeping of the stone continues in Gedvilė's line (ch 29, 30).
- **D, Henė:** done on stage at ch 24 and 26; ch 29 says only that he did not come ("nėra ko atnešti"); ch 30 shows him unseen, empty-handed, watching a book being written.
- **E, Perkūnas:** spent; his gift is the stoneless, nameless lightning-fire left in the bog (ch 28). No thunder at the pyre.
- **F, Root-Mother / Eglė:** abandoned groves put out shoots after Pilkoji lies down (ch 28; Šiluva stump); the forest later cut for Danzig timber and "no longer dusk" (ch 30). Eglė is not shown (ch 1/Book IV closed her).
- **G, Iron Wolves:** Pilkoji (last Vilkų Motina) goes into the bog with the skull (ch 28); the keeper is the last sworn wolf for five weeks, then a man, and is the only wolf ever burned (ch 29). The pack's Word ends (ninth rule dissolved). Echo: half-made wolves drinking Christmas beer (ch 30); the silver wolf as a family heirloom.
- **H, cousins' blood-debt:** closed in ch 21-26 (others). In my chapters only as the oath told wrong (ch 27) and the Amber Debt as the unspoken clause; not re-opened.
- **I, Hostages and heirs:** Sofija's bead on Vytautas's tomb warms; blinded Vasily II in Moscow, his mother old (ch 29 coda).
- **J, Vulture gods:** the Order a Polish vassal (1466), the Statute of 1529 (ledger, the Counter under the floor), timber for Danzig; no gods, only institutions (ch 30).

## Verify

1. **Wolves' dead list** (ch 28, 29, 30): Kirmėlė, Spyglys, Šiurpas, Šermukšnis (d. ch 16), Dobilas (d. 1441-46, undated), Uosis (d. winter 1448-49). If other chapters have a different death order, change only the list order; all are dead by Vėlinės 1449 except Pilkoji. Check Jonas, Rūdis, Kalvis, Pušis, Šešiapirštis are dead by 1430 per earlier books.
2. **Pilkoji's age** 84 (ch 28), per ch 21/24.
3. **Žiežirba's left arm**: dead to the elbow since ch 16 (ch 27 uses this; the two-fingers numbness from ch 13 was dropped).
4. **The unwritten true name.** Ch 29 never gives it. If ch 1's first draft or ch 20 renders a name, correct ch 29.
5. **Sworn years**: sworn Kūčios 1405, so "keturiasdešimt ketveri" at Dec 1449 (the outline says 45; ch 1 says 25 in 1430).
6. **Rimgailė's age** ~60 (ch 25 notes); granddaughter Gedvilė 8 invented; Dargis, Gaudas, midwife Gedvilė (older) from ch 25. Ch 30 makes the grandfather's mother this young Gedvilė (b. ~1442, son b. ~1459).
7. **Grandfather aged 94 in 1553** is a stretch to give him 1466 as a boy of seven. If a younger narrator is wanted, drop "septynerių" and have him hear of 1466 from his mother.
8. **Olaus Magnus**: described as ex-archbishop of Uppsala in exile in Rome (outline says "Swedish bishop"); book printed Rome 1555. Check wording.
9. **First Statute 1529** and bee-tree/forest-ownership clauses (kept vague).
10. Summer solstice/Kūčios drift: used "gruodžio dvylikta" as the longest night (`calendar_and_festivals.md`: Praurimė zenith, cost halved).
11. Ch 7 must show the vision of the Pact arriving when the keeper first holds the pebble (ch 5 notes); ch 27 says he first looked into it 17 years before (1431).
