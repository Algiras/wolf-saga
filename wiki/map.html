The Map

The Lands of the Iron Wolf, in Lithuanian Geležinio Vilko žemės: Lithuania, the Baltic and her neighbours, 1341–1450. The map is drawn by code (scripts/make_map.py) from public-domain Natural Earth coastlines and rivers; the parchment, border and sea serpent are painted art. The main map names only the places a reader needs at a glance. The two insets below show the saga’s closest country at a larger scale, and the table lists every place with its wiki page.

Inset 1: Vilnius, Trakai and Kreva, 1382

The camp between the lakes and the pines where Kęstutis and Vytautas were taken on 3 August 1382 (Jogaila’s camp, the road to it), the ride that led there (1), and Vytautas’s flight from Krėva in the autumn (2). The camp and the pine hill are the saga’s places: their exact spots are not in the record. Download (SVG).

Inset 2: Prussia and the road to Grunwald, 1410

Jogaila’s march from Kraków over the pontoon bridge at Czerwińsk to Grunwald (3), and the road the Order’s Reise took from Königsberg up the Nemunas to Vilnius (4). Download (SVG).

How to read it

  • Signs: a castle is a fortified place; a small circle a town; crossed swords a battlefield; a diamond a place that exists only in the saga or whose spot is uncertain; a few small trees mark the great forests, where the Dykra lies between the Nemunas and the Order.
  • Colours: the soft washes show the lordships about 1430 (Grand Duchy of Lithuania in gold, the Order’s Prussia in grey-blue, Livonia in violet, the Kingdom of Poland and Masovia in red, Moscow in green). They are not borders: the frontiers moved every decade.
  • The dashed line on the main map joins the two journeys of 1382, Kęstutis’s ride and Vytautas’s escape. The other journeys are on the insets. The ride’s course is the saga’s own (the sources give only its start and end); Vytautas’s route follows the record: Krėva, Masovia, then the Order.
  • Accuracy: coasts, rivers and lakes are Natural Earth data at 1:10 million. Places carry real coordinates; those marked below are approximate or stylised.
