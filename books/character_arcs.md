# Character Arcs: Protagonist, Antagonists, Transformations

*Companion to `series_arc.md`. The series has **one protagonist across Books I–IV: Vytautas.** Each book gives him a **mortal antagonist** he must defeat (or be defeated by) and a **mythic antagonist** working behind them. Book V passes the torch (§4).*

---

## 1. The protagonist: Vytautas (c. 1350–1430)

### 1.1 The through-line

**The lie he starts with:** *"Honour and blood will keep us safe."* (inherited from Kęstutis)
**The lie he adopts after Krėva:** *"Only cunning survives; trust is weakness."* (the Wolf)
**The truth he finally earns:** *"Cunning is a tool, faith is a choice, and some fires must be kept even at the cost of a crown."*

He is not a hero who becomes good. He is a good man who becomes **effective**, pays for it in people, and in the end chooses **one** thing to stay faithful to.

### 1.2 The seven forms (his "morph")

Each form gets a name the narrative can use, a **visible tell** (body, objects, speech) and a **trigger** that ends it.

| # | Form | Book / chapters | Want | Visible tells | Trigger that ends it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sūnus** (the Son) | I, ch. 1–9 | Protect his father; be proven right | Kęstutis's sword; talks too much; sees wolves everywhere | Kęstutis strangled (ch. 9) |
| 2 | **Išgyventojas** (the Survivor) | I, ch. 10–17 | Live | Shaved beard, cut hair, the maid's dress; silent; animal | Kneels at the Order's border (ch. 17) |
| 3 | **Įrankis** (the Tool) | I, ch. 18–22 | Revenge through borrowed strength | The Order's sword; white cloak; the dead amber; speaks German | The burned village and his friend's death (ch. 20–21) |
| 4 | **Išdavikų Išdavikas** (Traitor of Traitors) | I, ch. 23–30 → II, Part 1 | Freedom from every master | Burned Teutonic armour; father's amulet shown openly; the warm amber | His sons poisoned (II, 1392) |
| 5 | **Didysis Kunigaikštis** (the Grand Duke / would-be emperor) | II, Parts 2–3 | Empire: an east that makes him equal to kings | Ruthenian robes; the Tatar court; Henė at his side; the "golden" period | Vorskla (II, 1399) |
| 6 | **Geležinis Vilkas** (the Iron Wolf) | III → IV, Part 1 | Finish the Order; protect Samogitia | Plain armour again; the vilkatai at his back; the lantern flame on campaign | Victory at Grunwald that changes nothing for the gods (III) |
| 7 | **Ugnies Sargas** (Keeper of the Flame) | IV | A crown, **or** the flame. He learns he cannot have both | Old, half-deaf (five-voice costs), the secret vault | Chooses the flame; dies uncrowned (IV end) |

### 1.3 Arc beats per book (want / need / lie / truth)

| Book | Want (external) | Need (internal) | Lie believed | Truth learned | Costs paid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | Avenge his father; get back Trakai | Survive without becoming his enemies | "Trust is weakness" | Even the Wolf needs a pack, and a mother's love has a price that someone else pays | His honour (the dress), his father, his gods (the false baptism), his mother (surety paid), Trakai (to Skirgaila) |
| **II** | Rule; be a king among kings | Know when to stop | "The horizon belongs to the bold" | Empire eats its builder (the Vorskla) | **His sons**, 20 princes, his surety-free body (full cost of every working) |
| **III** | Break the Order | Reconcile with Jogaila, the man who killed his father | "I can do it alone" | Victory requires the cousin he hates | Pride; Samogitian blood; the Root-Mother's last strength |
| **IV** | The crown that would make Lithuania sovereign | Decide what he is faithful to | "Legitimacy comes from Rome or Kraków" | It comes from what you keep | The crown itself; Ona (1418); his life |

### 1.4 What keeps him sympathetic while he does terrible things

- **He always pays personally** for every betrayal (rule from `series_arc.md` §3.2).
- **He keeps one promise** across forty-three years (the flame), which the reader knows about and no one in-world does.
- **He saves Vilnius twice** (I ch. 24 and II 1390, where this time he is besieging it, and he has to live with that).
- **Ona sees through him.** Their marriage is the book's moral mirror. When she stops looking at him (II, after the sons), the reader feels it.

## 2. Antagonists by book

Each book needs **one mortal antagonist with a face**, **one mythic antagonist** working behind them, and a clear **resolution**: defeated, escaped, or victorious.

| Book | Mortal antagonist | Why they're compelling | Mythic antagonist | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Skirgaila** (the hands) with **Jogaila** (the will) | Skirgaila does the dirty work and is never thanked; Jogaila believes he is saving the realm | **Šešėlių Pardavėjas** (Henė) | **Partial victory, then a deal.** Vytautas outwits Skirgaila (the escape) and turns the Order against itself, but must make a truce with Jogaila. Skirgaila keeps Trakai: **the villain wins the land**, setting up Book II. |
| | *Secondary:* **Konrad Zöllner von Rotenstein** and the Order | Sincere, patient, sees Vytautas as a chess piece | **Geležinis Šventasis** | **Defeated by betrayal** (the July 1384 burnings) |
| **II** | **Skirgaila** (Part 1) → **Edigu** (Parts 2–3) | Skirgaila, Henė-ridden and drunk on Trakai; Edigu, the steppe's cold genius, Vytautas's equal and opposite | **Stepių Alkis** (*Erdene-Chuluu*) + Henė's vision | **Skirgaila defeated** (Ostrów 1392; dies 1397, and Vytautas is suspected). **Edigu WINS** (the Vorskla). *The book the protagonist loses.* |
| **III** | **Ulrich von Jungingen** | Brave, proud, convinced; the last true believer of the knightly Order | **Geležinis Šventasis** (in person on the field) | **Defeated and killed** at Grunwald, 15 Jul 1410 (the series' great victory) |
| | *Secondary:* the Samogitian collaborators and Order commanders | — | **Baltoji Tyla** (Jadwiga's anchor) | Holds; foreshadows IV |
| **IV** | **Zbigniew Oleśnicki** (Bishop of Kraków) | Brilliant, devout, utterly sure Lithuania must not be a kingdom; a statesman, not a cartoon | **Aukso Rijūnas** + **Henė's** last bargain | **Antagonist WINS** (the crown embassy blocked), but **Vytautas wins the inner battle**: refuses Henė, keeps the flame, dies free. |
| | *Ally-turned-temptation:* **Sigismund of Luxembourg** | Offers the crown for his own reasons | Henė (the jester at his court) | Withdraws |
| **V** | **Žygimantas Kęstutaitis** vs **Švitrigaila** (both antagonists to the new protagonist) | Vytautas's own brother turns tyrant; Jogaila's brother, the wild East | **The Unbinding itself** (the unfed dead) + Henė's final unmasking | Both fall (Pabaiskas 1435; Žygimantas assassinated 1440); the realm survives as an ordinary state |

### 2.1 The overarching antagonist: Henė / the Shadow-Seller

- He is the **only** antagonist present in every book. He **never wins by force** and **never loses by force**; he is beaten only by **refusal** or **cleverness**.
- **His score across the series:** Jogaila (bought, Book I); Skirgaila (bought, collected 1397); Vytautas (half-bought at the Vorskla; **refuses** at Lutsk). **The series climax is Vytautas's refusal in Book IV.**
- **Visible decline:** three stones (Book I) → two (after Skirgaila, Book II) → one (after Vytautas, Book IV) → none (Book V: he unmasks because there is no one left to deal with).

## 4. Book V's protagonist: passing the torch

Vytautas dies at the end of Book IV. Book V needs a **new point of view with a clear want**:

- **Recommended: the Flame-Keeper.** An invented **vilkatas** of the Iron Wolves *(Saga invention)*, a boy rescued during Book II's Unbinding hunts and raised in the Trakai vault as the flame's secret guard. **Want:** relight the flame after Vytautas's death. **Antagonists:** Žygimantas (who would use him) and the unbound dead. **Arc:** he learns the Pact cannot be re-sworn by one man, and lets the gods *become stories*. That ending is the series' last word on memory (the Law of Memory: "the land remembers").
- **Narrative frame:** Birutė (the Amber Queen) narrates his story as the **last thing she remembers before the sea takes her statue**, closing the loop opened by `00_pradzia.qmd`.

## 5. Book I revision checklist (character)

- [ ] Ch. 1–2: show Kęstutis's **1381 mercy** toward Jogaila, the tragic flaw that pays off at Krėva.
- [ ] Ch. 3: stage the **Sutartis** (blood, true name, surety, flame promise).
- [ ] Ch. 5 or 6: give **Skirgaila** a POV beat. He must be a person, not a fist.
- [ ] Ch. 8: the **interrogation**. Kęstutis refuses abdication; Vytautas learns his father's 1361 escape story.
- [ ] Ch. 10–17: mark Form 2's tells (silence, body, the dress) consistently.
- [ ] Ch. 18–19: Konrad Zöllner as a worthy, sincere opponent.
- [ ] Ch. 21: the friend (renamed from "Gediminas") must be set up earlier (ch. 2 or 6) so his death lands.
- [ ] Ch. 25: the band are **surviving Iron Wolves** from Kęstutis's guard; Jonas is their *vilkų vyresnysis*.
- [ ] Ch. 28: **Skirgaila gets Trakai**; Vytautas swallows it, and the reader feels Book II's fuse lit.
- [ ] Ch. 29: Vytautas **lights the secret flame** after the baptism. Final image of Form 4.
- [ ] Ch. 30: Henė juggles **three** stones and lets one wobble (Skirgaila's), a promise of Book II.
