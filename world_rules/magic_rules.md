# Magic Rules: Senovės Kalba, Claims, and Bargains

*Canonical mechanics. Where this file and `books/1/senoves_kalba_magic_system.md` (or appendix ch. 31) disagree, **this file wins**. The other doc remains the flavour reference for the elements' deities, boons and lineages. The metaphysics behind these rules is in `cosmology.md`.*

> **Design principle (the Sapkowski rule):** magic is a *trade*, not a superpower. It is rare, it costs something you can see on the page, and it never solves the political problem. It only buys time to face it. Every chapter that uses magic should show the bill.

---

## 1. Three ways the supernatural touches mortals

| Mode | Who initiates | Consent? | Bound by the Three Laws? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kalba** (asking) | The mortal | Yes | **Yes** | Birutė's fog; Vytautas's five-voice invocation in the swamp (ch. 15) |
| **Claim** (*Žymė*) | A god or Force | **No** | No (the Force's own nature binds it) | Perkūnas marking Vytautas at Krėva (ch. 10) |
| **Bargain** (*Sandėris*) | Either party | Yes, but rarely *informed* | No: bound only by the **letter** of the deal | The Shadow-Seller's deals with Jogaila and Skirgaila |

The **Three Laws** (Balance, Truth, Memory) bind *mortal craft*. Gods and Forces are not bound by the Law of Truth. **The Shadow-Seller can lie, deceive and create illusions; no mortal caster can.** That asymmetry is why bargaining with him is so dangerous.

---

## 2. Who can use Kalba

1. **Prigimtis (born):** the soul resonates with an element from birth. It usually runs down the **maternal** line (the Palanga amber priestesses; Birutė). Gifted children show it early: candle flames lean toward them, žalčiai come to their cradles.
2. **Pažymėtas (noticed):** a life event so large that an element *notices* the person: near-death, great sacrifice, a sacred deed. Usually grants **one** element, often temporarily.
3. **Surištas (bound):** someone *else* binds the person into a **Senovės Sutartis**. **This is Vytautas's path.** Birutė binds him in ch. 3, and Krėva and his mother's sacrifice later *deepen* the bond (they do not create it). This resolves the conflict with the old "key" wording.

The baptised **can** still use Kalba. The gods do not care about creed; they care about **truth** (§5.3).

## 3. How a working is cast

### 3.1 The formula
Every working names an **element**, a **recipient** and a **verb of giving**, in Old Lithuanian:

> **[Element] [tau / man] [verb].**
> *Žvaigždės tau švytės · Ugnis tau degs · Vanduo tau teks · Žemė tau laikys · Vėjas tau neš*

- **"tau"** (for you): cast on **another**. The **caster** pays.
- **"man"** (for me): cast on **oneself**. The caster pays, but the cost is **lighter** (no transfer loss).
- This is why Birutė says *tau* in ch. 3 and Vytautas says *man* in ch. 15. Both are correct.

### 3.2 Requirements
- **Contact with a sacred medium:** amber (any element, the universal medium), living fire (Gabija), running or sea water (Bangpūtys), bare earth (Žemyna), open air with wind (Vėjopatis), a starlit sky (Praurimė).
- **The words aloud.** A whisper is enough. Gagged casters cannot work; this is why the Order gags captured *žyniai*.
- **Intent that is true** (Law of Truth). A caster who asks for what they do not truly want gets nothing, or gets what they *do* truly want.

### 3.3 Timing: zeniths
| Element | Zenith | At zenith | Off zenith |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stars (Praurimė) | Kūčios, winter-solstice midnight | Cost **halved** | Cost **doubled** in daylight; impossible under overcast |
| Fire (Gabija) | Rasos, summer-solstice noon | Halved | Doubled in rain or floods |
| Water (Bangpūtys) | Full moon or high tide; strongest at the spring ice-break | Halved | Doubled away from living water |
| Earth (Žemyna) | Dagotuvės, autumn-equinox dusk | Halved | **Impossible** at sea or in the air |
| Wind (Vėjopatis) | Storms; Vėlinės | Halved | Doubled in caves or still air |

*Book I check:* Birutė's Praurimė vision in ch. 3 happens at **Dagotuvės**, off zenith, so it costs **double**. Show her fully blind afterwards (review M1/M11). Ch. 27's Bangpūtys working at the **full moon** is correctly timed.

### 3.4 Range
- With an **amber anchor** that the caster has touched and charged (Vytautas's pebble), **distance is free**: Birutė can reach Krėva from Palanga.
- Without an anchor, range is **line of sight**, and cost rises steeply beyond it.

## 4. Costs (the Law of Balance made concrete)

### 4.1 Per element
| Element | Minor working (a candle-length) | Major working (a night, an army, a life) |
|---|---|---|
| **Stars** (Clear Sight) | Hours of blindness | Blind for days, **or** the permanent loss of one beloved face from memory |
| **Fire** (Soul Armour, strength) | Exhaustion | **Soul-chill**: empathy gone for days; the caster can kill without feeling |
| **Water** (Life Spring, healing) | Raging thirst | The caster **takes the patient's wound** in part |
| **Earth** (Unmovable Will) | Stiffness | **Petrification** for the length of the effect; permanent loss of grace |
| **Wind** (Shadow Speed, far-speech) | Vertigo | **Loss of voice**: only whistling, for days, and permanently with repeated use |

### 4.2 The Five-Voice invocation (*Penkių Balsų Šauksmas*)
Speaking all five lines together (ch. 3, ch. 15) calls a **little of every boon** at once: sight, warmth, healing, endurance, concealment. It is a desperate act. **Cost: a little of every price**, permanent and cumulative: a dimming of sight and hearing, a fraying of memory (exactly what ch. 15 describes). It is the **only** permanent cost a minor working can carry. Each use leaves the caster a bit less *present*. By Book IV, an old Vytautas who has used it perhaps five times hears the world as if through water.

### 4.3 Surety: who pays when someone is bound
In a **Senovės Sutartis**, the binder may stand **surety** (*laidas*) for the bound: *"Jo skola — mano skola"* ("his debt is my debt"). **Birutė stands surety for Vytautas.** Consequences:
- Part of every price Vytautas incurs is **paid by Birutė**, wherever she is.
- This is why his costs look light in Book I and why Birutė **declines**: voice failing after the Krėva fog, sight going, until ch. 27, when she pays the **whole** remaining debt at once.
- After ch. 27 **there is no surety.** From Book II, Vytautas pays full price himself. The Vorskla flight has "no mother's fog, only shame" (Book II outline, ch. 18) *because* the surety is gone.

### 4.4 Unpaid debts
A cost cannot be refused, only deferred, and deferred costs **compound**: memory first, then health, then years of life, then madness, then death.

## 5. The Three Laws, clarified

### 5.1 Balance: "Kiek paimi, tiek atiduok"
See §4. Write the bill on the page.

### 5.2 Truth: "Tiesa galingesnė už bet kokį burtą"
- **Allowed:** hiding (real fog, real shadow), revealing, strengthening, healing, carrying messages, *showing possibilities*.
- **Forbidden:** making anyone perceive what is not there. No glamours, no false faces, no illusions. The Krėva fog is **real fog** (ch. 14 is correct; `mythic_gaps.md` §2's "distortion reflecting expectations" is wrong and is corrected there).
- **Corollary:** magic always reveals *some* truth, often unwanted. Every Praurimė vision shows the seer something about **herself**.

### 5.3 Memory: "Atmintis amžina"
- The land remembers every working done on it. Places of great working carry **residue** that seers feel (the Krėva cell; the Palanga stone).
- **Amber stores memory** (it is Saulė's grief). Amber that has carried a working **answers to the truth of whoever holds it.**

### 5.4 The amber barometer (canon rule)
Vytautas's pebble responds to the **truth of the bearer**, not to his faith:
| Chapter | State | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Warm, pulsing | Freely given in love |
| 8–9 | Faint light in the dark | He is true to his grief |
| 19 | **Dead, cold stone** | Baptised as a **lie** (he does not believe and does not intend to keep the oath) |
| 20 | Burning | Žemyna's price for the burned village |
| 23 | **Hot** | He chooses truly (betray the Order to save Vilnius) |
| 29 | **Glowing** | His 1387 baptism is honest *about its dishonesty*: he does it for his people, with clear eyes |

## 6. The Senovės Sutartis (the Ancient Pact rite)

**Four requirements, all on the page:**
1. **Amber** charged by the binder.
2. **The binder's blood** on the amber.
3. **The bound person's true name** (*slaptas vardas*), the secret name the mother whispers at birth, known only to her and the elements (see `languages_and_naming.md`).
4. **An unbreakable promise** exchanged both ways.

**Canon for ch. 3 (to be written into the revision):**
- Birutė cuts her palm and presses the pebble into Vytautas's hand, so her blood is on the amber.
- She speaks his true name, which he has never heard. This gives the reader a jolt and leaves a mystery to pay off later.
- **Her promise:** *"Jo skola — mano skola"* (surety, §4.3).
- **His promise:** *"Kol aš gyvas, paskutinė ugnis neužges"* ("While I live, the last fire will not go out").
- **Consequence across the series:** after Jogaila extinguishes Šventaragis in 1387, Vytautas **secretly keeps one sacred flame** burning, in the Trakai island castle's deepest vault (Books II–IV). When he dies in 1430, it goes out. That is Book V ch. 1's "massive, unnatural silence". If he ever lets it die while he lives, the Pact's penalties apply (madness, then death). **Book IV temptation:** Henė offers him the crown in exchange for letting the flame die.

**Penalties for breaking the Sutartis:** loss of gift, curse of the elements, memory loss, madness, and death in the extreme. These are unchanged from the old doc.

## 7. Claims (Žymės)

- A Force may **mark** a mortal as its instrument without consent. The mark is felt (a rhythm in the fingers, a smell of ozone or of sap) and sometimes seen (a scar, eyes that catch lightning).
- A claimed mortal is **not** empowered at will. The Force acts *through* them at moments of its choosing, and **punishes** them for betraying its nature.
- **Perkūnas's claim on Vytautas (ch. 10):** Vytautas becomes the hand of **justice and oath**. Perkūnas rewards him when he keeps faith (the ch. 21 lightning is ambiguous by design) and strikes when he breaks an oath (the ch. 19 cracked window is a warning, because the baptism oath is false). Book II: the Vorskla defeat is partly Perkūnas's verdict on Vytautas's oath-breaking.
- **Claims can be contested.** The Root-Mother and Perkūnas both want Vytautas. That is the "reluctant hero" tension in `relationship_maps/mystical_connections.md`.

## 8. Bargains (Sandėriai) with the Shadow-Seller

- **Always kept to the letter.** Velnias never cheats. He *drafts*.
- **Always free at first.** The "thread" comes later: a small favour, then a larger one.
- **Beatable only by cleverness**, never by strength or faith. The model is the folk crop-division tale (ATU 1030, widespread in Lithuanian variants): the peasant agrees to give Velnias "what grows above the ground" and plants turnips, then "what grows below" and plants rye. Velnias keeps the bargain to the letter and is ruined by it.
- **Signs of a bargain in progress:** sounds die (his "Breath of the Void", which resolves Krėva's silence in `mythic_gaps.md` §1), warm coins, a figure at the edge of the firelight, three stones.
- **Who has bargained (canon):** Jogaila (1382: the "easy way" of the trap; the price is that his line inherits the crown and loses the land, so the Jagiellonians end up ruling Poland), Skirgaila (Krėva; the price is to be used and discarded), and later Vytautas (Book II's Vorskla vision; Book IV's crown).

## 9. Counter-forces

- **Consecrated stone** (Order castles, cathedrals) **dampens** Kalba: costs double and amber goes cold. This is part of why the pebble dies at Marienburg in ch. 19 (both explanations hold).
- **The Iron Saint** grants knights **fearlessness**, so Earth's "Unmovable Will" and Fire's terror have no grip on them.
- **The White Silence** smothers **Wind** (no sound, no far-speech) and freezes **Water** workings.
- **Cold iron** cut by a Christian smith on a feast day **burns** most spirits (bestiary). Peasants hang old Order horseshoes over doors after 1387, an irony the saga should enjoy.
- **Cremation and fire** release the dead and close a *vėlė*'s road back.

---

## 10. What magic cannot do
- Raise the dead (the Amber Queen *keeps* the dead, she does not return them).
- Create love or force a will (it can *strengthen* a will that is already there).
- Change the past (prophecy shows branches, never a fixed trunk).
- **Win a war.** At most it saves one person on one night. Battles are won by men, logistics and weather, sometimes weather that a Force has nudged.
- Work on a person who knows your **true name** better than you do (the defence against a binder who would enslave).

---

## 11. Quick checklist for writing a magic scene
1. Who is casting, and by which path (born, noticed, bound)?
2. Which mode: Kalba, Claim or Bargain?
3. Is it at the zenith or off it? What does that do to the cost?
4. **What is the bill, and where on the page is it shown?**
5. Does it obey the Law of Truth (no illusions)?
6. Does it leave residue (Memory) that a later chapter can find?
7. Does the political problem still have to be solved by mortals? (It must.)
