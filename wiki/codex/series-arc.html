Series arc

Source: books/series_arc.md

The braid that runs through all the books. Each book’s outline must advance every thread below, or deliberately rest it. Canon sources: world_rules/canon_decisions.md, cosmology.md, magic_rules.md, master_chronology.md.

The saga’s one-sentence spine: A pagan realm survives a century of crusade by a pact with its gods; one family’s kin-murder breaks it; the murdered man’s son spends fifty years building a Christian empire with his left hand while secretly keeping the old fire alive with his right, and the moment he dies the fire goes out and the gods become folklore.

2. Book by book: what each must hand to the next

Book VI (optional prequel): Šventasis Miškas, 1345–1377

  • Delivers: the oath scene of the Silent Pact; Kęstutis and Birutė’s meeting; Kęstutis’s 1361 Marienburg escape (the template for his son’s); Algirdas’s cremation.
  • Hands on: the reader knows the Pact’s three clauses, so every breach in Book I lands.

Book I: Vilko Tremtis, 1377–1387 (written; under revision)

  • Must plant: Pact clause 2 broken (Krėva); Birutė’s Sutartis with surety and the flame promise (ch. 3); Perkūnas’s claim (ch. 10); Eglė’s price (ch. 16); Henė’s three stones; the Iron Wolves massacred (ch. 7) and re-forming (ch. 25); Trakai given to Skirgaila (ch. 28); Ona as hostage; the flame lit in secret at the end of ch. 29.
  • Hands on: a Vytautas who is twice baptised, without his father’s land, with no surety, carrying a flame he cannot let die, and a land whose spirits are starting to go feral.

Book II: Auksinis Šešėlis, 1387–1399

  • Arc: ambition. From Skirgaila’s Trakai, through the second defection and Ostrów (where he becomes Grand Duke), to empire and then the Vorskla.
  • Must include (currently missing): the 1389–92 second civil war, the 1390 siege of Vilnius (the Crooked Castle and Bolingbroke), the poisoning of his sons (the moral price of defection), Ostrów 1392, and Skirgaila’s death in 1397, not in Part 3.
  • The Unbinding reaches the countryside; first vilkatai hunts.
  • Hands on: a humbled Vytautas who has seen what empire-hunger costs; Samogitia sold (Salynas 1398) and burning to rise.

Book III: Žaliasis Miškas, 1399–1411

  • Arc: atonement through alliance. Reconciled cousins; Samogitia’s uprisings; Grunwald.
  • Threads: the Root-Mother’s last great act; the Iron Saint broken; Perkūnas rewards a kept oath; the flame carried in a lantern on campaign (its one public moment, seen by a single žynys).
  • Hands on: victory that changes nothing for the gods: Samogitia will still be baptised.

Book IV: Nematoma Karūna, 1411–1430

  • Arc: the temptation of legitimacy. The crown offered by Sigismund; the flame as the price.
  • Threads: Ona’s death (1418); the Hidden Groves felled; Henė’s final bargain; the crown turned to leaves. Vytautas refuses to let the flame die, and so he dies uncrowned. Make that refusal the secret meaning of the historical mystery.
  • Hands on: a land with one flame left, in a dying man’s vault.

Book V: Vilko Palikimas, 1430–1450

  • Arc: the ghosts. The flame goes out; the civil war of the heirs; the Unbinding peaks at Pabaiskas; the gods become folklore.
  • Frame: Birutė (the Amber Queen) narrates. This connects to 00_pradzia.qmd, which becomes the series frame, not just Book I’s.
  • Ends: the Iron Wolf’s last howl over Trakai; the amber statue taken by the sea.

3. Rules for keeping the braid tight

  1. Each book opens with a thread from the last book’s final chapter (Book I ends with Henė and Skirgaila, so Book II opens with Skirgaila in Trakai).
  2. Every magic use spends something that matters later (magic_rules.md §4). Costs accumulate across books.
  3. Henė appears at least once per book, always at a deal, juggling three stones (later two, then one, as the cousins die).
  4. The amber pebble’s state is the reader’s moral compass. Track it in each book’s continuity bible.
  5. Historical anchors are non-negotiable (D3). The myth explains why, never whether.
  6. Keep the grey. Every book must give one sympathetic Order brother, one decent Pole, one monstrous Lithuanian and one choice where the pagan option is worse.

4. Open design questions for the author

  • Does the flame have a keeper other than Vytautas (an Iron Wolf? a Karaim at Trakai?), so Book V can stage its going-out from a witness’s POV?
  • Is Book VI written before or after Book V’s flashbacks? (If after, Book V can quote it as a “song”.)
  • How much of Birutė’s frame narration should each book carry: a prologue only, or interludes?
