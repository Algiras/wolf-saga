Effect

The gifts. The sight of the dvasios, night-sight and scent, the Root-Mother's healing, cold and wet as nothing, pack-sense, the iron hour. It is not shape-change.

Half-wrong folk belief. Peasants think all vilkatai become wolves. Only the Seniai can put on the wolf.

At greater cost

Putting on the wolf. Real flesh, granted by the Root-Mother. Each time they come back less. The last time, they do not come back, and run into the Dykra on four feet.

Breaking it

Gediminid blood. Rule 2 of the Pack's Word: breaking it gives the wolf to the Root-Mother at once. He goes into the bog alive.

Lying on the skull. Every wolf in the Dykra hears it.

Leaving. Once, if someone who knew his true name speaks it at a living flame and offers bread on a knife-point. He loses every gift and ages the years in one winter.

Vilkų krikštas: the shapeshifter’s rite granted by the Root-Mother.

What it is¶

Vilkų krikštas, the Wolves‘ Baptism: granted power from the Root-Mother through the Dykra. It makes a man a vilkatas: night-sight, scent, sight of the dvasios, the Root-Mother’s healing and the berserk „iron hour“. It is not shape-change.

Who can use it (path & tier)¶

Unmarried men of 16 to 25, sponsored by a sworn wolf (his vilktėvis). Only wolves sworn twenty years or more (the Seniai) can put on the wolf; the folk belief that all vilkatai shape-shift is half wrong. Examples: Jonas (tier 3) and the Flame-Keeper Žiežirba (Book III ch. 9).

How it works (medium, words, timing)¶

Held in the twelve nights after Kūčios at a sacred flame: a wake as if he had died, his true name whispered to the fire, a throat-cut rubbed with live ash, nine nights without fire, then pressed under the mud of the oldest bog until the Root-Mother decides („Kas neiškils, tas liks“, Book III ch. 9), and an oath on the Iron Wolf’s skull. Full rite: the Iron Wolves.

Kaina¶

Taken: his true name, the road up (laid in the bog, never burned, never climbs to Dausos), his hearth, part of his soul, a little memory each iron hour, and his tears. Given: the pack, the sight, the iron hour. He can leave once, if someone who knew his true name speaks it at a living flame and offers bread on a knife-point (Book V ch. 29).

Limits & counters¶

No hearth under a roof; feast-day iron burns them; consecrated stone sickens them; the unfed dead hunt them. New wolves can be made only at a living flame, so the brotherhood ends when the last flame goes out in 1430. Bound by the Root-Mother’s nature, not the Three Laws.

Folk basis [Historical record]¶

[Historical record]. The wolf-warrior band is an Indo-European pattern (Männerbund, Old Norse úlfheðnar). Herodotus (4.105) has the Neuri becoming wolves for days each year; Olaus Magnus (1555) has werewolves gathering at Christmas in Prussia, Livonia and Lithuania. The Iron Wolf dream is a 16th-century chronicle legend.

Notable practitioners¶

Jonas; the Flame-Keeper; Šešiapirštis, wolf-father (Book II ch. 9); Pilkoji, taken „against all custom“ after her village’s dead rise (Book III ch. 7, 9); Kęstutis’s silver wolf (Book VI ch. 5).
