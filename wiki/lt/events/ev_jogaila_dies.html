Jogaila dies¶

What the chronicles say [Historical record]¶

Jogaila died on 1 June 1434 at Gródek near Lviv, in the forty-eighth year of his reign. According to Długosz he caught a cold in a late-April frost at Medyka (the chill), listening to nightingales at night, was ill for weeks and was taken to Gródek; the day before he died he summoned loyal knights and entrusted his sons to them. Queen Sophia was in Kraków; Oleśnicki and the chancellor, bound for Basel, turned back at Poznań. The body reached Kraków on 11 June and the funeral was at Wawel on 18 June (historia.org.pl, 11 October 2024). His birth year is c. 1362 on current scholarship (Tęgowski 1999), so about 72; an older view gives c. 1352. He dies at Gródek; the body was taken to Kraków.

Pastaba

King Jogaila dies at Gródek on 1 June 1434 at the age of seventy-two.

What really happened [The saga’s invention]¶

His death ends the cousins‘ feud, and Henė loses his last stone, Jogaila’s: it drops and his hands are empty. Jogaila, who had dreamed of a jester turning a black stone, sees Kęstutis at the foot of the bed and pretends not to. The last confession is invention (the record is silent).

Consequences¶

Žygimantas’s Orthodox-rights privilege follows on 6 May 1434, and the war of the heirs moves to Pabaiskas.

Dramatisation¶

Book V ch. 12 „Lakštingala“ (Jogaila; late April to 1 June 1434; Medyka and Gródek) and ch. 6 „Paskutinis Akmuo“ (his dream, 1431).
