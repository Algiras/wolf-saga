Jogailaičiai (Jagiellonians)¶

House card¶

Seat

Vilnius (as Grand Dukes), Krėva (Algirdas’s patrimonial castle, Jogaila’s refuge and prison of 1381–82), Vitebsk (Uliana’s dower lands), later Kraków (Wawel). [Historical record]

Arms / sigil

The Vytis for the Grand Duchy; Jogaila’s personal double cross on his seals after his baptism (1386); the Polish White Eagle quartered with the Vytis on royal seals. [Historical record] Saga sigil: a crow on a crown, the Shadow-Seller’s eyes. [The saga’s invention]

Words

Tyla toliau nueina. „Silence goes further.“ [The saga’s invention]

Patron / burden

The Shadow-Seller (Jogaila’s 1382 bargain: his line inherits the crown and loses the land); the White Silence where baptisms are bought; later the Gold-Glutton. [The saga’s invention]

Jogailaičiai (Jagiellonians)

Tipas

giminė

Status

Istoriniai duomenys

Pastaba

Jogaila’s line: Grand Dukes of Lithuania and, from 1386, kings of Poland; Vytis plus the double cross.

Lineage¶

Algirdas’s second marriage (1350) to Uliana of Tver produced the inner dynasty: Jogaila (c. 1362 – 1 Jun 1434; Władysław from 1386), Skirgaila, Kaributas, Lengvenis, Karigaila, Vygantas and Švitrigaila. [Historical record]

Jogaila’s four marriages: Jadwiga of Anjou (1386; one daughter, who died in infancy in 1399), Anna of Celje (1402; Jadwiga, 1408–31), Elżbieta Granowska (1417; none) and Sophia of Halshany (1422), mother of Władysław III (1424–44), Casimir IV (1427–92) and a short-lived Kazimierz. [Historical record] Sophia’s family is the Alšėniškiai. Casimir was elected Grand Duke on 29 Jun 1440, aged 12, and crowned King of Poland on 25 Jun 1447.

Feuds & alliances¶

Jogaila against Kęstutis (1380–82) and Vytautas (1382–84, 1389–92); Švitrigaila against everyone (1393, 1402, 1408, 1430–32); Skirgaila’s resentment of Jogaila’s ingratitude. Marriage web: Moscow (Lengvenis, Elena), Masovia (Aleksandra and Siemowit IV), Pomerania, Mecklenburg. After 1386 the house is also the Polish crown, and the Lesser Poland lords rule through it.

Fate across the books¶

  • I: Jogaila, bought by Henė, kills his uncle and agrees the Krėva Union (1385); baptised 1386. II: distant in Kraków; Skirgaila rules for him. III: reconciliation at Grunwald.

  • IV: outlives Vytautas, still guilty; his sons come only from the last wife, and the lords sell their succession for privileges.

  • V: Jogaila dies at Gródek (1 Jun 1434), Henė’s last stone dropping. Casimir inherits both realms, and the Grand Duchy becomes a province of his house, as the bargain’s letter said. [The saga’s invention]

Ryšiai

Ally: Alšėniškiai (Holszański, princes of Halshany) (1422–…) — Sophia of Halshany married Jogaila.

Member of: Algirdaičiai (Algirdas’s line) — Uliana’s line: Jogaila and his heirs.

Rules: Kingdom of Poland (Lesser Poland lords) (1386–…)
