Kamianets (Podolia)¶

In the 1380s [Historical record]¶

Kamianets (Pol. Kamieniec, today Kamianets-Podilskyi) sits on a peninsula wound by the Smotrych river, with a castle traditionally dated to the second half of the 14th century on a site occupied since the 12th-13th. The Koriatovych (Karijotaičiai) brothers, sons of Gediminas’s son Karijotas, held Podolia as Lithuanian vassals; a document of 1374 by Yuriy Koriatovych promises Magdeburg rights to the town. Fyodor Koriatovych was expelled in 1393, after which Lithuanian and Polish governors held Podolia in turn; Spytek of Melsztyn, a Polish magnate, modernised the castle c. 1400, adding ten towers. From the mid-14th to mid-15th century it lay on the Polish-Lithuanian frontier with the steppe. After Vytautas’s death (27 Oct 1430) the Polish lords seized Podolia in the next weeks; Kamianets, according to one account, refused to surrender. Poland organised the Podolian Voivodeship in 1434 with Kamianets as its seat. Sources: en.wikipedia (Kamianets-Podilskyi Castle); VLE; lt.wikipedia (Švitrigaila).

Sensory sketch¶

A fortress on a rock island in a canyon: limestone cliffs, a green river far below the walls, a single bridge to the mainland. Stone castle, thick walls, new towers around the courtyard; Armenian, Ruthenian, Polish and Wallachian traders in the town. Autumn mist in the canyon, steppe wind overhead, cold stone in November, the smell of horses and tanneries. The frontier is audible as a hush of watchers on the walls.

In the saga¶

Podolia is Vytautas’s southern shield; its loss within weeks of his death is the first sign that the empire will not outlive him. Book V ch. 3 has an envoy ride twelve days through the first frost to tell Švitrigaila that the Polish lords “took Kamianets”, which matches the record: Polish and Podolian lords took the castle in Oct-Nov 1430 and the Poles then declined to return it to Lithuania as the truce required. No mythic overlay of its own.

Getting there¶

Kamianets is some 670 km south of Vilnius in a straight line (850-900 km by road, about four weeks on horse). The saga’s messengers from Podolia took twelve days to reach Švitrigaila in the late-autumn frost. From Lutsk about 250 km; from Lviv about 225 km; from Kraków about 500 km. The Dniester lies 30 km south-west; the steppe edge lies a few days’ ride east.

Scenes set here¶

Book V ch. 3 Švitrigailos pakilimas (the Podolian envoys arrive late November 1430), ch. 6 Paskutinis akmuo (the Polish lords speak of Podolia, December 1431).

Relations

Events here: Polish lords seize Podolia after Vytautas’s death — Polish and Podolian lords took Kamianets castle in Oct-Nov 1430 and the Poles did not return it as the truce required (lt.wikipedia, Švitrigaila); retaken by Podolians in 1433.
