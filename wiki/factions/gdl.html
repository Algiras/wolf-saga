Grand Duchy of Lithuania: sovereign pagan-Ruthenian realm from the Baltic to the Black Sea.

Overview¶

[Historical record] A pagan realm, the last in Europe, stretching from the Baltic coast to the Rus’ lands. Mindaugas was crowned King in 1253; Gediminas (c. 1316-1341) made Vilnius its capital in 1323; Algirdas (east) and Kęstutis (west) ruled as a diarchy from 1345 to 1377. Grand Dukes in the saga’s years: Jogaila (1377-81; 1382-92), Kęstutis (1381-82), Vytautas (1392-1430, by the Ostrów agreement, 4-5 Aug 1392), Švitrigaila (1430-32), Žygimantas (1432-40) and Casimir (elected 29 Jun 1440, aged 12). Baptism came with Jogaila in 1387 and Samogitia in 1413-17.

Patron Force¶

None as a state. Its two ruling lines have separate patrons, the Shadow-Seller for the Algirdaičiai and the Amber Queen for the Kęstutaičiai (see Algirdaičiai and Kęstutaičiai). After 1430 the Gold-Glutton speaks through its council’s ledgers (Book V ch. 17).

Internal tensions¶

The realm is a family firm until 1392. The appanage princes then give way to governors. Boyars gain Polish arms at Horodło, but Orthodox princes are shut out of the top offices, which is the grievance behind Švitrigaila’s army in 1432. Samogitia obeys no one it did not choose.

Notable members¶

Algirdas, Jogaila, Kęstutis, Vytautas, Švitrigaila, Žygimantas.
