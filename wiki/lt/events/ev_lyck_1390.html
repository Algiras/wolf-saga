Treaty of Lyck; hostages demanded¶

What the chronicles say [Historical record]¶

Signed at Lyck (Ełk) on 19 January 1390 by Marquard von Salzbach, komtur Arnold von Bürglen and Thomas son of Survila; it confirmed the 1384 Königsberg treaty (Samogitia to the Nevėžis, vassalage). Hostages demanded: his brother Sigismund (Žygimantas), wife Anna (Ona), daughter Sofija, sister Rymgajla, brother-in-law Ivan Olshanski and other nobles. Sofija was a hostage; the Moscow embassy reached Prussia at the end of the summer and she left from Marienburg by ship via Danzig and Pskov (Grala, citing Posilge). The marshal is not attested; some doubt the treaty’s originality.

What really happened [The saga’s invention]¶

Vytautas swears „honestly“, to keep faith as long as the Order does; the pebble is dim in the consecrated stone. Marquard von Salzbach, his old captive and friend, offers a velvet threat to the hostages. The Order lets mother and daughter spend a day together before Sofija’s autumn departure.

Consequences¶

Caused by the second defection. Leads to the siege of Vilnius.

Dramatisation¶

Book II ch. 6 „Įkaitų Pilis“ (Ona; January 1390, Lyck); ch. 8 (Sofija’s road to Moscow: Marienburg - Danzig - ship - Pskov).
