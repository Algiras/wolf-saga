1.5 Vytautas and Ona¶

  • Ona (Anna). Married Vytautas c.1370. Her parentage is unknown. One hypothesis makes her a princess of Smolensk, another a daughter of a Lithuanian boyar house. Died 1418 at Trakai. She organised the Krėva escape of autumn 1382, in which her maid (Elena) stayed in Vytautas’s bed and was executed. Hostage of the Order in 1383–84 (saga) and, in the record, from the Treaty of Lyck (19 Jan 1390); the Order freed her in May 1392 to spread pro-Lithuanian rumours; she confirmed Ostrów in two letters. Born c. 1350–52 (the record has no date; Book IV’s ch. 9 makes her 66 in 1418).

  • Sofija (1371 – 1453). Betrothed to Vasily Dmitrievich of Moscow. By tradition the match was made when Vasily escaped from the Horde in 1386–87 and passed through Lithuanian lands. Married 9 Jan 1391. Grand Princess of Moscow 1391–1425; regent for her son Vasily II from 1425 in the Muscovite civil war. Her children include Vasily II the Blind (1415–62), Anna (1393–1417), who married the Byzantine heir John VIII Palaiologos in 1411 and died of plague, and Anastasija, who married Olelka of Kyiv, so Vytautas’s granddaughter married Algirdas’s great-grandson.

  • The sons: a CONTESTED tradition. Sofija is the only child of Vytautas in the record. The late tradition (Simon Grunau, 16th c.) says that two young sons, Iwan and Georg (Jurgis and Ivanas: the names are the tradition’s, not the saga’s), were poisoned by a knight while hostages of the Order, as revenge for the betrayal (after the break, before Ostrów, summer 1392). en.wikipedia (Jonynas 1984) calls it a false rumour; Vytautas’s letters never accuse the Order of it; a „Jurgis and Jonas“ on the 1390–92 hostage list were kinsmen who came back alive. The saga adopts the tradition and says so in Book II’s afterword.

  • Second wife: Julijona Alšėniškė (Uliana Olshanska), ⚭ 13 Nov 1418 at Grodno (en.wikipedia; VLE: „before Christmas 1418“), daughter of Prince Ivan of Halshany (Alšėnai). No children.

Consequence [Historical record]. Vytautas died with no male heir. His blood survived only in Moscow: his grandson Vasily II. Vasily I’s testament (1423) placed the boy under Vytautas’s protection. The Kęstutaičiai line that the Order tried to wipe out ends up on the Muscovite throne, which is the bitterest irony in the series.

2. House cards¶

Mottos are [The saga’s invention] unless stated. Heraldry follows the record where it exists and says so where it does not.

A note on Lithuanian heraldry [Historical record]¶

  • The Vytis (Pogonia, Pogoń): an armed rider charging, sword raised. Mounted-warrior seals appear among the Gediminids in the second half of the 14th century. Which duke used it first is debated (attributions to Algirdas’s era and to Narimantas or Kęstutis circulate). Under Jogaila and Vytautas it becomes the arms of the Grand Duchy. In the 1380s it is a princely seal, not yet a „national“ flag.

  • The Columns (Gediminaičių stulpai): the „Gediminid“ name is a 19th-century label. The sign is first attested on Vytautas’s coins and seals in the late 14th century, and is used afterwards by Žygimantas Kęstutaitis. It is plausibly a family tamga (property mark) of the Kęstutaičiai. In the period the Columns are Vytautas’s mark, not Jogaila’s.

  • The double cross (Jagiellonian cross): appears on Jogaila’s seals after his baptism (1386). It is Jogaila’s personal sign as a Christian king.

  • Długosz on Grunwald [Historical record]: the Lithuanian banners bore mostly the rider, a minority the Columns.

  • Horodło 1413 [Historical record]: 47 Catholic Lithuanian boyar families were „adopted“ into Polish heraldic clans and took Polish arms (see §3). Before 1413, Lithuanian boyars used house-marks, not Western coats of arms.

Consequence for the books. In Book I (1382–87), no one should wear the Columns as a dynastic blazon on Jogaila’s side. That is an anachronism, and it also inverts the symbolism. The Vytis on a ducal seal or banner is acceptable, with care.

2.1 House of Gediminas (the dynasty as a whole)¶

  • Seat: Vilnius: the Upper Castle on the hill, the Lower Castle, and the wooden Crooked Castle (burnt 1390). Older seats: Kernavė and Trakai.

  • Arms: the Vytis [Historical record, with the dating caveats above].

  • Sigil (saga use): the Iron Wolf howling on the hill [The saga’s invention; the dream itself is Historical record as legend].

  • Words: Kol staugia vilkas, stovi miestas. „While the wolf howls, the city stands.“ [The saga’s invention]

  • Character: pagan-pragmatic. They marry Orthodox, Catholic and steppe brides, accept baptism when it pays and drop it when it does not. They rule a mostly Ruthenian and Orthodox empire from a pagan Baltic core. Their genius is the appanage: every son gets a city, and every city gets a son who might rebel.

  • Feuds: Jaunutis against his brothers (1345); Algirdaičiai against Kęstutaičiai (1380–92, 1432–40); the elder Algirdaičiai against the younger (1377–99).

  • Mythic patron or burden: the Silent Pact (Cosmology §4.1) is sworn by the house, not by one branch. Clause 2, no Gediminid sheds Gediminid blood, binds every branch, which is why every kin-killing in the tree above costs something in the Unbinding.

2.2 The Algirdaičiai of Uliana: Jogaila’s line (the Jogailaičiai)¶

  • Seats: Vilnius (as Grand Dukes), Krėva (Algirdas’s patrimonial castle; Jogaila’s refuge after 1381 and the prison of 1382), Vitebsk (Uliana’s dower lands), later Kraków (Wawel).

  • Arms: the Vytis for the Grand Duchy; Jogaila’s personal double cross after 1386 [Historical record]. After 1386 Jogaila quarters the Polish White Eagle with the Vytis on royal seals.

  • Sigil (saga use): a crow on a crown [The saga’s invention].

  • Words: Tyla toliau nueina. „Silence goes further.“ [The saga’s invention] This echoes the chronicle tradition that Algirdas never told even his captains where an army was marching (record as tradition).

  • Character: patient, calculating, Orthodox-raised by their mother and Catholic by necessity. They win by waiting. Jogaila outlives every rival: Kęstutis, Skirgaila, Jadwiga, Vytautas. The house’s virtue is endurance; its vice is that it lets others do the killing.

  • Feuds: Jogaila against Kęstutis (1380–82) and Vytautas (1382–84, 1389–92). Švitrigaila against everyone. Skirgaila’s resentment of Jogaila’s ingratitude. The Uliana sons against Maria’s sons.

  • Marriage web: Moscow (Lengvenis ⚭ Maria Dmitrievna; Elena ⚭ Serpukhov), Masovia (Aleksandra ⚭ Siemowit IV), Pomerania, Mecklenburg; Jogaila’s four queens (Anjou, Celje–Piast, Pilica, Halshany).

  • Mythic patron or burden: the Shadow-Seller (Šešėlių Pardavėjas). Jogaila’s 1382 bargain: his line inherits the crown and loses the land (Magic rules §8). The White Silence settles where his baptism is bought (Cosmology §3.3: Vilnius 1382, Krėva 1385). Later, the Gold-Glutton feeds on his heirs‘ Polish privileges. The Root-Mother treats him as „a tumour in her side“. [The saga’s invention]

2.3 Skirgaila: „the hand that did the work“ (cadet seat)¶

  • Seats: Trakai (1382, and by Jogaila’s grant after 1384), Polotsk (taken from Andrius, 1387), Kyiv (1395–97) [Historical record].

  • Arms: used the dynastic rider as Jogaila’s lieutenant.

  • Sigil (saga use): a mailed fist closing on a chain, the chain that strangled Kęstutis [The saga’s invention].

  • Words: Darbas nekalba. „The work does not speak.“ [The saga’s invention]

  • Character: the family’s enforcer. Brave in the field (he took Polotsk and defended Vilnius in 1390), feared and drinking, never thanked. Historically he was Jogaila’s most capable lieutenant and the man Polish lords expected to govern Lithuania for the king.

  • Feuds: with Vytautas (Trakai is the open wound until Ostrów 1392); with Andrius of Polotsk (whom he captured); with Švitrigaila (whom he treats as a child).

  • Mythic patron or burden: the Shadow-Seller’s second stone. His price was to be used and then discarded (Magic rules §8). Perkūnas’s enmity follows his broken safe-conduct of August 1382 (Power systems §2.6). He dies in Kyiv in 1397 with no heir; the stone drops. [The saga’s invention]

2.4 The elder Algirdaičiai: Maria’s sons, the Orthodox princes¶

  • Seats: Polotsk (Andrius), Bryansk and Trubchevsk (Dmitrijus), Kyiv (Vladimiras), Chartoryisk (Konstantinas’s heirs).

  • Arms: the rider on Ruthenian-style princely seals. Orthodox icons and princely signs.

  • Sigil (saga use): a church dome under a rider [The saga’s invention].

  • Words: Pirmasis kraujas neužmiršta. „First blood does not forget.“ [The saga’s invention]

  • Character: the first-born, Orthodox and Russian-speaking, with Kyivan Rus‘ manners. Loyal to their lands, not to Vilnius. They change sides (Moscow, the Livonian Order, Vytautas) to keep their patrimonies. Yet two of them die for Vytautas at the Vorskla (1399), which shows that the Kęstutaitis cousin treated them better than their half-brother had.

  • Feuds: Andrius against Jogaila and Skirgaila (1377–87); their descendants against the Kęstutaičiai (the Czartoryski murder of 1440). [The saga’s invention suggestion]: the Steppe-Hunger takes its price from them at the Vorskla; their dvoeverie keeps the domovoi fed even as the gods fall silent.

2.5 Švitrigaila: the storm-bringer¶

  • Seats: Vitebsk (1390s), Podolia and Chernihiv–Siverian lands, Lutsk (his last seat).

  • Arms: the rider; as Grand Duke 1430–32 he sealed with the Vytis.

  • Sigil (saga use): a black horse rearing in lightning [The saga’s invention].

  • Words: Audra neprašo leidimo. „A storm asks no leave.“ [The saga’s invention]

  • Character: the youngest, Uliana’s favourite, charming to Orthodox princes, Tatars and the Order alike. He rebels in every decade of his long life and dies in his bed at eighty, outliving every brother and cousin. He is the Gediminids‘ last pure appanage prince.

  • Feuds: against Jogaila (1393, 1402, 1430–32); against Vytautas (1408 defection to Moscow; prisoner 1409–18); against Žygimantas Kęstutaitis (the civil war of 1432–38). [The saga’s invention suggestion]: in Book V the Root-Mother’s wolves attack only men carrying „foreign seals“ (Book V ch. 11). Švitrigaila promises a „return to the Old GDL“, so the forest may briefly court him, then abandon him at Pabaiskas when his allies prove to be the Livonian Order, the Iron Saint’s own.

2.6 The Kęstutaičiai: Kęstutis’s line¶

  • Seats: Trakai, which includes Senieji Trakai (Old Trakai, Kęstutis’s early residence), the Peninsula Castle and the Island Castle (begun under Kęstutis, completed by Vytautas in the early 15th century). Also Kaunas (destroyed 1362, rebuilt), Grodno (Vytautas’s seat before 1382 and after 1384), and Samogitia as their sworn charge. [Historical record]

  • Arms: the Columns (Vytautas’s and Žygimantas’s mark [Historical record, late 14th century onward]); the Vytis as Grand Dukes after 1392.

  • Sigil (saga use): the boar for Kęstutis („the Old Boar“, Senasis Šernas, the series‘ own epithet); the wolf for Vytautas; amber for Birutė [The saga’s invention].

  • Words: Duotas žodis nerūdija. „A given word does not rust.“ [The saga’s invention] This is grounded in Kęstutis’s real reputation among the Order’s own chroniclers for keeping faith, and ironic because his sons break their word to the Order again and again.

  • Character: soldiers, oath-swearers, frontiersmen. Western-facing (Prussia, Masovia, the Order) where the Algirdaičiai face east. Kęstutis himself was admired by his enemies; the knights told stories of his escape from Marienburg (1361). His sons inherit the courage, not the honesty.

  • Feuds: Butautas’s betrayal (1365); Vytautas against Jogaila and Skirgaila; Žygimantas’s paranoia against the boyars and against Švitrigaila (Book V).

  • Marriage web: Masovia (Danutė ⚭ Janusz I; Rimgailė ⚭ Henryk), Moldavia (Rimgailė’s second marriage), Moscow (Sofija ⚭ Vasily I), Halshany (Julijona).

  • Mythic patron or burden: the Amber Queen’s line through Birutė, and claimed by Perkūnas (Cosmology §3.2; Magic rules §7), because the feud between Perkūnas and Jūratė is unfinished, and Vytautas is the vessel’s son. The Root-Mother contests the claim (Magic rules §7). The house carries the flame vow (Magic rules §6): while I live, the last fire will not go out. [The saga’s invention]

2.7 Vytautas’s own line¶

  • Seat: Trakai (Island Castle) and Vilnius; the court at Lutsk for the 1429 congress.

  • Arms: the Columns on his seals and coins, the Vytis for the state [Historical record].

  • Sigil (saga use): the Iron Wolf with an amber eye [The saga’s invention].

  • Words: Kas atsimena, tas valdo. „Who remembers, rules.“ [The saga’s invention] This is the Amber Queen’s office of memory turned into politics.

  • Character: the man of two baptisms (Wigand 1383, Aleksandras 1386) and two defections (1384, 1392). He builds an empire from the Baltic almost to the Black Sea, and dies without a son.

  • Feuds: with Skirgaila (Trakai); with Švitrigaila (Moscow, Kremenets); with Oleśnicki and the Lesser Poland lords (the crown, Book IV).

  • Burden: the sons poisoned (1392) as the price of defection. Sofija in Moscow as the only survivor. The flame vow, which costs him the crown in Book IV. [The saga’s invention for the vow; Historical record for the heirlessness]

2.8 The Palanga amber-priestess line (Gintaro vaidilutės) [The saga’s invention]¶

  • Basis [Historical record]: Birutė’s Palanga origin is a strong Lithuanian tradition (Birutė Hill at Palanga, her legendary role as a vaidilutė guarding a sacred fire). Wigand of Marburg (c. 1394), not Długosz, has Jogaila drown her (en.wikipedia: at Brest, with relatives Vidimantas and Butrimas killed); VLE thinks Kęstutis sent her to Brest in 1382; at Constance (1416) delegates said she lived long and died a natural death; the Palanga burial first appears in Stryjkowski (1582). Her death date is unknown. Everything else below is invention.

  • Seat: Birutės kalnas (Birutė Hill), its shore flame and the sea-stone (Šventasis akmuo, ch. 11).

  • Sigil: a drop of amber with a flame inside [The saga’s invention].

  • Words: Jūra atsimena. „The sea remembers.“ [The saga’s invention]

  • Inheritance: through mothers (Magic rules §2: the gift „usually runs down the maternal line“). Each generation gives one daughter to the shore flame. The line supplies the vessels of the Amber Queen (Cosmology §3.4).

  • Birutė’s maternal kin: keep them unnamed and few. A grandmother who was a vessel before her (the last „walking“ of the Queen before 1384) is the cleanest invention. The chronicle tradition that Birutė’s relatives were killed in 1382 can mean, in the saga, that Jogaila’s men tried to end the line. Birutė is the last vessel: after 1384 the Queen acts only through amber Birutė has touched.

  • Status after 1387: a remnant of women keeping a hidden fire at Palanga without a vessel. By Book V the statue no longer glows (Book V ch. 25).

Era notes

  • Book I (1382–87): name boyars by patronymic or personal name only. The Kęstutis loyalists massacred in ch. 7 can be the Iron Wolves, invented retainers. Their names should be pagan dithematic (Dau-gird-as, Ram-baud-as).

  • Books II–III: Vytautas’s companions (Astikas, Goštautas’s father’s generation, Valimantas’s sons) rise. Many princes and boyars die at the Vorskla (1399), which makes room for new men.

  • Book IV (1413–30): Horodło makes the Catholic boyars a heraldic nobility. The new voivodes of Vilnius and Trakai become offices worth fighting over. Orthodox lords are excluded from the highest offices: a Record grievance that feeds Book V.

  • Book V (1430–50): the council lords (Goštautas, Daugirdas, Kęsgaila) are the state. They make and unmake grand dukes, as in 1440.

4. The neighbouring houses¶

4.1 The Masovian Piasts [Historical record]¶

  • Seat: Warsaw and Czersk (Janusz I); Płock and Rawa (Siemowit IV).

  • Arms: the Masovian (Piast) eagle.

  • Words: Między dwoma ogniami. „Between two fires.“ (Polish) [The saga’s invention]

  • Key figures: Janusz I (c.1346–1429) ⚭ Danutė Kęstutaitė: Vytautas’s brother-in-law and steady ally. Siemowit IV (c.1352–1426) ⚭ Aleksandra Algirdaitė (1387): a failed candidate for the Polish crown in 1382–84, who tried to seize Kraków by force. Henryk (d. 1392/93), bishop-elect of Płock and Jogaila’s envoy at Ostrów, ⚭ Rimgailė Kęstutaitė in 1392 and was dead within a year.

  • Character: small, proud, squeezed between the Order, Poland and Lithuania. Two brothers married the two Gediminid lines: Janusz to Kęstutis’s daughter, Siemowit to Algirdas’s. In the saga Masovia is the neutral ground where cousins meet and where Vytautas runs first after Krėva.

4.2 The Polish court and the Lesser Poland lords¶

  • The Anjou queen. Jadwiga (see 1.6). Daughter of Louis I of Hungary and Poland (d. 10/11 Sep 1382) and Elizabeth of Bosnia (murdered 1387). Write her as used by it.)

  • The Lesser Poland (Małopolska) lords [Historical record]: the oligarchy that chose Jogaila over Wilhelm of Habsburg and Siemowit IV (1384–85). Key names: Spytko II of Melsztyn (Leliwa arms; voivode of Kraków; killed at the Vorskla, 1399); Jan of Tarnów (Leliwa); Dobiesław of Kurozwęki; Jan Tęczyński; Krystyn of Ostrów, castellan of Kraków.

  • Two Spytkos, do not merge them. Spytko II (d. 1399), Jogaila’s backer, dies beside Vytautas at the Vorskla. Spytko III of Melsztyn, his son (d. 4 May 1439 at Grotniki), led the Hussite-leaning confederation against Oleśnicki.

  • Zbigniew Oleśnicki (1389–1455) [Historical record], Dębno arms. Royal secretary; at Grunwald he saved Jogaila from a German knight’s charge (Długosz). Bishop of Kraków (appointed 9 Jul 1423), cardinal (created 18 Dec 1439 by Eugene IV; the antipope Felix V also named him on 20 Jan 1440). Leader of the Polish party that blocked Vytautas’s coronation (1429–30). Patron in the saga: the Gold-Glutton, with Henė’s last bargain.

  • Words (Lesser Poland lords): Rex regnat, nos regimus. „The king reigns; we rule.“ (Latin) [The saga’s invention]

4.3 The Luxembourgs [Historical record]¶

  • Seat: Buda (Hungary), Prague (Bohemia), the imperial court.

  • Arms: the Luxembourg lion; Sigismund’s arms combine Hungary, Bohemia and the imperial eagle. Sigismund founded the Order of the Dragon (1408) [Historical record].

  • Key figure: Sigismund (1368–1437). ⚭ Mary of Hungary (1385), then Barbara of Celje (1405/08). King of Hungary 1387, King of the Romans 1411, of Bohemia 1419/1436, Emperor 1433. Hosted the Council of Constance. Arbiter between Poland and the Order (the 1420 Breslau award went against Poland). Offered Vytautas a crown at Lutsk (1429).

  • Words: Divide et arbitra. „Divide, then arbitrate.“ [The saga’s invention]

  • Patron: the Shadow-Seller serves him in 1429–30 and the Gold-Glutton flatters him.

4.4 The Rurikids of Moscow [Historical record]¶

  • Seat: the Moscow Kremlin.

  • Arms: the mounted St George appears on Moscow coins from the late 14th century. Two riders, Lithuania’s and Moscow’s, facing each other across the Ugra.

  • Key figures: Dmitry Donskoy (1350–89), victor of Kulikovo (8 Sep 1380), negotiated with Uliana in 1384 to marry a daughter to Jogaila (an Orthodox alternative to Krėva). Vasily I (1371–1425) ⚭ Sofija Vytautaitė: three wars with his father-in-law (1406–08), ended by the stand-off on the Ugra (1408). Vasily II (1415–62), Vytautas’s grandson, under Vytautas’s protection from 1425.

  • Words: Sobirai i terpi. „Gather and endure.“ (Russian) [The saga’s invention] [The saga’s invention suggestion]: Moscow is the realm the Horde taught to collect tribute, so the Steppe-Hunger’s shadow lies on it lightly.

4.5 The Horde khans [Historical record]¶

  • Tokhtamysh (khan c.1380–95; killed c.1406 near Tyumen). Reunited the Horde, burned Moscow in August 1382 (the same month Kęstutis died), was broken by Timur (1391, 1395), and fled to Vytautas, granting him rights over the Rus‘ lands in exchange for restoration. Sons Jalal ad-Din (fought beside Vytautas at Grunwald; briefly khan 1411–12) and Kadir Berdi (killed Edigu in 1419).

  • Temur Qutlugh (khan 1397/98–99). Nominal victor of the Vorskla; died months later.

  • Edigu (c.1352–1419). Manghit emir and beglerbeg, the kingmaker of khans; field commander at the Vorskla; raided Moscow (1408) and Kyiv (1416).

  • Hacı Giray (d. 1466): raised in Lithuania, made first khan of an independent Crimea with Lithuanian backing in the 1440s. Book V seed.

  • Arms: tamgas (clan marks).

  • Words: Tengri – dangus, žemė – arklio. „The sky is Tengri’s; the earth belongs to the horse.“ [The saga’s invention, phrased in Lithuanian as heard at Vytautas’s court]

  • Patron: Stepių Alkis (Steppe-Hunger), which is not Tengri (Cosmology §2.2) but the egregore of horizon-conquest.

5. The Teutonic Order as an institution-house¶

5.1 House card¶

  • Seat: Marienburg (Malbork), the Grand Master’s seat since 1309. The Marshal at Königsberg. Frontier houses on the Neman: Ragnit, Georgenburg (Jurbarkas), Christmemel, Marienwerder (Naujapilis), built for Vytautas in 1384.

  • Arms: the black cross on white [Historical record]. The Grand Master’s arms add a gold cross over the black, with the imperial eagle on an inescutcheon. The fleur-de-lis ends attributed to St Louis are legend.

  • Words: no period motto. Helfen – Wehren – Heilen is a modern motto; do not use it in 14th-century scenes. Saga motto: Per crucem, ferro. „Through the cross, by iron.“ [The saga’s invention]

  • Character: a corporation, not a family. Celibate brother-knights recruited mainly from the German lands, with no heirs, so the house never dies and never forgives. It is rich (amber monopoly, grain, the Prussian Hanse towns) and diplomatically skilled. It ran the Reisen, the winter and summer raids with Western „guests“ (Henry Bolingbroke in 1390 among them).

  • Feuds: internal: the Grand Master against the Prussian estates (the Prussian Confederation, founded 1440); Heinrich von Plauen deposed by his own chapter (1413). External: Lithuania, Poland, the archbishops of Riga.

  • Patron: Geležinis Šventasis (Iron Saint) (Cosmology §3.3), fed by the Reise and forced baptism. Not the Christian God.

5.4 Why Vytautas could not be Marshal¶

  1. The Marshal was a professed brother-knight: a monk under vows. A married man could not take the vow of chastity without his wife’s consent and separation, and Vytautas was married to Ona, who was then a hostage in the Order’s hands.

  2. The great offices were filled from senior brothers with decades in the Order. A convert of weeks (baptised at Tapiau on 21 Oct 1383) was not even a novice.

  3. Recruitment was effectively German-only. No Lithuanian ever held a great office.

  4. The Marshal led the Reisen against Lithuania. Handing the Order’s war against Lithuania to a Lithuanian claimant would have been absurd to the chapter, and the Order was suspicious of converts who had sworn first by pagan forms (Power systems §2.6).

  5. What he really was [Historical record]: a baptised ally and vassal under the Treaty of Königsberg (30 Jan 1384). He pledged Samogitia to the Nevėžis and was entrusted with castles to hold (Marienwerder, Georgenburg) and with Order troops „lent“ to his war. This is the correct Book I status: a vassal-prince with borrowed knights, not an officer. The Marshal of ch. 20 is a real Order brother. (The Marshal around 1383 may be Kuno von Hattenstein before naming him.)
