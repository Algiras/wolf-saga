# Žygimantas Kęstutaitis (The Quiet Brother)

**True Name**: Žygimantas (Sigismund) [Record]
**Title**: Duke of Starodub; Grand Duke of Lithuania 1432–40
**Era**: Book I (a mention: Kęstutis's youngest son), Books II–IV (hostage, then loyal lieutenant), **Book V antagonist**
**Age**: About 17 in 1382 (born c. 1365). Assassinated at Trakai, 20 Mar 1440 (Palm Sunday)
**Archetype**: The Overlooked Brother / The Paranoid Tyrant
**Pronunciation**: ži-gi-MAN-tas kęs-tu-TAI-tis · /ʒʲiːɡʲɪˈmɐntɐs kʲæːstʊˈtɐɪtʲɪs/ · English sound-alike "zhee-gih-MAHN-tahs kess-too-TYE-tis"
**Power**: **Tier 0**. No gift. In Book V **Henė whispers** to him, feeding his paranoia; whether he strikes a formal bargain is open.

## Record [Record]
*   Son of Kęstutis (and, in the saga, of Birutė). Long a **hostage of the Order** (c. 1384–98; *verify the span*).
*   Seized power from **Švitrigaila** in the coup at **Ašmena** (1 Sep 1432); won at **Pabaiskas** (1 Sep 1435).
*   Ruled harshly and was murdered at Trakai by princes of the **Czartoryski** family, descendants (if the genealogy holds) of Algirdas's first marriage. Son: **Mykolas Žygimantaitis** (d. 1452 in Moscow, reputedly poisoned; *verify*).

## Appearance [Saga invention]
*   Slight, careful, unremarkable; the family's grey eyes without their fire. Dresses plainly, like his brother in old age.

## Personality
*   **Patient**: Decades in his brother's shadow and in Order custody taught him to wait.
*   **Suspicious**: He trusts no one, and by the end rightly so.
*   **Cold**: He has inherited the cunning and none of the faith.

## Motivation
*   **The throne his brother never crowned**: To rule what Vytautas built, as a Kęstutaitis.
*   **Fear**: Of the boyars, of Švitrigaila, and of the dead his realm no longer feeds.

## Relationships
*   **Vytautas (Brother)**: Loyal while Vytautas lives. Book V reads him as "Vytautas without the flame".
*   **Švitrigaila (Cousin)**: Overthrown by him (1432).
*   **The Flame-Keeper**: Wants to use him and the last wolves as his secret police.
*   **The boyars (Goštautas and others)**: Named among those behind his murder.
